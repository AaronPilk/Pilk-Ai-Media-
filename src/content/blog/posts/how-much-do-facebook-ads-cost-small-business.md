The honest answer to "how much do Facebook ads cost" is "as much as you decide to spend," which is not very helpful on its own. What owners really want to know is how much it costs to get a customer, and how much they need to spend before they find out. This guide explains how Meta charges, what drives your costs and how to set a sensible first budget.

## How does Facebook charge for ads?

Meta runs an auction every time an ad could be shown. You set a budget, daily or for the whole campaign, and choose what you want: clicks, leads, messages or purchases. Meta then spends that budget trying to get those results at the lowest cost it can find among the people you allow it to reach.

You never pay more than your budget over the campaign, although Meta may spend somewhat more on some days and less on others within its own rules. You are charged as the ads run, usually by impressions, and the results determine your effective cost per click or lead.

## What decides how much your Facebook ads cost?

Several factors move your cost per result:

- **Your offer.** A clear, appealing offer gets cheaper results than a vague one.
- **Your creative.** Ads people stop and engage with get shown more efficiently.
- **Your audience and location.** Competitive areas and audiences cost more.
- **Your industry.** Some categories are crowded with advertisers.
- **Seasonality.** The weeks around major shopping holidays get expensive as large retailers compete for attention.
- **Your landing page.** If people click but the page does not convert, every lead costs more.
- **Your tracking.** When Meta gets good conversion data from your site, it finds the right people faster. See [Meta Pixel vs Conversions API](/blog/meta-pixel-vs-conversions-api/).

## How much should a small business spend on Facebook ads to start?

Spend enough to learn, but not more than you can afford to lose while learning. A practical approach:

1. **Decide what a customer is worth** to you, including repeat business.
2. **Pick one offer and one audience** to test.
3. **Set a daily budget you can sustain for at least two to four weeks** without changing everything every day.
4. **Judge results by cost per lead and cost per customer**, not likes or clicks.
5. **Scale what works gradually**, and cut what clearly does not.

Very small budgets spread across many ads rarely give Meta enough data to optimise, so it is usually better to run fewer ads with a meaningful budget behind each.

## What costs come on top of ad spend?

Ad spend goes to Meta. Running ads well usually involves other costs:

- **Creative**: photos, video and copy. Real footage of your work and team usually beats stock.
- **A landing page** built for the ad, rather than your homepage.
- **Tracking setup**: the Meta Pixel, Conversions API and conversion events.
- **Management**, if you hire someone to run and optimise campaigns.
- **Follow-up**: a CRM and fast replies, so the leads you paid for turn into customers.

## Are Facebook ads different for real estate, mortgage and other regulated businesses?

Yes. Ads about housing, employment, and financial products and services must run under Meta's special ad categories. In those categories you cannot target by age, gender or ZIP code, lookalike audiences are not available, and location targeting must cover at least a 15-mile radius. That usually means your creative and landing page carry more of the weight. We explain the rules in [Meta special ad categories](/blog/meta-special-ad-categories/).

## Why do some businesses waste money on Facebook ads?

The common reasons we see:

1. **Boosting posts** instead of running campaigns with a clear goal.
2. **Sending traffic to a homepage** with no clear next step.
3. **No tracking**, so nobody knows which ads produce customers.
4. **Changing campaigns every day** before Meta has enough data.
5. **Slow follow-up on leads**, so paid leads go cold. See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

Run them yourself if you have time to learn, a simple offer and a small budget. Hire help when the budget is large enough that mistakes are expensive, when you are in a special ad category, or when tracking and landing pages need building. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the minimum budget for Facebook ads?

Meta allows very small daily budgets, so you can technically start small. The practical minimum is whatever lets an ad collect enough results to learn from in a few weeks, which depends on your cost per result.

### Are Instagram ads cheaper than Facebook ads?

They run through the same Meta ads system, and costs depend on audience, format and placement rather than the app alone. Many campaigns run on both and let Meta choose. We compare them in [Instagram ads vs Facebook ads](/blog/instagram-ads-vs-facebook-ads/).

### How long before Facebook ads work?

Give a campaign at least a couple of weeks of steady spend before judging it, unless it is clearly failing. Meta needs data to optimise, and frequent changes reset that learning.

### Do I pay Facebook if nobody clicks my ad?

Often yes, because many campaigns are charged by impressions (people seeing the ad), not clicks. Your results then decide your effective cost per click or lead.
