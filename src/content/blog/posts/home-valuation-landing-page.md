"What's my home worth?" is a question almost every homeowner wonders about, often long before they decide to sell. A home valuation page turns that curiosity into a conversation with you. Done well, it is the best seller lead tool most agents have. Done badly, it collects addresses from people who feel tricked. Here is how to do it well.

## What makes a home valuation page work?

1. **A clear, honest offer**: an estimate of the home's value, with a personal follow-up from a local agent.
2. **A short form**: address first, then contact details.
3. **Local credibility**: your photo, recent sales nearby and reviews.
4. **Fast follow-up**: a text within minutes and a personal valuation within a day.
5. **Mobile speed**: most visitors come from social media on phones.

## What should the form ask?

A two-step form usually converts best:

- **Step 1**: property address. It feels low-commitment and gets people started.
- **Step 2**: name, email, phone and one optional question, such as timeline: "Thinking of selling in the next 3, 6 or 12 months?"

Keep it at that. Ask about condition, upgrades and motivation on the phone.

## Should you show an instant automated estimate?

Automated estimates are convenient but can be off, sometimes by a lot, especially for unique homes or areas with few sales. Options:

- **Instant estimate plus personal review**: show a range immediately, then promise a more accurate valuation from you.
- **Personal valuation only**: "I'll prepare a custom valuation and send it within 24 hours." Fewer submissions, often higher quality.

Either way, set expectations honestly. Overpromising an exact number damages trust.

## What should happen after someone submits?

Speed and personal attention win seller leads:

1. **Instant text and email**: "Thanks, I'm pulling recent sales near [address] now. I'll have your valuation shortly."
2. **Agent alert** with the address.
3. **Personal valuation** within a day: recent comparable sales, a range and what could move the number.
4. **Offer a walkthrough** for a more precise price.
5. **Long-term nurture** for owners not ready to sell: quarterly value updates and market notes.

## How do you drive traffic to a valuation page?

- **Meta ads** under the housing special ad category, with local imagery and a clear offer. See [Facebook ads for real estate agents](/blog/facebook-ads-for-real-estate-agents/).
- **Social posts** showing recent sales and market changes.
- **Email** to your past clients and sphere.
- **Your website**: link from the home page, seller page and neighborhood pages.
- **Mailers and signs** with a short web address or QR code.

## What should the page itself include?

- A headline tied to your area: "What is your [neighborhood] home worth right now?"
- A one-line explanation of what they will get and when.
- The form, visible quickly on phones.
- Your photo and a short introduction.
- Recent nearby sales or results, shown honestly.
- Reviews from sellers.
- Brokerage and license details, plus equal housing language.

## What mistakes hurt valuation pages?

- Promising an "exact value" from an automated tool.
- Asking for contact details before the address with no explanation.
- Waiting days to follow up.
- Generic national pages with no local proof.
- Treating every submission as ready to list now.

## How do you measure a valuation funnel?

## What should the personal valuation include?

Keep it simple and useful: the estimated range, three to five recent comparable sales with addresses, prices and dates, a short note on what makes the home different, and what could raise or lower the price. End with an offer to walk through the home for a sharper number. A one-page summary from a real agent beats a long automated report every time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do home valuation pages really generate seller leads?

Yes, they are one of the most reliable seller lead sources, especially with fast, personal follow-up and long-term nurturing.

### Are automated home value estimates accurate?

They can be close in areas with many similar sales and far off for unique homes. Present them as estimates and follow with your own analysis.

### Should I ask for a phone number on a valuation form?

Yes, if you will call or text, since speed matters for seller leads. Make texting consent clear and optional.

### How long until a valuation lead lists?

Some list within weeks; many take months. Stay in touch with periodic value updates.
