Contractors, cleaners, consultants, mobile services and many home-based businesses do not want their home address on Google Maps, and Google does not want it there either if customers do not visit. A service area business profile solves both. We set these up for our own businesses and clients, including our own profile. Here is how it works and where people go wrong.

## What is a service area business on Google?

Google separates businesses into two broad types:

- **Storefront businesses**, where customers come to you: a shop, office or restaurant. The address shows on the profile.
- **Service area businesses**, where you go to customers or serve them remotely: plumbers, cleaners, mobile notaries, consultants, agencies. The address is hidden, and the profile shows the areas you serve instead.

Some businesses are both, such as a shop that also delivers. They can show their address and list service areas too. Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) set out who qualifies.

## How do you set up a service area business profile?

The basic steps in Business Profile Manager:

1. **Add your business** with the exact business name you use everywhere else. Do not add keywords or cities to the name; it breaks Google's guidelines.
2. **Choose your primary category** carefully. It has a big effect on which searches you show up for. Add a few secondary categories that match services you actually offer.
3. **Enter your address.** Google needs it to verify you, even though it will not be shown.
4. **Say you do not serve customers at this address**, or turn off the setting to show it.
5. **Add service areas**: cities, counties or ZIP-based areas you really cover, up to 20.
6. **Add your phone number and website.**
7. **Verify.** Google decides the method. It may be a phone or text code, an email, a video showing your business and tools, or a postcard.

## How should you choose your service areas?

List the places you genuinely serve and can reach in a reasonable time. Google's guidance is that service areas should generally not extend further than about two hours' driving from where you are based.

A few practical points:

- Use cities and counties people recognise, such as St. Petersburg, Clearwater, Pinellas County and Tampa.
- Listing an area does not guarantee you will show up there. Distance, relevance and reputation still matter.
- More areas are not better. Twenty far-flung cities can make the profile look less credible than five accurate ones.

## How does Google verify a service area business?

Google chooses the method based on your business type and history. Common ones:

- **Phone or text code**, the quickest.
- **Video verification**, where you record a short, continuous video showing your location or vehicle, tools or signage and proof of management, like logging into your business accounts.
- **Postcard** with a code mailed to your address.

The code goes to the business, not your web designer, so the owner usually completes this step. For a very new business, verification can take more than one attempt. Do not create a second profile while you wait; duplicates cause more problems than they solve.

## What mistakes get service area profiles suspended?

These are the issues that cause the most trouble:

1. **Using a virtual office, coworking desk or PO box** as your address when you do not actually work there.
2. **Showing a home address as a storefront** when customers never visit.
3. **Stuffing keywords or cities into the business name**, like "Best Plumber St Pete Tampa Clearwater."
4. **Creating several profiles** for the same business in different cities without real locations.
5. **Mismatched details** between the profile, website and other listings.

## How do you get more calls from your Google Business Profile?

Once verified, the profile works best when you keep it active:

- **Add real photos** of your team, work and vehicles, and keep adding them.
- **Ask every happy customer for a review** with a direct link, and reply to reviews, good and bad. Never offer anything in exchange for reviews. Our guide to [getting more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) covers how to automate the ask.
- **List your services** with short descriptions.
- **Post updates** occasionally: new projects, seasonal offers, holiday hours.
- **Link to the right page on your website**, ideally a page about the services and areas on the profile.
- **Answer calls and messages fast.** A missed call from a profile is a lost customer, which is why [missed call text back](/blog/missed-call-text-back/) is one of the first automations we set up.

## How does your website support your Google Business Profile?

Google uses your website to understand your business. Make sure it:

- Shows the same business name and phone number as the profile.
- Has a page for each main service and mentions the areas you serve naturally.
- Includes structured data with your business type and service area.
- Links back to the profile, so visitors can find your reviews.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I list my business on Google without showing my address?

Yes. Service area businesses enter their address for verification and then hide it, so the public profile shows only the areas they serve.

### How many service areas can I add?

Google allows up to 20 service areas. List the ones you genuinely serve rather than filling every slot.

### Can I use a PO box or virtual office on Google Business Profile?

No. Google's guidelines do not allow PO boxes, and virtual offices or coworking desks are only acceptable if you are staffed there during business hours. Using them otherwise risks suspension.

### Why does my profile not show up in nearby cities I listed?

Service areas tell Google where you work, but rankings still depend on distance, relevance and reputation. More reviews, better service pages on your website and an active profile help more than adding cities.
