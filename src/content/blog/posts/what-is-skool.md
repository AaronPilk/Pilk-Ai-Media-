Skool has become one of the most talked-about places to run an online community, especially for coaches, course creators and business owners who want a paid group without stitching together a forum, a course platform and a payment tool. We help clients set up and grow Skool communities. Here is what the platform is, how it works and whether it fits what you want to build.

## What is Skool used for?

People use Skool to run:

- **Paid communities and masterminds**, where members pay monthly or yearly for access, coaching and peers.
- **Free communities** that build an audience and feed a paid offer.
- **Course libraries** with discussion attached, so students learn together.
- **Customer or client communities** for a business's buyers.
- **Internal teams and training** for companies that want a simple space for onboarding and updates.

## How does a Skool community work?

Every Skool group has a handful of core parts:

- **Community feed**: posts, comments and discussion, organised by categories you choose.
- **Classroom**: courses made of modules and lessons, with video and text.
- **Calendar**: scheduled calls and events, with reminders.
- **Members**: profiles, a member directory and messaging.
- **Leaderboard and levels**: members earn points when others like their posts and comments, and you can open courses to members once they reach a set level. This gamification is a big part of why Skool groups feel active.
- **About page**: the public page people see before joining, with your description, pricing and media.

The simplicity is the point. There are far fewer settings than most community or course platforms, which makes it easy to launch and easy for members to use.

## How much does Skool cost?

As of late 2026, Skool has two plans, and the main difference is the fee on member payments:

| Plan | Monthly price | Transaction fee on member payments |
|---|---|---|
| Hobby | $9 a month | 10% plus 30 cents per payment |
| Pro | $99 a month | 2.9% plus 30 cents, higher on very large payments |

Yearly billing gives a discount, and Skool offers a free trial. Prices and fees change, so confirm on Skool's own pricing page. For a paid community, the right plan depends on revenue: once member payments grow past the point where the lower fee covers the bigger plan price, Pro costs less overall. ## What are the pros and cons of Skool?

**What people like:**

- Very quick to set up and easy for members to use.
- Community, courses, calendar and payments in one place.
- The leaderboard encourages members to participate.
- A discovery directory where people browse communities, which can bring in new members.

**What people find limiting:**

- Few design and branding options.
- Basic course features compared with dedicated course platforms.
- Limited built-in marketing tools; most growth comes from outside the platform.
- Transaction fees on the Hobby plan are significant for paid groups.

We compare it with a popular alternative in [Skool vs Circle](/blog/skool-vs-circle/).

## Who should use Skool?

Skool fits best if:

- You want community to be the center of the offer, not an afterthought to a course.
- You value speed and simplicity over custom branding.
- You will run regular calls and posts that give members a reason to show up.
- You have, or will build, an audience outside Skool to invite in.

It fits less well if you need heavy branding, complex course logic or an all-in-one marketing system. Some businesses pair Skool with a CRM like GoHighLevel for the funnels and email around it. See [what GoHighLevel is](/blog/what-is-gohighlevel/).

## How do you grow a Skool community?

The platform does not grow a community on its own. What works:

1. **A clear promise** on your about page: who it is for and what changes for members.
2. **A strong first week** for new members, with a welcome post, an intro thread and an easy first win.
3. **A weekly rhythm** of posts and live calls.
4. **Traffic from outside**: social content, email, a free lead magnet, ads or partnerships.
5. **Member stories** that show results, shared with permission.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Skool free to use?

There is a free trial, but no permanently free plan for community owners as of late 2026. Members can join free communities at no cost; owners pay for the Hobby or Pro plan.

### Can I sell courses on Skool?

Yes. You can include courses in a paid membership or open them by level or purchase. The course tools are simple compared with dedicated course platforms, which suits many creators but not all.

### Does Skool take a cut of my membership revenue?

Yes, through transaction fees on member payments: higher on the Hobby plan and lower on Pro. Factor that into your pricing and plan choice.

### Can I use my own domain with Skool?

Skool communities live on Skool's platform under its address format. Check Skool's current plan features for custom URL options before you choose a plan.
