Mortgage brokers get pitched lead lists, ad packages and social media schemes all the time. Most of the business that actually closes comes from a few dependable channels, run consistently. Here are twelve ideas, grouped by what they cost you, with the compliance points to keep in mind.

## What are the best low-cost marketing ideas for mortgage brokers?

These cost mostly time and tend to produce the best clients.

### 1. Build real referral partnerships

Real estate agents, builders, financial planners, CPAs and divorce attorneys all meet people who need a mortgage. Pick a handful, learn how they work, and make them look good to their clients: fast pre-approvals, clear updates and closing on time. One reliable agent partner can be worth more than any ad campaign. Keep any arrangement within RESPA rules on referral fees and things of value.

### 2. Stay in touch with past clients

Your past borrowers will refinance, move, buy investment property and have friends who need loans. A simple schedule of useful touches (an annual mortgage review, a note when rates move meaningfully, a home anniversary message) keeps you the obvious choice. A CRM makes this automatic; see [CRM for mortgage brokers](/blog/crm-for-mortgage-brokers/).

### 3. Complete your Google Business Profile

Many borrowers search "mortgage broker near me." A complete profile with your categories, service area, hours, photos and regular reviews puts you in front of them for free. If you work from home, set it up as a service area business with your address hidden, which we explain in our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-service-area-business/).

### 4. Ask every happy borrower for a review

Reviews drive both trust and local rankings. Ask at closing, when gratitude is highest, with a direct link. Never offer anything in exchange, and do not filter who you ask, both of which break Google's review rules.

## How should a mortgage broker use their website for marketing?

Your website is where every other channel sends people, so it has to convert.

### 5. Make the first step short

A five-field form beats a full application as a first step. Name, contact details, goal and timeline is enough to start a conversation.

### 6. Write program pages that answer real questions

Pages that explain FHA, VA, first-time buyer programs or DSCR loans in plain language help borrowers and help your site appear in search over time. Our guide to [SEO for mortgage brokers](/blog/seo-for-mortgage-brokers/) covers which pages to build first.

### 7. Add calculators

Payment, affordability and refinance calculators keep people on your site and give them a reason to come back. Label assumptions clearly.

### 8. Keep it compliant

NMLS IDs on every page, careful rate wording and no promises you cannot keep. Our [mortgage broker website design](/blog/mortgage-broker-website-design/) guide lists what to include, and every site on our [mortgage websites](/mortgage/) page is built with those details in place.

## Do paid ads work for mortgage brokers?

They can, once your site and follow-up are ready to catch the leads.

### 9. Run Meta ads within the special ad category

Facebook and Instagram ads for mortgages must run under Meta's financial products and services special ad category, which removes age, gender and ZIP code targeting and requires a minimum 15-mile location radius. That makes the message and the landing page do more of the work. We explain the setup in [Facebook ads for mortgage brokers](/blog/facebook-ads-for-mortgage-brokers/).

### 10. Try Google search ads for high-intent terms

People searching "refinance rates [city]" or "FHA lender near me" are closer to acting than social media scrollers. Start small, send them to a matching program page rather than your homepage, and track which searches produce applications.

## What marketing do mortgage brokers usually skip but should not?

### 11. Answer every lead within minutes

Borrowers often contact more than one lender. The one who responds first, clearly and helpfully, usually gets the conversation. An automatic text reply that confirms you got their inquiry and offers a time to talk costs almost nothing and saves leads. We cover why in [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

### 12. Measure cost per funded loan

Track where every lead came from and which ones closed. Then compare channels by cost per funded loan, not cost per lead. A cheap lead source that never funds is the most expensive one you have.

## What mortgage marketing should you avoid?

Be careful with:

- **Bought lead lists** of unknown origin and consent, which can create texting and calling compliance problems.
- **Ads with rate or payment claims** that trigger disclosure requirements you have not included.
- **Paying for referrals** in ways RESPA prohibits.
- **"Guaranteed approval"** or similar promises.

When in doubt, run marketing past your compliance contact before it goes live.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best marketing for a new mortgage broker?

Start with referral partners, your personal network, a complete Google Business Profile and a simple compliant website with a short first-step form. Add ads once you can respond to every lead quickly.

### Can mortgage brokers pay real estate agents for referrals?

RESPA generally prohibits paying fees or giving things of value for referrals of settlement service business on federally related mortgage loans. Co-marketing has its own rules. Check with your compliance contact before any arrangement.

### Do mortgage brokers need social media?

It helps with trust and staying top of mind, especially with referral partners and past clients. It rarely produces many leads on its own without ads or a strong personal following.

### How much should a mortgage broker spend on marketing?

There is no standard figure. Start with the free and low-cost channels, then add paid channels in small tests and keep the ones with an acceptable cost per funded loan.
