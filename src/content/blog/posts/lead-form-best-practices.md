Your lead form is where interest turns into a real contact. Small changes here change results more than most redesigns. Here are the practices we use on every site and landing page, and the mistakes we fix most often.

## How many fields should a lead form have?

As few as you can. Each extra field costs submissions. A strong default for service businesses:

1. Name.
2. Phone or email, or both if you will use both.
3. What they need, as a dropdown or short text.
4. Optional: ZIP code or area, if you serve limited areas.

Ask for details like budget, timeline and project specifics on the call, or in a second step after the first submission.

## What should the form layout look like?

- **One column**, with labels above fields.
- **Large tap targets** for phones.
- **The right keyboard** for each field: number pad for phone, email keyboard for email.
- **Autofill support**, so browsers can fill name, email and phone.
- **A clear button label**: "Get my free estimate" beats "Submit."
- **Error messages** that say exactly what to fix, next to the field.

## Where should forms appear on a page?

Put a form or a clear button to it near the top of key pages, and again after the information people need to decide. On landing pages, keep the form visible early on desktop and one tap away on mobile. Pair it with proof: reviews, photos of work and a short line about what happens next.

## What should happen after someone submits?

This is where many forms fail:

1. **A thank-you page** that confirms receipt, says who will contact them and when, and offers a booking link.
2. **An instant text and email** from a real person.
3. **An alert** to the person responsible.
4. **A CRM contact** with the source and request.

## How do you handle text message consent on forms?

If you will text leads beyond responding to their request, add an optional, unchecked consent checkbox with clear language and links to your privacy policy and terms. Store the consent with the contact. See [TCPA text message consent](/blog/tcpa-text-message-consent/).

## How do you stop spam without losing real leads?

- **Invisible protections first**: honeypot fields, server-side validation and rate limiting.
- **Low-friction challenges** like Cloudflare Turnstile instead of puzzle CAPTCHAs.
- **Validate on the server**, not just in the browser.

Our own site limits how many submissions one visitor can send in a short period and validates every field on the server, which keeps spam down without puzzles.

## What should a marketing form never collect?

Social Security numbers, bank or card details, government ID numbers, income documents and health details do not belong on a marketing form. If your process needs them, send people to a secure portal built for that purpose. This matters especially for mortgage and financial businesses. See [mortgage lead generation websites](/blog/mortgage-lead-generation-websites/).

## How do you test whether your form works?

Before launch and every month after:

- Submit a test on a phone and a computer.
- Confirm the email, text and CRM contact all arrive.
- Check the thank-you page and booking link.
- Make sure the alert reaches the right person.

We regularly find forms that send to old email addresses or a CRM nobody checks. See [why a website does not generate leads](/blog/why-is-my-website-not-generating-leads/).

## Should you use multi-step forms?

Multi-step forms can convert well when you need more information: the first step asks something easy, such as the service needed, and later steps collect contact details. They work best when each step is quick and there are only two or three. For simple requests, a single short form is usually better.

## How do you improve a form over time?

Change one thing at a time and watch the results for a few weeks: the button label, the number of fields, the headline above the form, or adding reviews nearby. Small, steady improvements add up.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a lead form be?

Three or four fields is a good default for most service businesses. Collect more on the call or in a second step.

### Should I require a phone number?

Only if you will call or text. Making it optional can increase submissions; making it required can improve contact rates. Test both.

### Do CAPTCHAs hurt conversions?

Puzzle CAPTCHAs can. Invisible protections and low-friction challenges stop most spam without frustrating real people.

### What should a thank-you page say?

Confirm the request, say who will contact them and when, and offer a next step such as booking a time.
