Few topics split real estate agents like Zillow. Some build their whole business on it; others say they paid for months and closed nothing. Both can be true, and the difference usually comes down to cost structure, speed and follow-up rather than luck. Here is how to decide whether it makes sense for you, and what to build so you are not dependent on any one lead source.

## How does Zillow charge agents for leads?

Zillow sells buyer connections through its Premier Agent program. How you pay depends on your market and agreement. In many markets Zillow uses a model often called Flex, where you pay nothing up front and pay Zillow a referral fee, a share of your commission, when a deal closes. In other arrangements agents have paid for advertising placement in specific areas.

Terms, eligibility and fees change and vary by market, so get the current agreement in writing from Zillow and read the referral fee, response time requirements and any rules about the tools you must use before you sign.

## Why do so many agents struggle to convert Zillow leads?

When agents talk about Zillow leads online, the same frustrations come up again and again:

1. **The buyers are early.** Many are browsing months before they plan to buy, or curious about one house.
2. **Speed decides who gets the client.** A buyer who requests a tour often contacts more than one agent. The first one to respond well usually wins.
3. **Follow-up takes months.** The deals often come from leads that were not ready at first contact but were nurtured until they were.
4. **The cost per closing is real.** A referral fee on every closed deal adds up, and the agent still does all the work.
5. **You do not own the relationship source.** If the program changes or you leave, the flow stops.

None of these mean the leads are fake. They mean Zillow leads are raw material, not finished clients.

## Who tends to do well with Zillow leads?

Agents and teams who:

- Answer new inquiries within minutes, every day, including evenings.
- Have a system that tracks every lead and reminds them to follow up for months.
- Have enough deal volume that the referral fee works within their margins.
- Can handle showings quickly, often with a team.
- Turn closed Zillow clients into repeat and referral business they own.

Speed is the biggest factor. The research on response time is old but consistent: in a [Harvard Business Review study](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads), companies that contacted leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as those that waited longer. Whether or not you use Zillow, build lead sources you own so you are never dependent on one company:

- **Your own website** on your own domain, with neighborhood pages and a home valuation page. See [what makes a good real estate website](/blog/what-makes-a-good-real-estate-website/).

- **A Google Business Profile** with reviews from past clients.

## How do you calculate whether Zillow leads are worth it for you?

Do the math on your own numbers rather than anyone's averages:

1. Estimate how many leads you would get in a month in your area.
2. Estimate what share you would convert to closings over the next 12 months, honestly. Start low.
3. Multiply closings by your average commission.
4. Subtract the referral fee on each closing and your time and tools.

If the result is clearly positive and you can respond fast enough to protect it, test it for a fixed period. Track every lead in a CRM from day one so you know the real conversion rate, not a guess.

## Is there a downside to relying on Zillow for leads?

The main risk is dependence. A program change, a fee change or losing eligibility can cut your pipeline overnight. Agents who use Zillow as one source among several, with a website and database they own, are protected from that. Agents who use it as their only source are not.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Zillow take from each deal?

Under referral-fee arrangements, Zillow takes a percentage of your commission when a deal closes. The percentage depends on your agreement and market, so check the current terms directly with Zillow before you join.

### Why are Zillow leads so hard to reach?

Many people request information or a tour while browsing and are not ready to talk. Fast first contact by text and phone, followed by patient follow-up over weeks and months, reaches far more of them than a single call.

### Can new agents succeed with Zillow leads?

Some do, especially on teams that provide training and systems. A new agent without a follow-up system or fast availability often struggles, because the leads reward speed and persistence.

### What is a better alternative to buying leads?

Building your own sources: a website with neighborhood and home valuation pages, a CRM with automatic follow-up, past-client outreach and a Google Business Profile with reviews. These take longer to build but do not charge per deal.
