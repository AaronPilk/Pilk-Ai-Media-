Most agents try SEO once, find their site nowhere near Zillow or Realtor.com for "homes for sale in [city]," and give up. That is the wrong battle. Portals win generic listing searches. Local agents win searches that need local knowledge and a real person. Here is how to focus your SEO there.

## Why can agents not outrank portals for listing searches?

Portals have millions of pages, huge link profiles and the same listings you would show. Google has little reason to rank your copy of an MLS listing above theirs. IDX pages rarely change that. See [IDX websites for realtors](/blog/idx-website-for-realtors/).

## Which searches can local agents win?

- **Neighborhood questions**: "living in Old Northeast St. Pete," "best neighborhoods in Clearwater for families."
- **Agent searches**: "real estate agent near me," "realtor for condos in downtown St. Pete."
- **Seller questions**: "how to sell a house in [city]," "cost to sell a home in Florida."
- **Buyer questions**: "first-time buyer programs in [county]," "flood insurance when buying in [area]."
- **Market questions**: "[neighborhood] home prices this year."
- **Your name and team name.**

## What content ranks best for real estate agents?

1. **Neighborhood guides** written from real experience: what it is like to live there, housing types, typical price ranges with sources, amenities and recent activity.
2. **Market updates** for specific areas, monthly or quarterly.
3. **Seller and buyer guides** specific to your state and market.
4. **Your own listing pages** with original descriptions, photos and video.
5. **Answers to common questions**, each with a short direct answer at the top.

Write from what you know. Original local detail is what portals cannot copy at scale.

## How does Google Business Profile help real estate SEO?

For "realtor near me" searches, map results matter most. Keep your profile complete, choose the right category, add real photos, post updates and get steady reviews from clients. Follow Google's rules on addresses if you work from home or a brokerage office. See our [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-for-small-businesses/) and [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## What technical basics matter for real estate sites?

- Fast pages on phones, especially with large photos.
- A sitemap submitted to Google Search Console.
- Clean page titles naming the area and topic.
- LocalBusiness or RealEstateAgent structured data matching your profile.
- No duplicate neighborhood pages with swapped city names.

See [Google Search Console for beginners](/blog/google-search-console-for-beginners/).

## How do agents earn links and mentions?

- Local news features and quotes on market trends.
- Community sponsorships and events.
- Partnerships with local businesses, builders and lenders where appropriate.
- Guest content for local publications.
- Being listed in local business directories and associations.

## How long does real estate SEO take?

Neighborhood and question content can start ranking within months, faster in smaller markets. Competitive agent searches in large metros take longer. Consistency matters: a steady stream of genuinely local content compounds. See [how long SEO takes](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/).

## Does AI search matter for real estate?

Yes. People ask AI tools which neighborhoods suit them or how buying works in a state. Clear, specific, local answers with your name on them are what those tools cite. See [how to show up in ChatGPT search](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-chatgpt-search/).

## What should agents avoid in real estate SEO?

- Thin city pages generated from a template.
- Copying neighborhood descriptions from other sites.
- Buying links.
- Expecting IDX pages to rank on their own.
- Fair housing problems in neighborhood content, such as describing who "should" live somewhere.

## What does a month of real estate SEO work look like?

A sustainable routine: publish one market update for your main area, add or improve one neighborhood guide, ask every closed client for a review, post a couple of updates and new photos on your Business Profile, and check Search Console for new searches you appear in. Spend a few hours a month on it consistently and the results compound over a year. Agents who treat SEO as a monthly habit, not a one-time project, are the ones who eventually own their local searches.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a real estate agent rank on Google against Zillow?

Not usually for generic listing searches. Agents can rank well for neighborhood, market and question searches, and for local agent searches through Google Business Profile.

### Do neighborhood pages help real estate SEO?

Yes, when they are genuinely local and useful. Template pages with only the city name changed rarely help.

### How often should agents publish content?

Steadily. A monthly market update and a new neighborhood or question page each month is a strong, sustainable pace.

### Does social media help real estate SEO?

Indirectly. Social builds brand searches and can earn mentions, but your website and Business Profile drive search rankings.
