Facebook and Instagram can be a strong source of borrowers for mortgage brokers, but two sets of rules shape every campaign: Meta's special ad category restrictions and mortgage advertising laws. Brokers who understand both can still run efficient campaigns. Here is a practical playbook. It is general information, not legal advice; get compliance approval before launching.

## Which Meta rules apply to mortgage ads?

Mortgage and loan ads fall under Meta's special ad categories for housing or financial products and services. In those categories:

- No targeting by age or gender.
- No targeting or excluding ZIP codes.
- Location targeting must cover at least a 15-mile radius.
- Many detailed targeting options are removed.
- Lookalike audiences are not available.

Declare the category when creating each campaign. See [Meta special ad categories](/blog/meta-special-ad-categories/).

## Which mortgage advertising laws affect ad copy?

- **Trigger terms**: stating a repayment period like "30-year," a payment amount or a finance charge requires additional disclosures. See [trigger terms in mortgage advertising](/blog/trigger-terms-mortgage-advertising/).
- **Rates** must be stated as an APR.
- **No misleading claims**: "no closing costs" when costs exist, implied government affiliation, or guaranteed approval.
- **NMLS identification** and equal housing language as your state and company require.

See [mortgage advertising compliance](/blog/mortgage-advertising-compliance/).

## What mortgage ad offers work best?

Offers that help without quoting specific terms:

1. **Payment calculator**: "See what your payment could be in [area]." Leads to a calculator page with a soft call to action.
2. **First-time buyer guide**: down payment assistance programs and steps, linked to official program pages.
3. **Refinance review**: "Is a refinance worth it for you? Get a free review."
4. **Pre-approval explainer**: what pre-approval is and why sellers care.
5. **Local market education**: short videos from the loan officer answering real questions.

## What creative works for mortgage ads?

- **The loan officer on camera**, speaking plainly. People choose people.
- **Short educational videos** answering one question each.
- **Captions on everything**, since many people watch without sound.
- **Local references**: neighborhoods, landmarks and housing types your audience recognizes.
- **No stock photos of keys and handshakes.**

## Should mortgage ads use lead forms or landing pages?

Both work. Lead forms produce more leads at lower cost but often lower intent; landing pages produce fewer, more qualified leads and let borrowers use a calculator first. Add one qualifying question, such as purchase or refinance and timeline. See [Facebook lead ads vs landing pages](/blog/facebook-lead-ads-vs-landing-page/).

Never collect sensitive data in an ad form or landing page. Contact details only; send borrowers to your secure application for the rest.

## How should mortgage leads be followed up?

Borrowers from social ads are often early and comparing options. A strong process:

1. Instant text and email naming the offer they responded to.
2. Loan officer call within minutes during business hours.
3. Follow-up texts and calls over the following days.
4. Long-term nurture with helpful, compliant content for people months away.

See [CRM for mortgage brokers](/blog/crm-for-mortgage-brokers/) and [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

## How do you measure mortgage ad performance?

Track cost per lead, contact rate, applications, closed loans and cost per funded loan by campaign. Mortgage leads take time, so judge campaigns over months, and keep tracking connected with the Meta Pixel and Conversions API. See [Meta Pixel vs Conversions API](/blog/meta-pixel-vs-conversions-api/).

## What mistakes do mortgage brokers make with Facebook ads?

- Running ads without declaring the special ad category.
- Captions with "30-year fixed" or payment amounts and no disclosures.
- Sending traffic to a homepage instead of a focused page.
- Leaving leads in Meta's lead center for hours.
- Judging after a week.

## How should a loan officer show up in mortgage video ads?

Plainly and consistently. Film in good light, look at the camera, answer one question in under a minute, and finish with one next step, such as trying the calculator. Wear what you would wear to meet a borrower. Real, slightly imperfect videos usually beat polished productions on social.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can mortgage brokers target first-time buyers on Facebook?

Not by age or demographics. Target the area broadly and let first-time buyer content and offers attract the right people.

### Do mortgage Facebook ads need compliance approval?

Most companies require compliance review of all advertising, including social ads and loan officer posts. Get approval before launching.

### Can I advertise rates in Facebook ads?

You can if stated as an APR with required disclosures, but rates change daily, so most brokers advertise tools and education instead.

### What budget should a mortgage broker start with?

Enough to run one offer steadily for several weeks. Judge results by applications and funded loans, not clicks.
