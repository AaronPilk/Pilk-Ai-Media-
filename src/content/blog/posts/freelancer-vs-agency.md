Once you decide to hire help for your website, the next question is who. Freelancers and agencies can both do excellent work, and both can let you down. The right choice depends less on the label and more on the size of your project, how much it matters to your business and how the people work. Here is how to compare them honestly.

## What is the difference between a freelancer and an agency?

A **freelancer** is an independent designer or developer who works directly with clients. Some do everything; others specialize in design or development and partner with others for the rest.

An **agency** is a company with a team: typically design, development, copy, strategy and project management. Small studios sit in between: a few people, often with a founder involved in every project.

## How do freelancers and agencies compare?

| | Freelancer | Agency |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Usually lower | Usually higher |
| Who you talk to | The person doing the work | A project manager or lead |
| Skills | One person's range | A team's range |
| Speed | Depends on their workload | More capacity, more process |
| Continuity | Risky if they get busy or move on | Team continues if someone leaves |
| Process | Varies widely | Usually defined |
| Accountability | Personal | Company and contract |

## When does a freelancer make sense?

- **Simple, well-defined projects**: a small brochure site or a refresh of an existing one.
- **Tight budgets**, where an agency's overhead is not justified.
- **You know what you want** and need someone to execute it.
- **You found a great specialist** with relevant examples and strong references.

The biggest risk is availability. If your freelancer gets sick, takes a full-time job or disappears, you need access to everything to move on. That is why ownership terms matter. See [who owns my website](/blog/who-owns-my-website/).

## When does an agency make sense?

- **The website is central to your business**, generating most of your leads or sales.
- **You need several skills at once**: design, development, copy, SEO, CRM and ads.
- **Timelines matter** and you need reliable capacity.
- **You want ongoing support** without depending on one person.

The biggest risks with agencies are cost, being handed to junior staff after the sale, and slow communication through layers of people.

## How should you compare quotes?

Prices vary widely, so compare what is included rather than the headline figure:

- Number of pages and design rounds.
- Who writes the content.
- SEO setup, analytics and Search Console.
- Forms connected to your inbox or CRM.
- Hosting, support and what they cost after launch.
- Who owns the domain, code and accounts.

## What red flags apply to both?

- No live examples you can visit.
- No written proposal or contract.
- Domain registered in their name.
- Pressure to sign quickly.
- Vague answers on timeline and what happens after launch.
- No questions about your customers or goals.

## How do you decide?

Ask three questions:

1. **How much does this website matter to my revenue?** The more it matters, the more process and continuity are worth.
2. **How many skills does the project need?** One skill suits a freelancer; several suit a team.
3. **Who will look after it in two years?** Make sure the answer does not depend on one person you cannot reach.

## What should the contract say either way?

Whoever you hire, get the scope, price, payment schedule, timeline, number of revision rounds and launch responsibilities in writing. The contract should say you own the finished site and content once paid, that the domain and hosting accounts are in your name, and what happens to your files if either side ends the project early.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a freelancer cheaper than an agency?

Usually, because freelancers have lower overhead. Compare what is included, though; a cheap quote that leaves out content, SEO setup or support can cost more later.

### Can a freelancer handle SEO and marketing too?

Some can. Many specialize in design or development. Ask for examples of the specific work you need.

### What if my freelancer disappears?

If you own the domain, hosting and site files, another developer can take over. If they control the accounts, recovery can be slow and painful.

### Do agencies outsource work?

Some do, to freelancers or offshore teams. Ask who will actually design and build your site.
