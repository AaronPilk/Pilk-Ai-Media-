Choosing a web designer is a decision you will live with for years. The right one builds a site that brings in customers and is easy to update. The wrong one leaves you with a slow site, missed deadlines and accounts you cannot access. Here is a step-by-step way to choose well.

## Step 1: What should your website do?

Before talking to anyone, write down:

- **The main goal**: calls, quote requests, bookings, sales or credibility for referrals.
- **Your services and areas served.**
- **Must-have features**: booking, forms to your CRM, listings, calculators, payments, member areas.
- **Sites you like**, and why.
- **Your budget range and timeline.**

A clear brief gets you better proposals and makes comparisons fair.

## Step 2: Where do you find web designers?

- Referrals from businesses whose sites you like.
- Designers credited at the bottom of sites you admire.
- Local business groups and chambers.
- Agency directories and reviews.
- Search for designers who specialize in your industry.

Industry experience helps: a designer who has built mortgage or title sites already knows the compliance and feature needs.

## Step 3: How do you evaluate a portfolio?

- **Visit live sites**, not just screenshots.
- **Check them on your phone**: speed, readability and how easy it is to contact the business.
- **Look for variety**: do all sites look the same, or tailored to each business?
- **Look for results**, such as more leads or better rankings, where clients allow sharing.

## Step 4: What questions should you ask?

Ask every shortlisted designer the same questions about scope, timeline, ownership, hosting, SEO and support. We list fifteen in [questions to ask a web designer](/blog/questions-to-ask-a-web-designer/). The most important:

- Who owns the site, domain and accounts when it is done?
- What exactly is included in the price?
- How long will it take, and what do you need from me?
- Where do form submissions go?
- What happens after launch, and what does support cost?

## Step 5: How do you compare proposals?

Line them up side by side:

| Compare | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Scope | Pages, features, design rounds, copywriting |
| Timeline | Realistic dates and your responsibilities |
| Ownership | Domain and site in your name |
| SEO setup | Titles, sitemap, Search Console, speed, structured data |
| Support | Hosting, edits and costs after launch |
| Price | What is included, not just the total |

The cheapest proposal often leaves out content, SEO setup or support, which cost more later. See [how much a website costs](/blog/how-much-does-a-website-cost-small-business/).

## Step 6: Should you check references?

Yes. Ask two past clients: Was the project on time? Did the site improve results? How was communication? Would they hire the designer again? How are they supported now?

## Step 7: What red flags should make you walk away?

- No written proposal or contract.
- Domain registered in the designer's name.
- No live examples.
- Vague answers on timeline and ownership.
- No questions about your customers or goals.
- Pressure to sign immediately.

## Freelancer, agency or studio?

Each can be the right choice. Freelancers often cost less; agencies bring larger teams; small studios offer senior attention with a defined process. See [freelancer vs agency](/blog/freelancer-vs-agency/). Whatever you choose, ownership and clear scope matter more than the label.

## What happens after you choose a designer?

A good designer starts with a kickoff call to confirm goals, pages and responsibilities, then asks for your content, photos, logo and access to accounts like your domain. Expect a design draft to review, a round or two of changes, then a test version of the site before launch. Ask for a written timeline with dates for each step, and send content on time; delays on the client side are the most common reason launches slip.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I pay a web designer?

It depends on scope and design approach. Compare what is included across proposals rather than choosing on price alone.

### How do I know if a web designer is good?

Visit their live sites on your phone, talk to past clients, and notice whether they ask thoughtful questions about your business.

### Should I hire a local web designer?

Local helps for in-person meetings and local knowledge, but many excellent designers work remotely. Prioritize fit and track record.

### What should be in a web design contract?

Scope, price, payment schedule, timeline, revisions, ownership, hosting terms and what happens if either side ends the project.
