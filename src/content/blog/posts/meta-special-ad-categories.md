If you advertise real estate, mortgages, loans, insurance-related financial products or job openings on Facebook and Instagram, Meta requires you to declare a special ad category. Many advertisers find out the hard way when ads get rejected or targeting options disappear. Here is what the categories are, what changes inside them and how to run campaigns that still work.

## What are Meta's special ad categories?

Meta created special ad categories to reduce discrimination in ads for opportunities protected by law. The current categories are:

- **Housing**: real estate listings, rentals, home insurance, mortgage loans, home repairs and related services.
- **Employment**: job openings, recruiting and employment services.
- **Financial products and services**: credit cards, loans, financing and other financial offers. In January 2025 this category replaced the older "credit" category and widened its scope.
- **Social issues, elections or politics**: ads about candidates, elections or social issues, which require authorization and "paid for by" disclaimers.

If your ad fits a category, you must declare it when creating the campaign. Ads that should be in a category but are not can be rejected, and repeated issues can restrict your account.

## What targeting restrictions apply?

For housing, employment and financial products and services, Meta restricts targeting:

| Restriction | What it means |
|---|---|
| No age targeting | Ads must reach all adults |
| No gender targeting | Ads must reach all genders |
| No ZIP code targeting | You cannot target or exclude specific ZIP codes |
| Minimum 15-mile radius | Location targeting must cover at least 15 miles around a point |
| Limited detailed targeting | Many interest and demographic options are removed |
| No lookalike audiences | You cannot build lookalikes from your customer lists |

Political and social issue ads follow separate rules focused on authorization and transparency.

## How do you run effective ads in a special category?

When Meta controls more of who sees your ad, your creative and offer do the targeting:

1. **Speak to the situation, not the demographic.** "Thinking of selling your home this spring?" finds sellers without targeting them by age.
2. **Use specific local content.** Neighborhood names, local landmarks and real homes attract people in that area.
3. **Lead with a clear offer**: a home valuation, a payment estimate tool or a free consultation.
4. **Use video**, which tends to perform well and qualifies viewers for retargeting.
5. **Send traffic to a dedicated landing page** with a short form. See [Facebook lead ads vs landing pages](/blog/facebook-lead-ads-vs-landing-page/).
6. **Track conversions properly** so Meta optimizes for leads, not clicks. See [Meta Pixel vs Conversions API](/blog/meta-pixel-vs-conversions-api/).

## Do fair lending and fair housing rules still apply?

Yes. The categories limit targeting, but the content of your ads must also comply with fair housing and lending laws. Avoid language that suggests a preference for any protected group, and follow advertising rules for rates and terms. See [mortgage advertising compliance](/blog/mortgage-advertising-compliance/) and [Facebook ads for real estate agents](/blog/facebook-ads-for-real-estate-agents/).

## Can you still retarget website visitors?

You can still reach people who engaged with your page, videos or ads within the platform's options, though some audience types are limited in special categories. Rules change, so check what Ads Manager offers when you build the campaign.

## What are common special ad category mistakes?

- Not declaring the category and getting ads rejected or accounts restricted.
- Trying to work around restrictions with interest targeting proxies.
- Using a tiny radius that Meta will not allow.
- Writing ads that describe the ideal buyer instead of the home or offer.
- Judging results after a few days, before Meta has learned.

## How do special categories affect cost and results?

Broader targeting can mean less efficient delivery at first, so creative quality, landing pages and follow-up matter more. Give campaigns time and judge by cost per qualified lead and closed business, not cost per click. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do mortgage ads need a special ad category?

Yes. Mortgage ads fall under housing or financial products and services, depending on the offer, so targeting restrictions apply.

### Can I target by ZIP code in a special ad category?

No. Housing, employment and financial ads cannot target or exclude specific ZIP codes, and location targeting must cover at least a 15-mile radius.

### Can I use lookalike audiences for real estate ads?

No. Lookalike audiences are not available for ads in housing, employment or financial products and services categories.

### What happens if I do not declare a special ad category?

Meta may reject the ad, and repeated violations can restrict your ad account. Declare the category whenever your ad fits it.
