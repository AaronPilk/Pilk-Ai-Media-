Borrowers check out loan officers before they call, the same way they check reviews before a restaurant. A personal website gives referral partners and past clients something to send, and gives borrowers a reason to trust you. Here is what a loan officer website needs and how to set one up without compliance headaches.

## Do loan officers need their own website?

Not every loan officer does. A company profile page can be enough if your leads come from a single referral partner. A personal site is worth it when you:

- **Build a personal brand** on social media or in your community.
- **Work with many realtors** who want something professional to share.
- **Run ads** or content that need a home.
- **Want to keep your reputation** if you change companies (check your employment agreement on domains and leads).

## What should a loan officer website include?

**Identity and licensing**
- Your name, photo and title.
- Your individual NMLS number and your company's NMLS number.
- The states you are licensed in.
- A link to NMLS Consumer Access so borrowers can verify you.

**Programs and expertise**
- Pages for the loans you actually do: conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, jumbo, refinance, and any niche such as self-employed or investor loans.
- Plain-English explanations of who each program fits.

**Proof**
- Reviews from real borrowers, with permission.
- Realtor and partner testimonials.

**Contact and next steps**
- A short contact form: name, email, phone and what they are looking for.
- A booking link for calls.
- A clearly labeled button to your company's secure application.

**Tools**
- Payment and affordability calculators. See [mortgage calculators for websites](/blog/mortgage-calculator-for-website/).

## What should a loan officer website never collect?

A marketing form is not a loan application. Never ask for Social Security numbers, income documents, bank statements or file uploads on your marketing site. Send borrowers to your company's secure application portal for anything sensitive. This protects borrowers, keeps you out of trouble, and keeps your website simple.

## What compliance rules apply to loan officer websites?

Your website is advertising, so the same rules apply as for any mortgage ad:

- **NMLS identification** where your states require it.
- **Rate and payment wording** that follows the Truth in Lending rules. Mentioning certain terms can require additional disclosures. See [trigger terms in mortgage advertising](/blog/trigger-terms-mortgage-advertising/).
- **Equal housing language and logo.**
- **No misleading claims**, such as implying government affiliation or "no cost" loans that have costs.
- **Company approval.** Most lenders and brokerages require compliance to review and approve personal websites and social profiles.

Our guide to [mortgage advertising compliance](/blog/mortgage-advertising-compliance/) covers each of these. This is general information, not legal advice; your compliance team has the final word.

## Should a loan officer website show rates?

Usually not on a static page. Rates change daily and must be advertised with the right disclosures. If you want to talk about rates, a better approach is a "get today's rate" form or a market update that links to properly disclosed figures.

## How do loan officer websites get leads?

The site supports your existing channels more than it creates leads from nothing:

1. **Referral partners** share it with buyers.
2. **Past clients** find you again for refinances and referrals.
3. **Social media** sends followers to program pages and booking links.
4. **Local search** brings borrowers looking for a loan officer in your area, especially with a strong Google Business Profile where your company allows one.

## How do you get your company to approve your website?

- Send compliance a draft before launch, not after.
- Include every page, form and disclosure.
- Ask for their required disclosure text and logo files up front.
- Keep a record of the approved version and any changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a loan officer have a personal website?

Usually yes, with company approval. Many lenders and brokerages allow personal sites as long as compliance reviews them and they include required disclosures.

### Does a loan officer website need an NMLS number?

Showing your NMLS number is standard practice and required in many states for advertising. Check your state rules and company policy.

### Can I take applications on my website?

Link to your company's secure application system rather than collecting sensitive information through a website form.

### What happens to my website if I change companies?

It depends on who owns the domain and your employment agreement. Owning your own domain keeps your options open; update licensing and company details when you move.
