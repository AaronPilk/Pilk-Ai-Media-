Calculators are some of the most visited pages on mortgage websites. Borrowers love them because they answer the first question everyone has: "What would my payment be?" Done well, they keep visitors engaged and lead naturally to a conversation. Done badly, they show misleading numbers and create compliance risk. Here is how to get them right.

## Which mortgage calculators should a website have?

| Calculator | Answers | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly payment | "What would my payment be?" | Every visitor |
| Affordability | "How much house can I afford?" | First-time buyers |
| Refinance | "Would refinancing save me money?" | Existing homeowners |
| Rent vs buy | "Should I keep renting?" | Renters considering buying |
| Down payment | "How much do I need to save?" | Early-stage buyers |
| Extra payment | "What if I pay more each month?" | Current owners |

Start with payment, affordability and refinance. Add the others as your content grows.

## How does a mortgage payment calculator work?

The core is the standard amortization formula for principal and interest:

**M = P × [r(1 + r)^n] ÷ [(1 + r)^n − 1]**

where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12) and n is the number of monthly payments. A $300,000 loan at a 6% annual rate over 30 years gives a monthly principal and interest payment of about $1,799.

But principal and interest are only part of the real payment.

## What should a payment calculator include besides principal and interest?

A useful calculator shows the full monthly cost:

- **Property taxes**, which vary widely by county.
- **Homeowners insurance**, which can be substantial in coastal areas like Florida.
- **Mortgage insurance**: private mortgage insurance on many conventional loans with less than 20% down, or mortgage insurance premiums on FHA loans.
- **HOA or condo fees**, where they apply.

Leaving these out makes homes look more affordable than they are, which frustrates borrowers later.

## How do you keep mortgage calculators compliant?

This is general information, not legal advice:

- **Label defaults as examples.** If your calculator loads with a sample rate and payment, say clearly that they are illustrations, not offers.
- **Avoid presenting a payment as an offer.** Advertising a specific payment can trigger additional disclosures under the Truth in Lending rules. See [trigger terms in mortgage advertising](/blog/trigger-terms-mortgage-advertising/).
- **Add a disclaimer**: results are estimates, actual rates and payments depend on credit, loan program and other factors, and the calculator is not a loan approval or commitment.
- **Do not collect sensitive data** inside the calculator.

See [mortgage advertising compliance](/blog/mortgage-advertising-compliance/) for the broader rules.

## What makes a calculator pleasant to use?

- **Fast on phones**, with large inputs and instant results.
- **Plain labels**: "Home price," "Down payment," "Interest rate."
- **Sensible defaults** for your local market, labeled as examples.
- **A results breakdown** showing each part of the payment.
- **Accessible** to keyboard and screen-reader users.
- **A next step**: "Want exact numbers? Talk to a loan officer," with a short form or booking link.

## Should you use an embedded widget or a custom calculator?

Embedded widgets are quick but often slow, generic and hosted on someone else's domain, which can hurt the experience and any search value. Custom calculators built into your site load faster, match your brand and can be tuned to your local taxes and insurance. On the mortgage sites we build, calculators are part of the design, not iframes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are mortgage calculators accurate?

They are estimates. Accuracy depends on the inputs, especially taxes, insurance and the rate a borrower actually qualifies for.

### Do I need a disclaimer on a mortgage calculator?

Yes. State that results are estimates, not offers or approvals, and that actual terms depend on the borrower and loan program.

### Should a mortgage calculator include PMI?

Yes, for conventional loans with less than 20% down, and mortgage insurance for FHA loans. Leaving it out understates the payment.

### Can I use a free mortgage calculator widget?

You can, but custom calculators load faster, match your site and can include local tax and insurance assumptions.
