A real estate website is not a brochure; it is a set of pages that each do a job. Planning those pages before design starts is the difference between a site that captures leads and one that just looks nice. Here is the essential list, what belongs on each page and the optional extras worth adding.

## What pages does every real estate agent website need?

### Home

Your first screen should say who you are, where you work and what to do next. Below that: a short introduction, featured listings, a seller offer, reviews and links to your main pages. Keep it fast and focused.

### About

The page referrals read most. Include your story, experience, why you do this work, your team, credentials and real photos. Add reviews and recent results. People hire people they feel they know.

### Listings

Your own listings deserve rich pages: photos, video, floor plans, features, neighborhood details and a showing request form. A dedicated [single property website](/blog/single-property-websites/) for each listing goes further and doubles as marketing proof for future sellers.

### Home valuation

Often the best seller lead tool on the site. A short form leads to a personal valuation from you, not just an automated estimate. See [home valuation landing pages](/blog/home-valuation-landing-page/).

### Sellers

How you market homes, your process from listing to closing, what sellers can expect, and results. End with a call to book a listing consultation.

### Buyers

The buying process in your market, financing basics, what to expect at each stage, and how you help. Link to trusted local lenders if you work with them, following the rules on referrals.

### Neighborhoods

One page per area you know well: what it is like to live there, housing types, schools information from official sources, amenities and recent activity. Honest, specific guides beat generic copy. These pages also help you show up in local searches.

### Reviews

Real reviews with names and context, plus links to your Google profile. Fresh reviews matter more than a huge old collection.

### Contact

Phone, email, a short form, a booking link, the areas you serve and your brokerage details.

### Legal pages

A privacy policy that matches what your forms collect, plus brokerage and license information as your state requires, and the Equal Housing Opportunity logo or statement.

## What pages do real estate teams need?

- **Agent profiles** for each team member, with their own contact details and reviews.
- **Join the team** page for recruiting, with your culture, support and how to apply.
- **Team results** showing combined production honestly.

See [real estate team websites](/blog/real-estate-team-website/) for more.

## Which optional pages are worth adding?

- **Niche pages** for specialties: luxury, waterfront, condos, relocation, investors or first-time buyers.
- **Market updates** published monthly.
- **Sold homes** gallery.
- **Resources** such as moving checklists and preferred vendors.
- **Blog or guides** answering common questions.

## How should pages connect to each other?

Every page should lead somewhere useful:

- Neighborhood pages link to listings in that area and the valuation page.
- Listing pages link to the neighborhood guide and a showing request.
- Seller pages link to the valuation page and results.
- Every page has a clear contact option.

Good internal links help visitors and help search engines understand your site.

## What should every page include?

- A clear headline about that page's topic.
- Real photos, not stock.
- One main call to action.
- Fast loading on phones.
- Your contact details and brokerage information.

## Which pages should you build first?

If budget or time is tight, launch with home, about, a valuation page, your current listings and contact. Those five cover credibility, seller leads and buyer inquiries. Add neighborhood pages, buyer and seller guides and reviews over the following weeks, one or two at a time, so each gets proper attention instead of filler text.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most important page on a real estate website?

For most agents, the home valuation page drives the most valuable leads, and the about page does the most to convert referrals.

### Do I need a page for every neighborhood?

Only for areas you know well and can write about genuinely. A few strong neighborhood pages beat many thin ones.

### Should my listings be on my own website?

Yes. Your listings with rich pages show sellers how you market homes and give every listing campaign a professional destination.

### What legal information does a real estate website need?

Your brokerage name, license details as your state requires, a privacy policy and equal housing language. Check your state and brokerage rules.
