Most owners who ask us this question are really asking two things: "Will a template make me look like everyone else?" and "Is custom worth the extra money?" Both are fair. Here is how the two options actually differ, using our own builds and prices, and how to tell which one fits your business right now.

## What is the difference between a template and a custom website?

A **template website** starts from a design that already exists and has been tested: page layouts, sections, navigation and styles. The work is adapting it to your business with your branding, copy, photos and lead forms.

A **custom website** starts from a blank page. The design is made for your business: its structure, visual system, interactions and the path a visitor takes to become a lead.

The important part is that "template" does not have to mean a cheap theme bought online and left as-is. A good template build still gets your colors, fonts, content and integrations. ## When is a template website the right choice?

A template is usually the better call when:

- **You need to launch quickly**, for example before a busy season or an ad campaign.
- **Your business works like others in your industry.** A real estate agent, a title company or a mortgage broker needs the same core pages as their peers. A proven layout for that industry is an advantage, not a compromise.
- **Your budget is better spent elsewhere**, like ads, photography or a CRM.
- **You are still testing your offer** and expect to change it within a year.

Many of the sites in [our work](/work/) started from a strong base design and were adapted heavily, which is how we keep timelines short without sites looking alike.

## When is a custom website worth the money?

Custom earns its price when the template would fight you. Signs of that:

1. **Your offer does not fit a standard layout.** A product, app or service with an unusual buying process needs pages designed around it.
2. **Your brand is the product.** If your look is how customers tell you apart, a borrowed layout dilutes it.
3. **Your lead process is complex.** Quote calculators, multi-step intake, member areas or deep CRM connections often need custom structure.
4. **You have outgrown a template.** You keep asking for changes that the layout cannot support.

If several of those apply, start at our [custom website](/services/websites/) option. If only one applies, a heavily adapted template may still be the smarter spend.

## Will a template website hurt my SEO?

No. Search engines do not rank sites lower for using a template. What matters for search is that the site loads fast, works on phones, has clear page titles and descriptions, answers the questions people search for, and is easy to crawl.

The risk with cheap templates is different: bloated code that loads slowly, or every page sharing the same title. A properly built template site avoids both. We build every site, template or custom, with fast-loading pages, a sitemap, structured data and individual page titles, then connect it to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.

## Can I start with a template and go custom later?

Yes, and for many businesses that is the best path. Launch on a template, learn which pages bring in leads and which services sell, then invest in custom design where it matters. The second build is better because it is based on real data instead of guesses.

Two things make that move painless:

- **Own your site from the start.** If a template locks you into a platform you cannot leave, moving later is a rebuild. Our guide on [who owns your website](/blog/who-owns-my-website/) explains what to check.
- **Keep your URLs stable** so your search rankings carry over when you redesign.

## How do you decide between template and custom?

Answer these honestly:

- Do I need to launch in the next month? If yes, lean template.
- Is my business similar to others in my industry? If yes, lean template.
- Is my brand a main reason customers choose me? If yes, lean custom.
- Do I need calculators, portals or unusual flows? If yes, lean custom.
- Would the extra money do more as ad spend or a CRM? If yes, lean template.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will my template website look like other businesses' sites?

It shares a layout with other sites built on the same design, but your branding, photos, copy and color choices make it yours. Most visitors never notice a shared layout; they notice slow pages and stock photos.

### Is a custom website better for getting leads?

Not automatically. Leads come from clear offers, fast pages, easy contact options and fast follow-up. A custom site helps when your lead process is unusual; for a standard service business, a well-built template can convert just as well.

### Can I switch templates later?

Usually, yes, if you own the site and its content. The content and pages carry over, and the new design is applied on top. Keep the same URLs so search rankings follow you.

### How many revisions do I get?

That depends on the package and is set out in your written quote. Template builds focus revisions on content and styling; custom builds include design rounds before the build starts.
