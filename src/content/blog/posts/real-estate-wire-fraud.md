For most people, the down payment is the largest single payment they will ever make. That is exactly why criminals target real estate closings. Wire fraud schemes are well organized, convincing and fast. The good news is that a few habits stop nearly all of them. This guide is for buyers, sellers, agents and title companies.

## How does real estate wire fraud work?

The most common pattern:

1. Criminals gain access to an email account belonging to an agent, title company, lender, attorney or buyer, often through phishing.
2. They watch the transaction quietly, learning names, dates and amounts.
3. Just before closing, they send an email that looks genuine, with "updated" wiring instructions pointing to their account.
4. The buyer wires the money. It is moved quickly through other accounts.

Sometimes they use look-alike email addresses that differ by one letter instead of a hacked account. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center reports large losses to business email compromise every year, and real estate transactions are a frequent target.

## What are the warning signs of wire fraud?

- Wiring instructions that change at the last minute.
- Urgency: "Wire today or you will lose the house."
- An email address or phone number that is slightly different from before.
- Requests to keep the change confidential.
- Poor grammar or an unusual tone from someone you know.
- Instructions to wire to a person rather than a company, or to an unfamiliar bank or state.

## How can buyers protect themselves?

- **Get wiring instructions in person or by a known phone number** at the start of the transaction.
- **Never trust emailed changes.** Title companies rarely change wiring instructions.
- **Call to verify** using a number from the title company's official website or your contract, never a number in the email.
- **Confirm receipt** by phone right after wiring.
- **Consider alternatives** such as a cashier's check if your title company accepts it.

## How can agents and title companies prevent it?

- Tell clients at the first contact, in writing and verbally, that wiring instructions will never change by email.
- Put a wire fraud warning in email signatures and closing documents.
- Use secure portals for sharing wiring instructions.
- Turn on multi-factor authentication for every email account.
- Train staff to spot phishing.
- Verify any change to payment instructions by phone, both directions.
- Keep a dedicated wire fraud page on your website. See [title company website design](/blog/title-company-website-design/).

- A clear statement that your wiring instructions will not change by email.
- How to verify instructions: call a listed number and ask for a named role.
- Warning signs, in plain language.
- What to do immediately if something seems wrong.
- A link to report fraud at ic3.gov.

## What should you do if you wired money to a scammer?

Act within minutes, not hours:

1. **Call your bank immediately** and ask them to recall the wire and contact the receiving bank. Ask for their fraud department.
2. **Report it to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center** at ic3.gov as soon as possible. Fast reporting improves the chance of recovery.
3. **Contact local police** and get a report number.
4. **Notify your title company, agent and lender.**
5. **Secure your email**: change passwords and turn on multi-factor authentication.

## Does wire fraud insurance help?

Some title companies and agencies carry policies that may cover certain losses, but coverage varies and is not guaranteed. Prevention is far more reliable than recovery.

## How should agents talk about wire fraud with clients?

Make it part of every transaction kickoff: a short conversation, a printed or emailed notice, and a reminder a few days before closing. Clients remember warnings that come from a person they trust. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do title companies change wiring instructions by email?

Legitimate title companies very rarely change wiring instructions, and most say they never will by email. Treat any emailed change as suspicious and verify by phone.

### Can wired money be recovered?

Sometimes, if the bank and the FBI are contacted very quickly. The chances drop sharply with every hour of delay.

### Who is responsible if a buyer is scammed?

It depends on the facts, contracts and laws involved. Victims should contact their bank, the FBI and an attorney promptly.

### How do I verify wiring instructions?

Call the title company using a phone number from its official website or your contract, not from the email, and confirm the instructions verbally.
