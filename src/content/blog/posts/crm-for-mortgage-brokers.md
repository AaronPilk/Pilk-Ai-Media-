Mortgage is a follow-up business. Borrowers shop several lenders, realtors expect updates, and past clients are your best source of future loans. A CRM is what keeps all of that from depending on memory and sticky notes. Here is what a mortgage CRM should do and how to choose one.

## Why do mortgage brokers need a CRM?

Without one, leads fall through cracks:

- Website, referral, social and phone leads land in different places.
- Follow-up depends on who remembers.
- Realtors chase you for updates.
- Past clients hear from you once a year, if that, and refinance with someone else.

A CRM puts every contact, conversation and next step in one place and automates the routine touches.

## What should a mortgage CRM do?

**Lead capture and response**
- Pull in leads from your website, ads, referral partners and calls automatically.
- Send an instant text and email to every new lead.
- Alert the right loan officer immediately.

**Pipeline tracking**
- Stages from new lead to pre-approval, application, processing, closing and funded.
- Tasks and reminders so nothing stalls.

**Referral partner communication**
- Track which realtors and builders send business.
- Send partners milestone updates, such as application received or clear to close, where your process allows.

**Past-client nurturing**
- Birthday and home-anniversary messages.
- Market and rate updates.
- Prompts to review refinance opportunities when conditions change.

**Reporting**
- Leads and closings by source, response times and conversion rates.

## What compliance points matter in a mortgage CRM?

This is general information, not legal advice:

- **Texting consent and registration.** Automated texts need consent and carrier registration. See [A2P 10DLC registration](/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration/).
- **Advertising rules apply to messages.** Rates and payment amounts in texts and emails need the same disclosures as ads.
- **Data security.** Keep sensitive borrower data in your secure loan system, not in CRM notes or form fields.
- **Opt-outs** honored immediately across text and email.

## How should mortgage lead follow-up work?

A strong default sequence:

1. Instant text and email confirming the request, with a booking link.
2. Loan officer call within minutes during business hours.
3. Follow-up text the same day if no answer.
4. Calls and texts over the next several days.
5. Longer nurture with helpful content for borrowers who are not ready yet.

Your website forms should create contacts in the CRM with the source, loan type and timeline, then trigger the follow-up sequence. Calculators and program pages can tag contacts by interest, so a borrower who used the refinance calculator gets refinance content. ## How do you move to a new mortgage CRM without losing anything?

Export contacts, past clients and partner lists from your current system, clean out duplicates and old test records, and import them with tags for source and loan status. Rebuild your most important automations first: new lead response, application reminders and past-client touches. Run the old and new systems side by side for a week or two so no lead slips through, then switch your website forms and phone number over. Tell your team the date everything moves.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best CRM for mortgage brokers?

It depends on your size and needs. Larger teams often prefer mortgage-specific CRMs with loan system integration; independent brokers often prefer flexible platforms with texting and funnels built in.

### Can GoHighLevel work for mortgage brokers?

Yes. Many independent brokers use it for lead capture, texting, nurture sequences and review requests, connected to their loan system through integrations.

### Should borrower documents go in the CRM?

No. Keep sensitive documents and data in your secure loan origination system. The CRM should hold contact details, stages and conversation history.

### How do I get past clients to come back for refinances?

Stay in touch with useful, compliant updates and personal check-ins, and use your CRM to flag clients when a refinance review might make sense.
