Skool and Circle are two of the most popular platforms for paid and free online communities. They solve the same problem with very different philosophies: Skool keeps everything simple and the same for everyone; Circle lets you build almost anything. Here is how they compare and how to choose.

## What is the main difference between Skool and Circle?

**Skool** is opinionated. Every community has the same structure: a feed, a classroom for courses, a calendar, member profiles, a leaderboard with levels and an about page. You cannot change much about how it looks. That simplicity makes it fast to launch and easy for members to use. See [what Skool is](/blog/what-is-skool/).

**Circle** is flexible. You build your community from spaces, such as discussions, courses, events, chat and member directories, arranged the way you want, with your own branding. Higher plans add more customization, automation and branded apps.

## How do Skool and Circle compare?

| | Skool | Circle |
|---|---|---|
| Setup speed | Very fast | More setup |
| Branding | Minimal | Extensive, including custom domains |
| Layout | Fixed | Customizable spaces |
| Courses | Simple | More options |
| Gamification | Built-in leaderboard and levels | Available, less central |
| Discovery | Public directory of communities | Mostly your own traffic |
| Payments | Built in, with transaction fees by plan | Built in through payment integrations |
| Pricing structure | Two plans | Several tiers with more features at higher levels |

Both change plans and prices regularly. As of late 2026, Skool's Hobby plan is $9 a month with a 10% plus 30 cents transaction fee, and its Pro plan is $99 a month with a 2.9% plus 30 cents fee. Check Circle's current pricing page for its tiers.

## When is Skool the better choice?

- You want to launch this week, not next month.
- Engagement matters more than branding.
- Your members are not technical and want something obvious to use.
- You like the idea of members finding you through Skool's discovery directory.
- Your offer is community-first: calls, discussion and accountability, with courses as support.

## When is Circle the better choice?

- Your brand matters and you want the community to look and feel like your business.
- You need a custom domain and more control over structure.
- You run several programs or tiers with different spaces and access levels.
- You want more advanced course, event or automation features.
- You plan to offer a branded mobile app, available on higher Circle plans.

## How do fees compare for a paid community?

Look at total cost at your expected revenue: monthly plan plus transaction fees plus payment processing. On Skool, the Hobby plan's higher transaction fee costs more than the Pro plan once your member revenue grows. Circle's tiers and fees differ, so model both with your real numbers. ## Which platform grows communities faster?

The platform rarely decides growth. Your audience, offer, onboarding and weekly rhythm do. Skool's discovery directory and leaderboard give it a slight edge for engagement and organic discovery; Circle's branding can help an established business convert its own audience. See [how to grow a Skool community](/blog/how-to-grow-a-skool-community/).

## Can you move from one platform to the other?

Yes, with effort. Members can be invited to the new platform, course content can be rebuilt, and payments migrated depending on how they are set up. Plan the move, give members notice and keep both open briefly. Pick carefully at the start to avoid moving later.

## What should you test before committing?

Both platforms offer ways to try before you commit. Build a small test community on each with your real welcome post, one lesson and one event, invite a few trusted members, and watch what they do. Notice which one members actually come back to, how long setup took you, and how payments and access feel from the member's side. Those observations beat any feature comparison.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Skool cheaper than Circle?

It depends on your plan and revenue. Skool's two plans are simple; Circle has several tiers. Compare total cost including transaction fees at your expected membership revenue.

### Can I use my own domain with Skool?

Skool communities live on Skool's platform under its address format. Circle offers custom domains. Check each platform's current plan features.

### Which is better for courses, Skool or Circle?

Circle offers more course options; Skool's classroom is simpler. For community-first offers with supporting courses, Skool is often enough.

### Which platform do members prefer?

Members often find Skool easier because every community looks the same. Circle can feel more polished and branded when set up well.
