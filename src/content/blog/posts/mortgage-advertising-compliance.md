Every page on a mortgage website and every social post is an advertisement in the eyes of regulators. Most compliance problems we see are not bad intentions; they are a rate quoted without an APR, a "no closing costs" headline, or a missing NMLS number. This guide explains the main rules in plain English. It is general information, not legal advice. Your compliance officer or attorney has the final word.

## Which rules apply to mortgage advertising?

| Rule | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Truth in Lending Act, Regulation Z (12 CFR 1026.24) | How rates, payments and terms are advertised |
| Mortgage Acts and Practices rule, Regulation N (12 CFR 1014) | Misleading claims in mortgage ads |
| RESPA Section 8 | Kickbacks and referral fees |
| Fair Housing Act and Equal Credit Opportunity Act | Discrimination in advertising and lending |
| Unfair, deceptive or abusive acts and practices | Anything that misleads consumers |
| SAFE Act and state licensing rules | NMLS identification and licensing disclosures |

The [CFPB's Regulation Z](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/24/) text is the primary source for the advertising section.

## How do you advertise mortgage rates correctly?

Under Regulation Z, if an ad states a rate of finance charge, it must state it as an annual percentage rate, using that term. You can show the note rate too, but not more prominently than the APR. If the rate can increase after closing, the ad must say so.

Because rates change daily, most brokers avoid static rates on websites and use a "get today's rate" form instead.

## What are trigger terms?

Certain details in an ad "trigger" extra disclosures. Regulation Z lists four: the amount or percentage of a down payment, the number of payments or repayment period, the amount of any payment, and the amount of any finance charge. The official commentary limits the down payment trigger to credit sale transactions, so for most loan ads the practical triggers are the repayment period, payment amounts and finance charges. If you use one, you must also disclose the down payment, the terms of repayment and the APR.

So "30-year mortgage" or "Payments from $1,500 a month!" requires more information nearby, and ads for loans secured by a home have extra rules on how payments are shown. General phrases like "low down payment options" usually do not trigger disclosures. Our guide to [trigger terms in mortgage advertising](/blog/trigger-terms-mortgage-advertising/) goes through examples.

## What does the MAP rule prohibit?

The Mortgage Acts and Practices rule bans material misrepresentations in mortgage ads. Common examples:

- Calling a rate "fixed" when it can change.
- Implying government affiliation or endorsement.
- Claiming "no fees" or "no closing costs" when costs are paid another way.
- Misstating the amount of savings, payments or how a product works.
- Using names or logos that suggest you are a different company.

## How does RESPA affect mortgage marketing?

RESPA Section 8 prohibits giving or accepting anything of value for referrals of settlement business. That matters for co-marketing with realtors, lead purchasing and joint ventures. Shared ads must reflect each party's fair share of the cost. Affiliated relationships need proper disclosure; see [affiliated business arrangement disclosures](/blog/affiliated-business-arrangement-disclosure/).

## What fair lending rules apply to ads?

Ads must not express a preference or limitation based on protected characteristics, and targeting must not exclude protected groups. On Meta, housing and financial products ads run under special ad categories that restrict targeting. See [Facebook ads for real estate agents](/blog/facebook-ads-for-real-estate-agents/) for how those limits work in practice.

Display the Equal Housing Opportunity or Equal Housing Lender logo or statement as your regulators and company policy require.

## What should every mortgage website show?

- Company legal name and NMLS number.
- Individual loan officer NMLS numbers where they appear.
- State licensing information as required.
- A link to NMLS Consumer Access.
- Equal housing language.
- Accurate program descriptions without guarantees of approval.
- A privacy policy that matches what your forms collect.

And never collect sensitive data like Social Security numbers through a marketing form. Send borrowers to your secure application system.

## Do the rules apply to social media?

Yes. Federal regulators have said social media is subject to the same consumer protection rules as other advertising. A rate in an Instagram caption needs the same disclosures as a rate on a billboard, and loan officers' personal accounts can count as advertising when they promote loans.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I put mortgage rates on my website?

You can, if they are stated as an APR with required disclosures and kept accurate. Most brokers avoid static rates because they change daily.

### Is "no closing costs" allowed in mortgage ads?

Only if it is true. If costs are covered through a higher rate or another way, saying "no closing costs" can be a misrepresentation.

### Do loan officers' social posts need disclosures?

Yes, when they advertise loans. The same rules apply on social media, and many companies require compliance review of loan officer accounts.

### Who enforces mortgage advertising rules?

The CFPB, the FTC and state regulators, depending on the company. In Florida, the Office of Financial Regulation licenses and supervises mortgage businesses.
