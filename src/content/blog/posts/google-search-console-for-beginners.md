If you only use one free SEO tool, make it Google Search Console. It shows what Google sees: which pages are indexed, which searches you appear for and what is broken. It looks intimidating at first, but small business owners only need a handful of features. Here they are.

## How do you set up Google Search Console?

1. **Go to Google Search Console** and sign in with a Google account the business controls.
2. **Add a property.** A Domain property covers every version of your site (with and without www, and all subdomains). It is verified with a DNS record at your domain provider.
3. **Verify ownership** by adding the DNS record, or another method if you choose a URL-prefix property.
4. **Add users** such as your web developer, with the right permission level, rather than sharing your login.

## How do you submit a sitemap?

Your sitemap lists the pages you want Google to know about, usually at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. In Search Console, open Sitemaps, enter the sitemap address and submit. Check back for a "Success" status and the number of discovered pages. Resubmit after big changes such as a redesign.

## Which reports matter most for a small business?

### Performance

Shows the searches (queries) your site appears for, with impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position. Filter by page to see what each page ranks for. Use it to find:

- Searches with many impressions but few clicks: improve titles and descriptions.
- Pages gaining or losing traffic.
- New topics customers search for that deserve their own page.

### Pages (indexing)

Shows which pages are indexed and why others are not. Common reasons include "Discovered – currently not indexed," "Crawled – currently not indexed," "Page with redirect," "Alternate page with proper canonical tag" and "Not found (404)." Not every non-indexed page is a problem; redirects and intentionally hidden pages are normal. Focus on important pages that should be indexed but are not.

### Core Web Vitals and HTTPS

Flag slow pages and security issues. Fix problems on your most important pages first.

## How do you use URL inspection?

Type any page address into the bar at the top. Search Console tells you whether that page is on Google, when it was last crawled and whether it can be indexed. If it is new or updated, click **Request indexing** to ask Google to crawl it sooner. Google limits how many requests you can make per day, so save them for important pages. Inspecting a page does not use that limit.

## What should you check every month?

A fifteen-minute routine:

1. **Performance**: total clicks and impressions versus last month, and top queries.
2. **Pages**: any important pages newly not indexed.
3. **Sitemaps**: status still "Success."
4. **Experience**: new speed or security warnings.
5. **Messages**: any notices from Google about manual actions or security issues.

## What common problems does Search Console reveal?

- **Pages accidentally set to noindex** after a redesign.
- **Missing redirects**, showing up as 404 errors for old addresses.
- **Duplicate pages** with and without trailing slashes or parameters.
- **Slow mobile pages.**
- **Pages Google found but chose not to index**, often thin or similar to other pages.

## Does Search Console show AI Overview traffic?

AI Overview impressions and clicks are counted within the normal Performance report, not in a separate report, so watch query trends over time. See [how to show up in Google AI Overviews](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-google-ai-overviews/).

## Who should have access to your Search Console?

The business should own the property through an account it controls, such as the owner's business Google account. Add your developer or agency as a user with Full or Restricted permission instead of sharing a password, and remove access when the relationship ends. If a former agency set it up under their account, ask them to add you as an Owner so you are never locked out of your own data.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Google Search Console free?

Yes. It is a free tool from Google for site owners.

### What is the difference between Search Console and Google Analytics?

Search Console shows how your site performs in Google Search before people click. Analytics shows what visitors do on your site after they arrive.

### How many indexing requests can I make per day?

Google limits URL indexing requests per property per day. Use them for new or significantly updated important pages.

### Why does Search Console show fewer pages than my site has?

Some pages may not be indexed yet, may redirect, may be duplicates or may be intentionally hidden. Check the Pages report for reasons.
