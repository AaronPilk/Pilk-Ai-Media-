Redesigns are supposed to improve results, but many sites lose search traffic and leads after launch because important steps get skipped. This checklist is the one we work through on every redesign. Use it with any designer, including us.

## Before the redesign: what should you plan?

- [ ] **Define goals**: more leads, better conversion, clearer services, faster pages.
- [ ] **Record current numbers**: monthly visitors, leads, top pages, and search queries from Search Console.
- [ ] **Confirm access**: domain registrar, hosting, DNS, Google Search Console, analytics, Business Profile and CRM.
- [ ] **Back up the current site** and keep a copy of every page's content.
- [ ] **List integrations**: forms, booking, chat, CRM, payment and tracking tools.

## What should the content audit cover?

- [ ] **List every page** with its traffic and search performance.
- [ ] **Decide for each page**: keep, improve, merge or remove.
- [ ] **Keep pages that rank** and improve them rather than deleting them.
- [ ] **Rewrite weak pages** with clear answers, real photos and specific details.
- [ ] **Plan new pages** for missing services and areas. See [how many pages a website should have](/blog/how-many-pages-should-a-website-have/).

## How do you protect SEO during a redesign?

This is where traffic is won or lost:

- [ ] **Keep page addresses the same where possible.**
- [ ] **Map every changed address** to its new equivalent.
- [ ] **Set up permanent (301) redirects** for every old address that changes or is removed.
- [ ] **Carry over page titles and descriptions**, improving them carefully.
- [ ] **Keep headings and key content** on pages that rank.
- [ ] **Add structured data**: Organization or LocalBusiness, plus FAQ and Article where they fit.
- [ ] **Keep internal links** pointing to the right new pages.
- [ ] **Check the new site is not set to noindex** at launch. This happens more often than you would think.

## What functionality should you test?

- [ ] **Every form** on phone and desktop, confirming emails, texts and CRM contacts arrive. See [lead form best practices](/blog/lead-form-best-practices/).
- [ ] **Booking calendars** and confirmations.
- [ ] **Click-to-call and click-to-text links.**
- [ ] **Analytics and conversion tracking**, including the Meta Pixel if you run ads. See [Meta Pixel vs Conversions API](/blog/meta-pixel-vs-conversions-api/).
- [ ] **Speed** on a real mobile connection.
- [ ] **Accessibility basics**: contrast, alt text, keyboard navigation and form labels.
- [ ] **Privacy policy** updated to match what forms and tracking collect.

## What should happen on launch day?

- [ ] Point the domain and confirm HTTPS works on every version (with and without www).
- [ ] Test redirects from old addresses.
- [ ] Submit the new sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- [ ] Request indexing for the most important pages.
- [ ] Submit a test lead through every form.
- [ ] Update your Google Business Profile and listings if any details changed.

## What should you watch after launch?

- [ ] **Search Console** for crawl errors, 404s and indexing drops, weekly for the first month.
- [ ] **Leads** compared with your pre-launch numbers.
- [ ] **Rankings** for your main searches.
- [ ] **Broken links** reported by visitors or tools.

A small dip right after launch can be normal while Google recrawls. A large or lasting drop usually means missing redirects or indexing problems. See [Google Search Console for beginners](/blog/google-search-console-for-beginners/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a website redesign hurt my SEO?

It can if addresses change without redirects or content is removed. With careful redirects and content carried over, redesigns often improve SEO.

### How long does a website redesign take?

From a few days for a refresh on a proven design to several weeks for a custom rebuild. Content readiness is usually the biggest factor.

### Should I keep my domain name in a redesign?

Yes, in almost all cases. Changing domains adds risk and requires a careful migration.

### What is a 301 redirect?

A permanent redirect from an old address to a new one. It sends visitors and search engines to the right page and passes along most of the old page's search value.
