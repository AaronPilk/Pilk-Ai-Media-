## What actually slows a website project down?

In our experience the build is the predictable part. These are the things that move launch dates:

1. **Content that is not ready.** Service descriptions, team bios, photos and pricing take owners longer to gather than they think. A site cannot launch with placeholder text.
2. **Access to accounts.** The domain registrar, old hosting, Google Business Profile, CRM and ad accounts each need a login or an invite. One missing password can hold up launch day.
3. **Slow approvals.** Every review round that waits a week adds a week.
4. **Scope that grows mid-project.** Adding a booking system or five new pages halfway through resets parts of the schedule.
5. **Decisions by committee.** When three partners each need to approve the homepage, plan for extra time.

None of these are anyone's fault. They are simply where the time goes, so planning for them is the fastest way to launch.

## How can you get your website built faster?

If you want to launch quickly, do these before the project starts:

- **Write down your services**, one short paragraph each, even if it is rough. Editing is faster than writing from nothing.
- **Gather 10 to 20 real photos** of your work, team or location. Real photos beat stock and save search time.
- **List every login** you have: domain, hosting, email, Google, social accounts.
- **Pick one decision maker** for approvals.
- **Choose a template** if speed matters more than originality. Our guide to [template vs custom websites](/blog/template-vs-custom-website/) explains the trade-off.

Owners who show up with those five things ready are the ones who launch on the first date we give them.

## Does a faster website build mean a worse website?

Not necessarily. A template build is fast because the design and structure are already proven, not because steps are skipped. What a fast build cannot do is invent content you have not supplied, or design something original from scratch in a few days.

Speed becomes a problem when it means skipping the parts you cannot see: mobile testing, page speed, form testing, analytics and search setup. Before launch, every site we build goes through the same checks whether it took three days or three months: forms tested end to end, pages checked on a phone, the sitemap submitted, and the site connected to Search Console.

## How long does a website redesign take compared to a new site?

A redesign is often faster on design and slower on everything else. You already have content, but some of it is out of date, and you usually have search rankings to protect. That means mapping old URLs to new ones and setting redirects so you do not lose the traffic you have.

Plan for roughly the same build time as a new site of the same type, plus time to review the old content and set up redirects. Our guide to [website redesign cost](/blog/website-redesign-cost/) covers what changes in price.

## What happens in the first weeks after launch?

Launch day is not the finish line. In the first few weeks:

- **Search engines discover the new pages.** Submitting the sitemap and requesting indexing speeds this up, but new pages can still take days or weeks to show up.
- **You will spot small fixes**: a phone number on one page, a photo you want swapped. Budget for a short round of edits.
- **Leads start coming through the new forms.** Check that every form reaches the right inbox or CRM and that someone answers fast. A fast reply matters more than most design choices, which we explain in our post on [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a website be built in one day?

A very simple one-page site can be put together in a day if every piece of content is ready. A multi-page business site with forms, tracking and search setup takes longer to do properly, usually one to two weeks on a template.

### When does the timeline start?

For our projects, the timeline starts once we have your content, account access and approvals. If those arrive late, the launch date moves by the same amount, which we set out in every written quote.

### Why does a custom website take longer than a template?

A custom site needs original design rounds before any building starts, and each round needs your feedback. A template starts from a finished, tested design, so the time goes into adding your branding and content instead.

### Is it faster to build a website myself?

Not usually. A website builder removes the wait for a designer, but you take on every task yourself, from writing pages to connecting the domain. Most owners find it takes longer than they planned because the work happens around their day job.
