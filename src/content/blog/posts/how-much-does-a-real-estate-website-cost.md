Real estate agents get pitched websites constantly: by their brokerage, by lead-gen companies, by platforms that bundle a site with leads. The prices are hard to compare because each offer includes different things and some never let you own the result. This guide lays out the real costs, what IDX adds, and how to decide what you actually need.

## What are the main options for a real estate agent website?

Agents usually choose between four routes:

1. **The brokerage profile page.** Free, but it belongs to the brokerage, looks like every other agent's page and leaves with you never.
2. **An agent website platform.** A monthly subscription that bundles templates, IDX search and sometimes lead tools. Fast to set up, but you rent it and usually cannot move the design.
3. **A template-based site you own.** A professionally adapted design on your own domain, with IDX added if you want it.
4. **A custom site you own.** Designed around your brand, your farm area and how you get business.

There is no single right answer. A newer agent might start with option 2 or 3. A team or an established agent with a strong personal brand often outgrows rented platforms.

## What does IDX cost, and do you need it?

IDX (Internet Data Exchange) is what lets visitors search MLS listings on your site. It runs through an IDX provider approved by your MLS, and it comes with its own monthly fee paid to that provider, separate from your website. Some MLSs also have their own rules and paperwork for IDX display.

You need IDX if you want buyers to search all active listings on your site and register to save searches, which can be a good source of buyer leads. You may not need it if most of your business is sellers, referrals or a specific niche, where strong neighborhood pages and listing pages do more work. Our guide to [IDX websites for realtors](/blog/idx-website-for-realtors/) covers the trade-offs in more depth.

## What monthly costs come with a real estate website?

Budget for these ongoing costs on top of the build:

- **Domain renewal** each year.
- **Hosting**, which you can manage yourself or hand off. Our hosting and support plans start at {{price:hosting}}.
- **IDX subscription** if you add listing search.
- **CRM** if leads go into one, such as GoHighLevel or a real estate CRM.
- **Ads**, if you send paid traffic to the site.

Platform subscriptions often bundle several of these, which is why their monthly price looks higher than hosting alone.

## Why do some agents pay much more for a website?

The price climbs when the site has to do more than look professional:

- **Lead capture that feeds a CRM** with automatic text and email follow-up.
- **Neighborhood and community pages** with real local information, which help with search.
- **Home valuation pages** that capture seller leads.
- **Team features**: agent profiles, lead routing and recruiting pages.
- **Custom design** for agents whose brand is a big part of why clients choose them.

Each of these can pay for itself, but only if it fits how you get business. A referral-based agent does not need the same site as a team running ads every month.

## Should you rent a platform site or own your website?

Renting is fine while you are testing. The downside shows up when you leave: you lose the design, often the content structure, and sometimes the search rankings you built, because the site lives on the platform.

Owning means your domain, your design and your content stay with you if you change brokerages, platforms or CRMs. Our [own vs rent](/own-vs-rent/) page explains the difference in detail, and our post on [who owns your website](/blog/who-owns-my-website/) lists what to check in any contract.

## How do you choose the right real estate website?

Answer these first:

1. Where do most of your clients come from today: referrals, sphere, ads, open houses or online search?
2. Do you work mainly with buyers, sellers or both?
3. Do you plan to run ads to the site?
4. Will you stay with your current brokerage, and does it matter if you move?
5. Do you need the site to follow up with leads automatically?

If you mostly need a polished home base plus pages for active listings, a template site and [single property websites](/blog/single-property-websites/) are often enough. If online leads are your growth plan, budget for a CRM and follow-up as well. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a brokerage website enough for a real estate agent?

It is enough to be found by name, but it belongs to the brokerage and goes away if you switch. An agent site on your own domain builds a brand and search presence that moves with you.

### How much does IDX add to a real estate website?

IDX is a monthly subscription paid to the IDX provider, plus a one-time setup on the website side. The provider fee varies by provider and MLS, so check current pricing with the provider your MLS approves.

### Can I get a real estate website without IDX?

Yes. Many agents do well without full listing search by focusing on their own listings, neighborhood guides, home valuation pages and a clear way to contact them.
