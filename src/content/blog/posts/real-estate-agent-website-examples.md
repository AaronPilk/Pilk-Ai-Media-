Looking at examples is the fastest way to decide what you want your own site to feel like. Rather than list random agent sites, this guide groups the examples that work into seven styles, explains what each does well and who it suits. Every style below is one we design and build, and you can browse live versions on our [templates](/templates/) page.

## What style suits a luxury agent?

**The luxury showcase.** Full-screen photography and video, generous white space, elegant type and very little clutter. Listings are presented like editorial features, with a story for each home.

- **Works for**: luxury, waterfront and high-end agents.
- **Watch out for**: heavy video that slows the site on phones. The luxury feel has to load fast.
- **Our versions**: Signature Estate and Coastal Luxe.

## What style suits a neighborhood expert?

**The local expert site.** Built around the areas you know: neighborhood guides, market updates, recent sales by area and clear calls to request a valuation.

- **Works for**: agents who dominate a few neighborhoods or a town.
- **Strength**: helpful local pages that can rank in search and impress sellers.
- **Key pages**: neighborhood guides and a valuation page. See [real estate website pages](/blog/real-estate-website-pages/).

## What style suits an agent building a personal brand?

**The personal brand site.** The agent is the center: a strong about page, video introduction, social content, reviews and a clear point of view.

- **Works for**: agents active on social media and video.
- **Strength**: turns followers into clients by giving them a place to go.
- **Example**: our work for [Stephenie Tocado](/work/stephenie-tocado/).

## What style suits a real estate team?

**The team site.** Team brand up front, agent profiles with their own contact details, combined results and a join-the-team page for recruiting.

- **Works for**: teams and small brokerages.
- **Strength**: routes leads to the right agent and supports recruiting.
- **Our version**: Brokerage Command.

## What style suits a single listing?

**The single property site.** One home, one site: gallery, video, floor plans, neighborhood details and a showing request form, often on its own domain or address.

- **Works for**: every listing, especially premium ones.
- **Strength**: impresses sellers and gives every listing campaign a professional destination.
- **More**: [single property websites](/blog/single-property-websites/).

## What style suits rentals and property management?

**The rental and management site.** Available rentals, owner services, tenant resources, applications through a secure portal and maintenance requests.

- **Works for**: property managers and agents with rental portfolios.
- **Strength**: separates owner and tenant journeys clearly.
- **Our version**: Rental Management.

## What is the simplest effective agent website?

**The listing landing page.** A single page for one listing or one offer, built fast for ads and social campaigns. It is not a full website, but it is often the quickest win.

- **Works for**: agents who need a campaign page now.
- **Strength**: speed and focus. See [landing page vs website](/blog/landing-page-vs-website/).

## What do all the good examples have in common?

Whatever the style, the best agent sites share the same basics:

1. **A clear local focus** in the first screen.
2. **Real photos** of the agent, listings and area.
3. **Proof**: reviews and results.
4. **A seller offer** such as a home valuation.
5. **Fast pages** on phones.
6. **Forms that reach you instantly** and trigger follow-up.

See [what makes a good real estate website](/blog/what-makes-a-good-real-estate-website/) for the full list.

## How do you choose the right style?

Ask where your business comes from. Luxury referrals point to a showcase. A farm area points to a local expert site. Social media points to a personal brand site. A team points to a team site. Many agents combine a main site with single property sites for each listing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What makes a real estate website stand out?

Real photography, a clear local focus, honest proof and a fast, simple experience on phones. Most agent sites look alike; specifics make yours memorable.

### Should I copy another agent's website?

Use other sites for ideas about structure and features, not copy. Your content, photos and story should be your own.

### Can I change my website style later?

Yes. Starting with a proven design and moving to custom later is common. Keep your domain and page addresses to protect search value.

### Which style generates the most leads?

The one that matches where your business comes from, backed by a seller offer and fast follow-up. Style matters less than offer and response time.
