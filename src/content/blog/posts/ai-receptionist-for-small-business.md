Missed calls are lost customers, especially for service businesses where the next company on the list is one tap away. AI receptionists promise to answer every call, day or night. Some are impressive. Some make callers hang up. This guide explains what they do well, what they cost and how to set one up so it helps rather than hurts.

## What does an AI receptionist do?

An AI receptionist is a voice AI connected to your phone line. Depending on setup, it can:

- Answer calls with your business name and a natural voice.
- Answer common questions about services, hours, areas served and pricing ranges.
- Book, reschedule or confirm appointments on your calendar.
- Qualify leads with a few questions and log details in your CRM.
- Transfer urgent calls to a person or take a detailed message.
- Send a follow-up text with a booking link or summary.

## Where do AI receptionists work best?

- **After hours and weekends**, when calls would otherwise go to voicemail.
- **Overflow**, when your team is busy and calls stack up.
- **Repetitive questions**, like hours, directions and service areas.
- **Simple bookings** with clear rules.

They work less well for complex sales conversations, upset customers, emergencies that need judgment, or anything where a wrong answer is costly.

## What does an AI receptionist cost?

Costs usually combine:

1. **Setup**: writing the call script and instructions, loading business information, connecting your calendar and CRM, and testing.
2. **Platform fee**: monthly software cost.
3. **Usage**: per-minute or per-call charges, which grow with volume.
4. **Phone number and carrier costs**, if you add a new line or texting.

## What rules apply to AI receptionists?

This is general information, not legal advice:

- **Call recording consent.** Some states, including Florida, require consent from everyone on a call before recording. If your AI receptionist records calls, the greeting should say so.
- **Disclosure.** Tell callers they are speaking with an automated assistant. People react better to honesty, and some laws and platforms require it.
- **Texting.** Follow-up texts need proper consent and carrier registration. See [TCPA text message consent](/blog/tcpa-text-message-consent/) and [A2P 10DLC registration](/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration/).
- **Regulated industries.** Mortgage, insurance, healthcare and legal businesses need tighter limits on what the AI may say.

## How do you set up an AI receptionist that callers do not hate?

1. **Keep the greeting short** and say it is an automated assistant.
2. **Limit its scope** to what it does well; let it say "let me get someone to call you back."
3. **Give callers an easy way to reach a person**, such as pressing a key or asking.
4. **Feed it accurate information** and update it when prices, hours or services change.
5. **Log every call in your CRM** with a summary and a follow-up task.
6. **Listen to recordings or read transcripts weekly** for the first month and fix what goes wrong.

## How do you know if it is working?

Track missed calls before and after, bookings made by the assistant, calls transferred, hang-ups during the call and customer complaints. If hang-ups are high, shorten the greeting and narrow its job.

## What information does an AI receptionist need from you?

The quality of the answers depends on what you give it. Before launch, prepare a short knowledge sheet: services you offer and do not offer, areas you serve, hours, how pricing works and the ranges you are comfortable sharing, booking rules, cancellation policy, and what counts as urgent. Add the ten questions your team hears most and the answers you want given. Decide who gets transferred calls and when. Then review it monthly, because an assistant quoting last year's prices does more harm than voicemail.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an AI receptionist book appointments?

Yes, when connected to your calendar with clear rules for appointment types, durations and availability. Test it carefully before going live.

### Will callers know they are talking to AI?

Many will, and you should tell them. A short, honest disclosure works better than pretending, and some rules require it.

### Is an AI receptionist better than an answering service?

It is cheaper and available instantly at any volume, but a human answering service handles unusual and emotional calls better. Some businesses use both.

### What happens if the AI does not know the answer?

A good setup has it take a message, promise a callback and alert your team, rather than guessing.
