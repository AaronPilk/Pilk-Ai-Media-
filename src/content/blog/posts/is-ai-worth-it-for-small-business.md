Every software company now sells "AI," and every owner is being told they will fall behind without it. Some of that is true; much of it is noise. This is an honest look at where AI pays off for small businesses, where it does not, and how to find out for your own business without wasting money.

## Where does AI pay off for small businesses?

AI earns its keep on work that is frequent, repetitive and has a clear right answer:

- **Responding to new leads instantly**, by text and email, around the clock. See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).
- **First drafts**: emails, proposals, job posts, social captions and descriptions.
- **Summarizing**: calls, meetings, long documents and email threads.
- **Moving information between apps**: forms to CRM, CRM to spreadsheets, invoices to accounting.
- **Answering common questions** on your website or phone, with handoff to a person.
- **Reminders and follow-up** that people forget.

## Where does AI disappoint?

- **Vague goals.** "Use AI to grow" leads to subscriptions nobody uses.
- **Unchecked output.** AI states wrong facts with confidence; customers notice.
- **Judgment-heavy work.** Pricing complex jobs, handling upset customers and negotiating still need people.
- **Messy data.** AI cannot fix a CRM full of duplicates and missing details.
- **Over-automation.** Customers dislike talking to bots that will not let them reach a person.

## What does AI really cost a small business?

| Cost | Typical range |
|---|---|
| AI assistant subscriptions | Free tiers; paid business plans often roughly $20 to $30 per user per month |
| Usage-based tools | Per message, minute or task; grows with volume |
| Automation platforms and CRMs | Monthly plans plus usage |
| Setup and integration | Your time or a consultant's |
| Ongoing upkeep | Updating prompts, rules and integrations |

The subscription is rarely the biggest cost. Setup time and the hours spent checking and correcting output matter more.

## How do you calculate whether AI is worth it?

A simple test for any task:

1. **Hours per week** the task takes now.
2. **Value of an hour** of that person's time.
3. **Hours saved** with AI, honestly estimated, minus time spent checking.
4. **Monthly cost** of the tool and upkeep.

If (hours saved × hourly value) clearly beats the monthly cost, it is worth testing. For lead follow-up, also count the value of leads you stop losing, which is often the biggest number.

## How do you test AI cheaply?

- Pick **one task** with the most hours and a clear right answer.
- Use a **free or low-cost plan** for two to four weeks.
- **Measure** hours saved and quality.
- **Keep, fix or drop** it.

## Which industries see the clearest returns?

Service businesses where speed and follow-up drive revenue tend to see returns fastest: home services, real estate, mortgage, legal intake, healthcare scheduling and agencies. Businesses with lots of documents, such as title and insurance, benefit from summarizing and drafting, with careful review. See [AI for real estate agents](/blog/ai-for-real-estate-agents/) and [AI for mortgage loan officers](/blog/ai-for-mortgage-loan-officers/).

## What risks should you weigh?

- **Privacy**: customer data in consumer tools.
- **Accuracy**: wrong information sent to customers.
- **Compliance**: regulated industries have rules about what you say and how.
- **Dependence**: tools change pricing and features.

A short internal policy covers most of this. See [AI policy for employees](/blog/ai-policy-for-employees/).

## So, is AI worth it?

For most small businesses, yes, in specific places: fast lead response, drafts, summaries and connecting apps. No, as a vague promise to transform the business. Start narrow, measure honestly, and expand what works.

## What does a good first month with AI look like?

Week one, pick the task and set up the tool. Week two, run it alongside your normal process and compare results. Week three, fix what went wrong, such as tone, timing or wrong answers. Week four, measure hours saved and decide whether to keep it, expand it or drop it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AI too expensive for a small business?

Usually not. Many useful tools cost less than an hour of staff time per month. Setup and checking time are the real costs to plan for.

### What is the easiest AI win for a small business?

Instant replies to new leads by text and email, and missed call text back, tend to pay off quickly for service businesses.

### Can AI replace an employee?

It usually takes over parts of jobs, freeing people for higher-value work, rather than replacing whole roles in small businesses.

### How long until AI pays for itself?

For a well-chosen task like lead follow-up, often within the first month or two. Vague projects may never pay off.
