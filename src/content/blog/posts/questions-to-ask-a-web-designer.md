Most website disasters we get called in to fix were preventable with a few questions at the start: the owner who cannot log in to their own domain, the site that took nine months, the form that emailed an address nobody checks. Here are the questions worth asking any web designer, including us, and what a good answer sounds like.

## What should you ask about price and scope?

**1. What exactly is included in the price?** Pages, design rounds, copywriting, photos, forms, SEO setup, analytics and launch. Get it in writing.

**2. What costs extra?** Additional pages, revisions beyond a set number, new features, rush delivery. A good designer lists these up front.

**3. Is this a template or custom design?** Both can be right. You should know which you are paying for. See [template vs custom website](/blog/template-vs-custom-website/).

**4. How do payments work?** A deposit and a final payment at launch is common. Be cautious of paying everything up front.

## What should you ask about timeline?

**5. How long will it take, and what do you need from me?** Most delays come from waiting on content and feedback. A good answer includes your responsibilities, not just theirs.

**6. What happens if the timeline slips?** You want a clear process, not silence.

Our guide to [how long a website takes to build](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-build-a-website/) gives realistic ranges.

## What should you ask about ownership and control?

**7. Who owns the website when it is done?** You should own the content and the custom work you paid for, once paid.

**8. Whose account is the domain registered in?** It should be yours. This one question prevents more problems than any other.

**9. Can I move the site to another host later?** If the answer is no, understand why.

**10. Will I have admin access to everything?** Domain, hosting, analytics, Google Business Profile, forms and CRM.

We cover this in detail in [who owns my website](/blog/who-owns-my-website/).

## What should you ask about leads and results?

**11. Where do form submissions go?** To an inbox you check, a CRM, or both. Ask them to show you a test submission before launch.

**12. How will the site help me get found on Google?** Look for specifics: page titles and descriptions, a sitemap, Search Console setup, fast pages, structured data and help with your Google Business Profile.

**13. How will we know if the site is working?** Analytics, form tracking and call tracking where it matters.

If a site is not producing leads, our guide to [why websites do not generate leads](/blog/why-is-my-website-not-generating-leads/) covers the usual causes.

## What should you ask about after launch?

**14. What does hosting and support cost after launch?** Get the monthly figure and what it covers. Our plans start at {{price:hosting}} and are optional. See [how much website hosting costs](/blog/how-much-does-website-hosting-cost/).

**15. What happens if we stop working together?** You should be able to take your site, domain and data and go.

## What answers should be red flags?

- "We register the domain for you" with no mention of putting it in your name.
- No written proposal or contract.
- A price with no list of what is included.
- "SEO is extra" with no basic setup included at all.
- Vague timelines like "a few weeks, depending."
- No examples of real, live work you can visit.
- Pressure to sign today.

## What should you have ready before talking to a designer?

You will get better quotes and a faster project if you bring:

- Three to five sites you like and why.
- Your list of services and the areas you serve.
- Your logo and brand colors, if you have them.
- Photos of your work, team and location.
- A clear idea of what a lead is worth to you.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many web designers should I get quotes from?

Two or three is usually enough. Compare what is included, ownership terms and examples of work, not just price.

### Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?

Both can do great work. Freelancers often cost less; agencies usually offer more capacity and continuity. Ask the same questions either way.

### What should a web design contract include?

Scope, price, payment schedule, timeline, revision rounds, ownership of work, hosting terms, and what happens if either side ends the project.

### Is it a bad sign if a designer will not share prices?

Not always, since custom work varies. But a good designer should explain how they price and give a written quote before any work starts.
