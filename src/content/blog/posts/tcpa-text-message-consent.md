Texting is one of the most effective ways to reach leads and customers, and one of the most regulated. TCPA lawsuits are common, and damages are counted per message, so a careless campaign can become expensive fast. This guide explains the basics in plain English. It is general information, not legal advice; talk to an attorney about your specific messaging.

## What is the TCPA?

The Telephone Consumer Protection Act is a 1991 federal law that restricts telemarketing calls, automated calls and texts, prerecorded messages and similar communications. The Federal Communications Commission writes the rules that implement it. Courts treat text messages as calls for TCPA purposes.

## When do you need consent to text someone?

Consent requirements depend on the type of message and how it is sent:

| Message type | Example | Consent generally needed |
|---|---|---|
| Responding to an inquiry | Reply to someone who just asked for a quote | The inquiry itself, for related responses |
| Informational | Appointment reminders, order updates | Prior express consent, often given by providing the number for that purpose |
| Marketing | Promotions, offers, sales campaigns | Prior express written consent |

When in doubt, treat a message as marketing and get written consent.

## What counts as prior express written consent?

Generally, a clear agreement, signed in writing or electronically, that:

- Identifies the specific phone number.
- Authorizes your business to send marketing messages, including automated ones.
- Makes clear that consent is not a condition of buying anything.
- Is not buried in fine print.

An unchecked checkbox on a web form with clear consent language, plus a record of when and how it was given, is a common approach.

## What should your website form say?

A typical optional consent line looks like:

> I agree to receive text messages from [Business Name] about my inquiry and occasional offers at the number provided. Consent is not a condition of purchase. Message frequency varies. Message and data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help. See our Privacy Policy and Terms.

Keep the checkbox optional and unchecked, link to policies that describe your texting, and store the consent record with the contact in your CRM. These details also help with carrier registration. See [A2P 10DLC registration](/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration/).

## How must you handle opt-outs?

- Honor STOP, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL and similar replies immediately.
- FCC rules say consumers can revoke consent by any reasonable means, and businesses must honor revocations promptly, within a set number of business days.
- Send one confirmation that they are unsubscribed, with no further marketing.
- Sync opt-outs across every system that can text the person.

## What are quiet hours for texting?

Federal telemarketing rules generally prohibit telephone solicitations before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time. Some states are stricter. Schedule marketing texts within business-friendly hours in the recipient's time zone.

## What extra rules apply in Florida?

Florida's Telephone Solicitation Act adds state-level requirements for marketing calls and texts, including tighter calling hours than the federal rule and limits on how often you contact the same person about the same subject in a day. It has been amended in recent years, so check the current version with counsel if you text Florida consumers. Other states have similar laws.

## What texting practices carry the most risk?

- Texting purchased or scraped lists.
- Marketing texts to people who only gave their number for a quote.
- Ignoring or delaying opt-outs.
- Sending outside allowed hours.
- No record of when and how consent was given.

## How do automations stay compliant?

Build compliance into the system:

1. Capture consent with the form submission and store it on the contact.
2. Separate informational workflows, such as reminders, from marketing campaigns.
3. Apply opt-outs everywhere automatically.
4. Schedule campaigns inside allowed hours.
5. Review templates before they go live.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I text a customer who gave me their number?

For messages related to why they gave it, such as their quote or appointment, generally yes. Marketing texts need prior express written consent.

### Does the TCPA apply to small businesses?

Yes. The TCPA applies to businesses of every size, and damages are counted per message.

### Is a checkbox on my website enough for consent?

A clear, optional, unchecked checkbox with proper consent language, linked to your policies and recorded with the contact, is a common approach. Have counsel review your wording.

### What happens if someone texts STOP?

Stop sending them messages promptly, confirm the opt-out once, and make sure every system that could text them respects it.
