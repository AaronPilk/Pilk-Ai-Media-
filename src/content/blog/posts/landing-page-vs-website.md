"Should I send my ads to my website or a landing page?" is one of the most common questions we get. The short version: send paid traffic to a landing page built for that ad. Here is why, and when a full website is the better tool.

## What is a landing page?

A landing page is a standalone page with one job. It speaks to one audience about one offer and asks for one action. It usually has:

- A headline that matches the ad or email that brought the visitor.
- A short explanation of the offer and who it is for.
- Proof: reviews, results, photos of real work.
- One clear call to action, often a short form or booking calendar.
- Few or no links elsewhere, so attention stays on the action.

## What is a website for?

A website is your business's home online. It answers every kind of visitor: the person comparing providers, the existing customer looking for your phone number, the job applicant, the referral partner checking you out. It has many pages, a navigation menu, and content that helps you show up in search.

## How do landing pages and websites compare?

| | Landing page | Website |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | One action | Many goals |
| Audience | One segment | Everyone |
| Navigation | Minimal or none | Full menu |
| Best traffic source | Ads, email, social campaigns | Search, referrals, direct |
| Lifespan | A campaign or offer | Years |
| Measured by | Conversion rate | Leads, calls, rankings, trust |

## Why should ads go to a landing page instead of your homepage?

Your homepage has to serve everyone, so it cannot speak directly to the person who clicked a specific ad. When the ad promises "free roof inspection in Clearwater" and the visitor lands on a general homepage, they have to hunt. Many leave.

A dedicated landing page:

1. **Matches the promise** of the ad, so visitors know they are in the right place.
2. **Removes distractions** that pull people away from the action.
3. **Gives cleaner tracking**, so you know which ads produce leads.
4. **Improves ad performance**, since ad platforms reward pages that convert.

## When is a website page better than a landing page?

Use a regular website page when:

- Visitors are researching rather than responding to an offer.
- You want the page to rank in search over time.
- The audience is broad, such as everyone looking for your service in your city.
- People need to explore several services before deciding.

Search traffic usually lands on website pages, which is why service pages and helpful articles matter. See [do I need a website for my business](/blog/do-i-need-a-website-for-my-business/).

## What makes a landing page convert?

- **One offer, one action.** Do not ask people to call, email, book and download all at once.
- **A short form.** Ask only what you need for the first conversation.
- **Speed.** Most ad clicks are on phones; slow pages lose them.
- **Real proof.** Reviews with names, photos of actual work, specific results.
- **Instant follow-up.** The page is only half the job; the reply wins the customer. See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

## How many landing pages should a business have?

One per distinct offer or audience. A roofer might have one for inspections, one for storm damage and one for financing. A real estate agent might have one for home valuations and one for a specific listing. Start with the offer that brings in the most valuable customers and add more as you run more campaigns.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a landing page replace a website?

For a short campaign or a brand-new business, briefly, yes. Long term, most businesses need a website for credibility, search and the visitors who are not responding to a specific offer.

### Should landing pages be indexed by Google?

Campaign pages built only for ads are often set to noindex to avoid thin duplicate pages. Evergreen offer pages that could rank can stay indexed.

### What is a good landing page conversion rate?

It varies widely by industry, offer and traffic source. Compare against your own past results and improve from there rather than chasing a universal benchmark.

### Is a funnel the same as a landing page?

A funnel is the whole path: ad, landing page, thank-you page and follow-up. The landing page is one step in it.
