Agents have more places to be seen online than ever: brokerage pages, Zillow and Realtor.com profiles, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and Google. So it is fair to ask whether a personal website still matters. For most agents, it does, but not for the reasons many website companies give. Here is the honest case for and against.

## What does a personal website do that other profiles cannot?

**It belongs to you.** Your brokerage profile disappears when you move brokerages. Portal profiles follow the portal's rules and show competitors next to you. Social platforms change their algorithms whenever they like. A website on your own domain is the one place you control completely.

**It captures leads you own.** Leads from portals and brokerage sites often route through someone else's system, sometimes with fees or competing agents attached. Leads from your site go straight to your CRM.

**It makes you look established.** When a referral looks you up, a professional site with your story, results and reviews answers the question "can I trust this person?" faster than any profile.

**It gives your marketing a home.** Listing campaigns, social posts and ads all need somewhere to send people. A single listing page or a home valuation page on your own site converts far better than a link to a portal.

## When might an agent not need a website yet?

- **Brand-new agents** working mostly from a team's leads may be better served by the team's site for the first months.
- **Agents who work purely by referral** from a small circle may get by with a strong Google Business Profile and social presence.
- **Agents on a team with a strong central brand** where the team site is the clear destination.

Even in these cases, owning your name as a domain early costs little and protects your future options.

## What should an agent website include?

You do not need dozens of pages. A focused site works:

1. **Home** that says who you are, where you work and what to do next.
2. **About** with your story, experience, real photos and reviews.
3. **Listings**: your own listings with rich pages, not just a search box.
4. **Home valuation** offer for sellers.
5. **Neighborhood pages** for the areas you know best.
6. **Contact** with a short form and booking link.

## Do agents need IDX home search on their site?

Not necessarily. Buyers can search listings on any portal; your site's job is to make them choose you. IDX helps if you focus on buyers and will follow up on every registration, but those pages rarely rank on their own. See [IDX websites for realtors](/blog/idx-website-for-realtors/).

## How does a website work with social media?

Social media builds attention; your website turns it into conversations. A typical path: a reel about a new listing, a link in bio to that listing's page, a showing request form, an instant text back. Without the website, that interest often goes to a DM that gets buried. With it, you capture the contact and follow up properly. See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

## What makes an agent website actually produce leads?

- A clear local focus in the first screen.
- A seller offer such as a home valuation.
- Real reviews and recent results.
- Fast pages on phones.
- Forms that create a CRM contact and trigger an instant text.
- Regular updates: new listings, sold homes and market notes.

A site that sits untouched for two years with an expired listing on the homepage hurts more than it helps.

## Should you put your website on your own domain?

Yes. Use your name or your team brand, not a brokerage subdomain or a builder's free address. It keeps your brand portable, looks more professional and builds search value that stays with you. See [who owns my website](/blog/who-owns-my-website/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a brokerage website enough for a real estate agent?

It is a start, but it belongs to the brokerage and promotes it first. A personal site builds your own brand and keeps your leads if you switch.

### Can social media replace a real estate website?

Social media is great for attention, but you do not own it and it is weak at capturing leads. A website turns that attention into contacts you control.

### How many pages does a real estate agent website need?

A focused site of six to twelve pages works well: home, about, listings, home valuation, neighborhoods and contact, plus pages for any specialty.

### Will a website get me leads from Google?

It can over time, especially with neighborhood content, reviews and a strong Google Business Profile. Most agents see the biggest early value from listings, social and referrals.
