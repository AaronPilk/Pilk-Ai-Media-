Agents, buyers and sellers all want to know closing costs early, and title insurance is one of the bigger line items. A good calculator on a title company website answers that question in seconds and makes the company easier to recommend. Here is how title premium calculators work, using Florida as an example, and what a good one includes.

## What does a title insurance calculator estimate?

Most calculators estimate:

- **Owner's title insurance**, based on the purchase price.
- **Lender's (loan) title insurance**, based on the loan amount.
- **Simultaneous issue** pricing when both policies are issued together.
- **Endorsements** commonly added to lender's policies.
- Sometimes **other closing fees**, such as settlement and recording charges.

## How are Florida title insurance premiums calculated?

Florida is a promulgated-rate state: the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation sets title insurance premiums, so the premium is the same whichever title company issues the policy. The original rate schedule for owner's policies works in tiers:

| Coverage amount | Rate per thousand dollars |
|---|---|
| Up to $100,000 | $5.75 |
| Over $100,000 to $1 million | $5.00 |
| Over $1 million to $5 million | $2.50 |
| Over $5 million to $10 million | $2.25 |
| Over $10 million | $2.00 |

The minimum premium is $100. Reissue rates, discounts and other rules can lower the premium in some situations. Check the current rule, Florida Administrative Code 69O-186.003, for any changes.

## What is a worked example?

For a $400,000 purchase in Florida, the original owner's premium is:

- First $100,000 at $5.75 per thousand: $575.
- Remaining $300,000 at $5.00 per thousand: $1,500.
- **Total owner's premium: $2,075.**

If a lender's policy is issued at the same time, Florida's simultaneous issue rate applies to the loan policy, commonly a small flat charge for the portion of the loan up to the owner's coverage, with additional premium if the loan policy exceeds it. Endorsements add their own charges.

## Who pays for title insurance in Florida?

It depends on the county and the contract. In many Florida counties the seller customarily pays for the owner's policy, while in some, including several in South Florida, the buyer customarily pays. The purchase contract decides. The buyer usually pays for the lender's policy.

## What should a title company calculator include?

- **Inputs**: purchase price, loan amount, county, and whether it is a purchase or refinance.
- **Clear results**: owner's premium, lender's premium, simultaneous issue and common endorsements, shown separately.
- **Optional fees**: settlement, search and recording estimates, labeled as estimates.
- **A disclaimer**: results are estimates, not a quote or commitment; final figures come from the title company.
- **A next step**: "Open an order" or "Get a full fee sheet."
- **Fast, mobile-friendly design**, since agents use it on their phones.

## What about states without promulgated rates?

In states where title companies set their own rates, calculators must use each company's filed rates and update when those change. The structure is similar; the numbers differ. Always label results as estimates.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are title insurance rates the same at every Florida title company?

The title insurance premium is set by the state, so it is the same. Other closing fees, such as settlement and search fees, can vary by company.

### How much is title insurance on a $300,000 home in Florida?

Using the original owner's rate, about $1,575: $575 for the first $100,000 and $5.00 per thousand on the remaining $200,000, before any reissue discounts or other charges.

### What is a simultaneous issue rate?

A reduced charge for the lender's policy when it is issued at the same time as the owner's policy on the same property.

### Is a title insurance calculator a quote?

No. It is an estimate. The title company provides final figures based on the transaction details.
