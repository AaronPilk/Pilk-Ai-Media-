"What time works for you?" can turn into six messages and a lost lead. Automated booking replaces that with a link: the customer picks a time that is actually open, and the system handles confirmations and reminders. Here is how to set it up well for a service business.

## How does automated appointment booking work?

1. You set your availability, appointment types, durations and rules.
2. The booking calendar syncs with your real calendar so you are never double-booked.
3. Customers book from your website, a text, an email or an ad.
4. The system creates the appointment and the contact in your CRM.
5. Confirmations and reminders go out automatically.
6. Customers can reschedule or cancel from a link, within your rules.

## What should a booking setup include?

- **Appointment types**: consultation, estimate, follow-up, each with its own length.
- **Availability rules**: working hours, days off, buffers between appointments, minimum notice and how far ahead people can book.
- **Locations**: in person, at the customer's address, phone or video, with the right details on each.
- **Intake questions**: two or three questions so you arrive prepared.
- **Team routing**: round robin or assignment by service or area.
- **Calendar sync** with Google or Outlook.

## How do reminders reduce no-shows?

Reminders are where much of the value lies. A common sequence:

| When | Message |
|---|---|
| Right after booking | Confirmation with date, time, address and reschedule link |
| One day before | Reminder by text and email, with a confirm reply option |
| One or two hours before | Short text reminder |
| After the appointment | Thank-you and next step, or review request |

Text reminders tend to work best because people read texts quickly. They need proper consent and carrier registration. See [TCPA text message consent](/blog/tcpa-text-message-consent/) and [A2P 10DLC registration](/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration/).

## Where should booking links appear?

- **Your website**: service pages, contact page and after form submissions.
- **Instant replies to new leads**: "Book a time that works for you." See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

- **Ads and landing pages** for consultation offers.
- **Google Business Profile**, where a booking link is supported.

## Should you take deposits for bookings?

For businesses with costly no-shows, such as long appointments or limited slots, a deposit or card on file can help. It adds friction, so test it. Many service businesses do better with strong reminders and an easy reschedule option than with deposits.

## What mistakes make booking systems fail?

- Availability that does not match reality, so people book times you cannot keep.
- No buffer time for travel between jobs.
- Too many intake questions before booking.
- Reminders sent from a number customers do not recognize.
- No easy way to reschedule, so people simply do not show up.
- Bookings that never reach the CRM, so follow-up breaks.

## How do you measure whether booking automation is working?

Track booked appointments per week, the share of leads who book without a phone call, no-show rate before and after reminders, and time spent scheduling. Most businesses see the change quickly in fewer scheduling messages and fewer empty slots.

## What does a good booking flow look like from the customer's side?

A homeowner clicks "Book an estimate" on a service page, sees the next available times in their area, picks one, answers two quick questions about the job and enters their name and phone number. Within seconds they get a text confirming the time and address, with a link to reschedule. The day before, a reminder arrives and they reply "C" to confirm. An hour before, a final text says the technician is on the way. The business never typed a single message, and the customer never waited on hold. That is the experience to design for, and it is worth walking through yourself on a phone before launch.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does automated booking work for businesses that travel to customers?

Yes. Set buffer times for travel, limit bookings by service area if needed, and collect the address during booking.

### How many reminders should I send?

A confirmation, a reminder the day before and a short reminder an hour or two before works for most businesses.

### Will customers book online instead of calling?

Many prefer it, especially outside business hours. Keep a phone option for people who want to talk.

### Can automated booking sync with my Google Calendar?

Most booking tools and CRM calendars sync with Google and Outlook calendars to prevent double-booking.
