Building the app is only part of the job. Getting it through Apple's and Google's review processes trips up many first-time app owners: rejected builds, missing privacy details, unclear login instructions. Here is the full path from finished app to live listing, and how to avoid the most common delays.

## What accounts do you need to publish an app?

- **Apple Developer Program**: $99 a year. Enroll as an organization if the app belongs to your business; that requires a D-U-N-S number for the company. Individual accounts publish under a personal name.
- **Google Play Console**: a one-time $25 registration fee. Organizations verify their business details; individual accounts verify identity.

Put both accounts in the business's name and control. If a developer publishes the app under their own account, moving it later is possible but slow.

## What do you need to prepare before submitting?

1. **App name, subtitle and description** that explain clearly what the app does.
2. **Screenshots** for the required device sizes, and optionally a preview video.
3. **App icon** that meets each store's specifications.
4. **Privacy policy URL** that matches what the app actually collects.
5. **Privacy details**: Apple's App Privacy labels and Google's Data safety form, describing data collection and use.
6. **Support URL and contact details.**
7. **Age rating questionnaire.**
8. **Demo login for reviewers**, if the app requires an account.

## How does Apple's App Store review work?

You upload a build, usually after testing it through TestFlight, Apple's beta testing service. Then you submit it for review. Apple checks the app against its App Store Review Guidelines: stability, accurate descriptions, privacy, payments, content and design. Apple says most submissions are reviewed within a day or two, but first submissions and rejections can add time.

Common reasons for rejection:

- Crashes or broken features during review.
- Missing reviewer login details.
- Apps that are mostly a website in a wrapper with little app-like value.
- Digital goods sold without Apple's in-app purchase system where Apple requires it.
- Account creation without an in-app way to delete the account.
- Privacy details that do not match the app's behavior.

## How does Google Play publishing work?

You create the app in Play Console, upload a build, complete the store listing, content rating and Data safety form, and roll it out to testing tracks before production. Google's rules require new personal developer accounts to run a closed test with a minimum number of testers for a set period before applying for production access; check the current requirement in Play Console when you start. Organization accounts have fewer hurdles. Google's review is usually quick, but new accounts can take longer.

## How long does it take to get an app live?

Plan for:

- **Account setup**: days, longer if you need a D-U-N-S number for an organization account.
- **Beta testing**: one to several weeks, depending on how many issues testers find.
- **Review**: often a day or two per store, longer on first submission or after a rejection.
- **Google's closed testing period** for new personal accounts.

Build these into your launch date. Do not announce a launch day before the app is approved.

## What does it cost to keep an app in the stores?

Beyond the developer accounts, budget for updates. Apple and Google release new operating system versions every year, and apps need updates to keep working and to meet new store requirements. ## Should your business build an app at all?

Many businesses that want an app need a fast mobile website instead. An app makes sense when people will use it often, need notifications or device features, or expect it as part of your product. See [app vs website](/blog/app-vs-website/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to put an app on the App Store?

Apple's Developer Program costs $99 a year. Google Play charges a one-time $25 fee. Building the app is the main cost.

### Can I publish an app under my business name?

Yes, with an organization developer account. Apple requires a D-U-N-S number for organizations; Google requires business verification.

### Why did Apple reject my app?

Common reasons are crashes, missing reviewer login details, incomplete privacy information, too little app functionality, or payment rules. Apple's rejection message names the guideline involved.

### Do I need a Mac to publish an iPhone app?

Building and uploading iPhone apps normally uses Apple's Xcode, which runs on macOS. Many developers use a Mac or a cloud build service.
