Most title business comes through referrals from real estate agents and lenders, so a title company's website has a different job from most small business sites. It has to make referral partners look good to their clients, make ordering easy and reassure buyers and sellers who have never heard of you. We have designed and built websites for several title companies and affiliated title agencies. Here is what the good ones have in common.

## Who visits a title company website, and what do they need?

Four groups, each with a different reason:

- **Real estate agents** want to open an order fast, find closing locations and send their clients somewhere that makes them look professional.
- **Lenders and processors** want contact details, how to send documents and confidence that you close on time.
- **Buyers and sellers** want to know what title insurance is, what happens at closing, what they need to bring and how to wire funds safely.
- **Attorneys and other title agents** want to know whether you are a credible partner.

A good site gives each group its own clear path from the homepage.

## What pages should a title company website have?

The core set we build for title clients:

1. **Homepage** with an "Open an order" button and your service area in the first screen.
2. **Order title online**: a form that collects the property, parties, contract and lender details and sends them to your team.
3. **Rate or quote calculator** where your state's rates allow it. In states where title premiums are set by the state, a calculator can be accurate; elsewhere it should show estimates clearly labelled.
4. **For agents, for lenders, for buyers and sellers**: one page each, written for that audience.
5. **The closing process**, step by step, with what to bring and how long it takes.
6. **Wire fraud warning** page, linked from every page and from closing emails. We cover the content in [real estate wire fraud](/blog/real-estate-wire-fraud/).
7. **Locations and mobile closings**, with the areas you serve.
8. **Team page** with real photos and direct contact details.
9. **Resources**: title insurance explained, glossary, FAQs.
10. **Legal pages and required disclosures.**

## What do affiliated title agencies need on their website?

Many title agencies are joint ventures with real estate brokerages, builders or lenders. If a referring business has an ownership interest in the title agency, RESPA's rules on affiliated business arrangements generally require a written disclosure to the consumer at or before the referral, and the consumer must be free to choose another provider. Your compliance contact will handle the formal disclosure form, but the website should be consistent with it: clear ownership disclosure language where appropriate, and no wording that implies a client must use you.

We explain the rules in [affiliated business arrangement disclosures](/blog/affiliated-business-arrangement-disclosure/). Have your attorney or underwriter review the site before launch.

## How should a title company website handle wire fraud?

Wire fraud targeting real estate closings is one of the biggest risks in the business, and the website is part of the defense. Include:

- A plain warning that your company will never change wiring instructions by email.
- Instructions to confirm wiring details by calling a known phone number, not one in an email.
- What to do immediately if someone suspects fraud: call their bank and your office, and report it to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/).
- A link to this page in your email signatures and closing documents.

## How does a title company website win more agent referrals?

Agents refer title companies that make their lives easier and make them look good. Your website can help with both:

- **Fast online ordering** that saves the agent a phone call.
- **Client-friendly guides** agents can send to buyers and sellers, branded to you.
- **Co-branded resources** where your underwriter and compliance rules allow.
- **Clear contact details** for your closers and processors, so agents know who to call.
- **Reviews from agents**, not just consumers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a title company website have a quote calculator?

If your state's rate structure allows a reliable estimate, yes. It is one of the most used features on title sites. Where rates vary by underwriter or negotiation, show a clearly labelled estimate and invite users to request an exact quote.

### Can clients open a title order online?

Yes, with a secure form that collects property, party, contract and lender details and routes them to your team. Avoid collecting Social Security numbers or bank details on a basic website form.

### What makes a title company website trustworthy?

Real team photos, clear office locations, a visible wire fraud warning, reviews from agents and consumers, and accurate licensing and underwriter information.

### Do title companies need a blog?

A resource section with guides for buyers, sellers and agents helps with search and gives agents something to send their clients. It does not need to be frequent; it needs to be accurate and useful.
