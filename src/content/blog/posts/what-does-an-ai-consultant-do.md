"AI consultant" covers everyone from researchers advising large companies to someone who just discovered ChatGPT last month. For a small business, the useful version is practical: someone who understands both your work and the tools, and who ends with something working rather than a slide deck. This guide explains what that looks like, what you should get, and how to tell whether you need one.

## What does an AI consultant actually do day to day?

For a small business, the work usually follows four steps:

1. **Learn how the business runs.** How leads arrive, how follow-up happens, where documents and customer data live, which tasks eat the week, and which software you already pay for.
2. **Find the opportunities.** Tasks that are repetitive, rules-based, or slow because someone copies information from one place to another. Typical ones: answering the same questions, writing first drafts, summarising calls, sorting inbound leads and following up.
3. **Say what AI can and cannot do there right now**, in plain language, including the costs to run it each month and the privacy questions it raises.
4. **Build it or hand it off.** Set up the automation, chatbot, AI features in existing tools or a custom app, then train the team.

That is how our own [AI consulting](/services/ai-consulting/) works: an intro call, a review of your workflows and tools, a short written plan and then the build if you want it.

## What should you get at the end of an AI consulting engagement?

If you finish with only a presentation about "the future of AI," you overpaid. You should get:

- **A prioritised list** of where AI helps your business and where it does not.
- **Specific tools** for each idea, with what they cost to run every month.
- **Effort and risk** for each, including what data the tool can see.
- **A build plan for the first project**, small enough to finish in weeks, not months.
- **Something working**, if you hired them to build it.

## Which tasks do AI consultants usually automate first?

For service businesses, the first wins are almost always in lead handling and repetitive writing:

- **Instant replies to new leads** by text and email, so nobody waits hours. We explain why in [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).
- **A website or messaging chatbot** that answers common questions and books calls. See [what an AI chatbot costs](/blog/how-much-does-an-ai-chatbot-cost/).
- **Call and meeting summaries** that land in the CRM automatically.
- **First drafts** of emails, proposals, listing descriptions and social posts, edited by a person before sending.
- **Data entry between apps**, like copying form leads into a CRM and spreadsheet.

Big, risky projects like replacing staff or automating decisions that affect customers' money or legal rights should come later, if at all, and with a person reviewing the output.

## How do you spot a bad AI consultant?

Warning signs we would watch for:

1. **They lead with a tool, not your problem.** If every answer is the same platform they resell, be careful.
2. **They promise to replace your team** or guarantee specific savings before seeing how you work.
3. **They never mention costs to run.** AI tools charge monthly or per use; a plan without running costs is incomplete.
4. **They ignore data privacy.** Customer information, financial data and anything regulated need care about which tools can see them.
5. **They cannot show anything they have built.**

## When do you not need an AI consultant?

You may not need one if:

- You only want to use an AI assistant for writing and research. A few hours of practice and a simple company policy are enough. Our guide to an [AI policy for employees](/blog/ai-policy-for-employees/) helps with that part.
- The software you already pay for has AI features you have not turned on yet.
- Your bigger problem is that leads are not coming in at all. That is a marketing problem, not an AI one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to be technical to work with an AI consultant?

No. You need to know your business. A good consultant handles the technical side and explains every recommendation in plain language.

### Will an AI consultant replace my employees?

That should not be the goal. The best small business results come from removing repetitive work so your team spends more time with customers. Be wary of anyone promising headcount cuts before seeing how you work.

### How long does an AI consulting project take?

A review and written plan for a small business usually takes a few weeks at most. The first build should be small enough to finish in weeks, so you see results before committing to more.

### Is it safe to put customer data into AI tools?

It depends on the tool, its settings and its data terms. Business plans of major AI tools usually offer stronger privacy controls than free versions. A consultant should note what data each tool can access before you use it.
