Portals like Zillow and Realtor.com show your listing next to dozens of others, with ads for other agents beside it. A single property website does the opposite: one home, your branding, your contact details and nothing competing for attention. Here is what goes into a good one, what it costs and how to get the most out of it.

## What is a single property website?

It is a web page dedicated to one listing. It might live on its own domain (like 123OakStreet.com), on a subdomain, or as a page on your agent website. It typically includes:

- A large hero photo or video of the home.
- A full photo gallery.
- Price, beds, baths, square footage, lot size and key features.
- A written description and highlights.
- Neighborhood information: schools, parks, commute, nearby shops.
- Open house dates.
- A contact or showing request form that goes straight to you.

The page works as a home for all your marketing on that listing: the link in your social posts, the QR code on the yard sign and flyers, and the destination for any ads.

## Why do agents use single property websites?

Three reasons come up most:

1. **Winning the listing.** Showing a seller that their home gets its own professional website is a strong part of a listing presentation. It is something they can share with friends and family, and it signals effort.
2. **Capturing buyer leads directly.** Inquiries go to you instead of a portal that may route them to another agent.
3. **Better marketing results.** Ads and social posts that point to a focused page tend to perform better than links to a generic search page, because there is one clear thing to do.

## What should a good single property website include?

Beyond the basics above, the details that make a difference:

- **Fast loading on phones.** Most people open these links from a social post or a QR code on their phone.
- **Real, high-quality photos and video**, displayed large. Compressed but sharp.
- **One clear action**: request a showing, ask a question or register for the open house.
- **Your branding and contact details**, without burying the home.
- **Fair-housing-compliant wording.** Describe the property, not the kind of buyer you want. HUD's [Fair Housing Act guidance](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_act_overview) is the reference point.
- **Correct listing details** that match the MLS, including price changes and status.
- **A plan for when it sells**: a "Sold" banner and a prompt for similar homes or a home valuation, instead of a dead page.

## Do single property websites help with SEO?

A little, mostly for searches of the exact address. Buyers sometimes search the street address after seeing a sign, and a dedicated page can show up for that. But a listing page is short-lived, so it is not where your long-term search traffic comes from.

Neighborhood guides, market updates and service pages build search visibility over time. Use single property sites for marketing and lead capture, and your main site for search. We cover that side in our [real estate SEO](/blog/real-estate-seo/) guide.

## How do you get leads from a single property website?

Put the page in front of people, then make responding easy:

1. **Link it everywhere**: MLS virtual tour field where your MLS allows it, social posts, email to your list, flyers and the yard sign via QR code.
2. **Run a small ad** to people in the area. Real estate ads on Facebook and Instagram run under Meta's housing category with targeting limits, which we explain in [Facebook ads for real estate agents](/blog/facebook-ads-for-real-estate-agents/).
3. **Answer fast.** Showing requests go cold quickly. An instant text reply that confirms the request and offers times keeps the buyer with you.
4. **Follow up after the sale** with similar homes, so a buyer who missed this one stays with you.

## When is a single property website not worth it?

Skip it when the home will likely sell in a weekend in a hot market with no marketing push, or when the seller does not want extra online exposure. In those cases, the MLS and your brokerage site may be all you need.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a separate domain for each listing?

No. A separate domain looks polished on signs and flyers, but a page on your own agent site works well and builds your main site instead. Some agents use a short domain for high-end listings and pages on their site for the rest.

### What happens to the page after the home sells?

Mark it sold, keep the photos as proof of your work if your MLS rules and the seller allow it, and add a link to similar listings or a home valuation page so visitors still have somewhere useful to go.

### Can I use MLS photos on a single property website?

Usually for marketing the listing while it is active, but photo rights depend on your photographer's agreement and your MLS rules. Check both before reusing photos after the sale.
