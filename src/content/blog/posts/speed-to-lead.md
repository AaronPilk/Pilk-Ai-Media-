You can spend thousands on a website and ads and still lose the customer in the hour after they fill out your form. Speed to lead is the simplest fix in marketing and one of the most ignored. Here is what the research says, where small businesses lose time, and how to get your response time down to minutes without hiring someone to watch the inbox.

## What does speed to lead mean?

It is the time between a person reaching out, by form, call, text, chat or message, and your business responding in a useful way. "Useful" matters: an automatic "we got your message" email helps a little; a text that confirms what they asked for and offers a time to talk helps a lot.

## What does the research say about lead response time?

The most cited study is "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads," published in [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) in 2011 by James Oldroyd, Kristina McElheran and David Elkington. They found that firms that tried to contact leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited even an hour longer, and more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited 24 hours or more.

The same research audited how quickly real companies responded to web inquiries. Only about a third responded within an hour, and nearly a quarter never responded at all.

The study is over a decade old and was about larger companies, so treat the exact multipliers as directional. The pattern matches what we see with small businesses today: the first helpful reply usually gets the conversation.

## Why do small businesses respond to leads so slowly?

It is rarely laziness. The common causes are structural:

- **Leads land in different places**: a form inbox, a Facebook message, a voicemail, a text to someone's personal phone.
- **Nobody owns the first reply.** Everyone assumes someone else saw it.
- **The owner is busy doing the work**, on a job site or with a client, and checks messages at night.
- **After-hours leads wait until morning**, when the person has already called someone else.
- **Forms send to an old or wrong email address.** We find this more often than you would think.

## How do you respond to leads within minutes?

The goal is an instant, helpful first touch, followed by a fast human conversation.

1. **Put every lead in one place.** A CRM that collects forms, calls, texts and social messages into one inbox.
2. **Send an instant text and email.** Confirm what they asked for, say who will contact them and when, and offer a booking link.
3. **Alert the right person immediately**, by text or app notification, not just email.
4. **Text back missed calls automatically**, so a caller who reached voicemail gets a reply in seconds. See [missed call text back](/blog/missed-call-text-back/).
5. **Follow up if they do not answer**: a second text later that day, a call the next morning, then a short sequence over the following weeks.
6. **Track response time** so you can see when it slips.

## Should the first response be automated or human?

Both, in sequence. Automation wins on speed and never sleeps. Humans win on judgment and trust. The best setup sends an automatic text within a minute and gets a person on the phone as soon as possible after that.

Keep automated messages honest and short. People can tell when a message is automated; they mind only when it pretends not to be or when it is irrelevant.

## Do you need consent to text new leads automatically?

You need to follow texting rules. A person who submits your form and asks to be contacted has started the conversation, but automated marketing texts require clear consent, and US business texting now runs through carrier registration known as A2P 10DLC. We cover both in [TCPA text message consent](/blog/tcpa-text-message-consent/) and [A2P 10DLC registration](/blog/a2p-10dlc-registration/). Include a consent line on your forms and an easy way to opt out of texts.

## How fast should you respond to a new lead?

As fast as you can make it. A practical target for small businesses:

- **Automated text and email**: within one minute, every time, day or night.
- **Human contact**: within 5 to 15 minutes during business hours.
- **After hours**: automated reply plus a booking link, and a call first thing the next morning.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good lead response time?

Within minutes. An automatic, useful reply in under a minute and a human follow-up within 5 to 15 minutes during business hours puts you ahead of most competitors.

### Does speed to lead matter for every industry?

It matters most when people contact several businesses at once: home services, real estate, mortgage, legal, healthcare and most local services. It matters less for slow, considered purchases, though a prompt reply still builds trust.

### Is the "respond in five minutes" statistic real?

The often-quoted five-minute figures come from separate research and are repeated loosely online. The Harvard Business Review study supports the broader point: responding within the hour is far better than waiting, and faster is better still.

### How can a one-person business respond to leads quickly?

Automate the first reply by text and email with a booking link, send yourself instant alerts, and text back missed calls. That covers most leads even when you are with a customer.
