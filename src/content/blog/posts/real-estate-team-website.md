A team website has two audiences: clients choosing who to work with, and agents deciding whether to join you. Most team sites serve the first poorly and ignore the second. Here is what a real estate team website needs to win both.

## How is a team website different from an agent website?

An individual agent site centers on one person. A team site has to:

- **Present one brand** while introducing several agents.
- **Route leads** to the right agent quickly.
- **Show combined results** honestly.
- **Recruit** new agents.
- **Stay consistent** across many people's content.

## What pages does a real estate team website need?

- **Home**: the team's promise, area, results and main next step.
- **About the team**: your story, how you work, and why clients choose a team.
- **Agent profiles**: one page per agent with photo, bio, specialties, areas, reviews and direct contact.
- **Listings**: rich pages for each listing, credited to the listing agent.
- **Home valuation**: the main seller offer. See [home valuation landing pages](/blog/home-valuation-landing-page/).
- **Neighborhood guides** for areas the team covers.
- **Buyer and seller guides.**
- **Reviews**: team-wide, with names of the agents involved.
- **Join the team**: culture, support, training, compensation approach in general terms, and how to apply.
- **Contact**, with brokerage and license details.

See [real estate website pages](/blog/real-estate-website-pages/) for what goes on each.

## How should leads be routed on a team site?

Decide the rules before launch:

- **By agent**: leads from an agent's profile go to that agent.
- **By area or specialty**: luxury, condos or a particular neighborhood go to the specialist.
- **Round robin**: general leads rotate among available agents.
- **Response rules**: if the assigned agent does not respond within a set time, the lead moves to the next.

A CRM handles this automatically, with instant texts to the lead and alerts to the agent. See [GoHighLevel for real estate](/blog/gohighlevel-for-real-estate/) and [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

## How should a team website help with recruiting?

Agents research teams online before taking a call. A good join-the-team page shows:

- What a week on the team looks like.
- Lead sources the team provides.
- Training, coaching and support.
- Technology and marketing provided.
- Stories from current agents.
- A simple application form.

## How do you keep a team site consistent?

- Use one design system for every agent profile.
- Write bios in a shared format with consistent photo styles.
- Keep listing and review information current.
- Assign one person to approve content changes.

## What compliance details apply to team websites?

This is general information:

- Show the brokerage name and license details as your state requires.
- Follow your state's rules on team names, which often must not imply the team is its own brokerage.
- Display equal housing language.
- Follow fair housing rules in every description and ad.
- Present team production honestly, without implying individual agents closed team totals.

Check your state's rules and brokerage policy before launch.

## What should a team site avoid?

- Agent profiles that are just a photo and phone number.
- Generic stock content that could belong to any team.
- Leads that go to a shared inbox nobody owns.
- Out-of-date agent pages for people who left.
- Slow pages overloaded with widgets.

## How do you measure a team website?

Track leads by source and by agent, response times, appointments set, closed deals from website leads and recruiting applications. If one agent's profile drives far more leads, learn what makes it work and apply it to others.

## How should team agents use the website day to day?

Each agent should share their own profile link in their email signature, social bios and text follow-ups, so referrals land on a page that shows both the agent and the team behind them. When listings go live, agents should share the listing page from the team site rather than a portal link, keeping leads on your site and in your CRM.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should each agent on a team have their own page?

Yes. Individual profiles build trust, help with referrals and let leads go straight to the right agent.

### Can team members also have personal websites?

Often, depending on team and brokerage policy. Many teams provide agent pages on the team site instead, to keep branding consistent.

### How do teams split website leads?

Common methods are by agent page, by area or specialty, and round robin, with rules that reassign leads if nobody responds quickly.

### Do team websites need brokerage information?

Yes. Display the brokerage name and license details as your state and brokerage require.
