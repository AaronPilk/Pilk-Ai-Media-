"How long until we rank?" is the most common SEO question, and the honest answer is: it depends, but it is measured in months, not days. Anyone promising first-page rankings in two weeks is either targeting searches nobody makes or taking shortcuts that can backfire. Here is a realistic timeline and what drives it.

## What does a realistic SEO timeline look like?

| Timeframe | What typically happens |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1 to 4 | Technical fixes, Search Console setup, pages crawled and indexed, Business Profile improved |
| Months 1 to 3 | Impressions grow, long-tail and local searches start ranking, map visibility improves |
| Months 3 to 6 | Rankings rise for main service and location searches, clicks and leads increase |
| Months 6 to 12 | Competitive terms improve, content and reviews compound, steady lead flow |
| Ongoing | Maintaining, expanding content, earning links and reviews |

These are typical patterns, not guarantees. Some businesses see faster results; competitive markets take longer.

## What makes SEO faster?

- **Low competition**: a niche service or a smaller town.
- **A technically sound site**: fast, crawlable, with a sitemap and clean structure.
- **Clear, specific pages** for each service and area.
- **A strong Google Business Profile** with steady reviews.
- **An established domain** with some history and existing links.
- **Fixing obvious problems**, such as pages accidentally set to noindex.

## What makes SEO slower?

- **Competitive markets** like legal, mortgage and real estate in big metros.
- **A brand-new domain** with no history.
- **Thin or duplicate content.**
- **Technical problems**: slow pages, broken redirects after a redesign, blocked crawling.
- **Inconsistent business details** across listings.
- **Stopping and starting** the work.

## Why does local SEO often move faster?

Map results depend heavily on your Google Business Profile, reviews and proximity. Fixing categories, services, photos and review flow can improve local visibility within weeks, faster than organic rankings for competitive terms. ## How do you know SEO is working before leads arrive?

Watch leading indicators in Google Search Console:

1. **Pages indexed** rising to match the pages you want in search.
2. **Impressions** growing: Google showing your pages more often.
3. **Average position** improving for your target searches.
4. **Clicks** following impressions.
5. **Leads from organic search** in your analytics and CRM.

See [Google Search Console for beginners](/blog/google-search-console-for-beginners/).

## Can you speed up indexing of new pages?

Yes, somewhat. Submit your sitemap in Search Console, request indexing for important new pages, link to them from existing pages, and use IndexNow for Bing and other engines that support it. Indexing is not ranking, but pages cannot rank until they are indexed. We do this for every page we publish.

## Does AI search change SEO timelines?

The same foundations apply. Google says no special optimization is needed for AI Overviews, and pages must be indexed to be cited. Clear, specific content and consistent business information help in both regular and AI search. See [how to show up in Google AI Overviews](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-google-ai-overviews/).

## Should you run ads while SEO builds?

Often yes. Ads bring leads immediately while SEO compounds. As organic traffic grows, many businesses shift budget. See [how much Facebook ads cost](/blog/how-much-do-facebook-ads-cost-small-business/).

## What should you avoid when SEO feels slow?

- Buying links or using link schemes.
- Publishing dozens of thin pages at once.
- Changing page addresses without redirects.
- Switching strategy every month.

Patience with steady, quality work beats shortcuts.

## What does a good first 90 days of SEO look like?

Month one: fix technical problems, set up Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit sitemaps, complete your Google Business Profile and start asking every customer for a review. Month two: improve your main service pages with clear answers, real photos and FAQs, and add pages for important services or areas you are missing. Month three: publish helpful answers to the questions customers ask most, build a few local links through partners and associations, and review which searches are starting to show impressions. By then you should see clear movement in Search Console, even if leads are still building.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can SEO work in 30 days?

Technical fixes and Business Profile improvements can show results within weeks, especially for local and very specific searches. Competitive rankings take longer.

### Is SEO worth it for a small business?

Usually yes, because organic leads have no cost per click and the work compounds. It works best alongside a strong website and fast follow-up.

### Why are my pages not showing on Google?

They may not be indexed yet, may be blocked by mistake, or may not answer searches well. Check the page in Search Console's URL inspection.

### How often should I add content for SEO?

Consistently, at a pace you can keep with quality. A few genuinely helpful pages a month beats many thin ones.
