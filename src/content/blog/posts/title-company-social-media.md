Title companies rarely get customers directly from social media. But agents, lenders and buyers check you out there, and agents share helpful posts with their clients. Done well, social media keeps you top of mind with referral partners and reassures buyers before closing. Here is a practical plan.

## Why should a title company be on social media?

- **Agents and lenders notice consistency.** A company that shows up helpfully every week stays in their minds.
- **Buyers look you up** before closing, especially after an agent recommends you.
- **Education reduces friction**: buyers who understand the process close more smoothly.
- **Fraud warnings protect clients**, and show you take security seriously.

## Which platforms should a title company use?

- **Facebook**: reaches agents, local groups and older buyers and sellers.
- **Instagram**: closing celebrations, team culture and short video.
- **LinkedIn**: lenders, attorneys, brokerage leaders and commercial partners.
- **YouTube**: longer explainers that answer common closing questions and can rank in search.

Start with two platforms done well rather than four done poorly.

## What should a title company post?

| Post type | Example |
|---|---|
| Team | "Meet our closer, Dana: 12 years, 3,000 closings" |
| Celebrations | Closing-day photo with the buyers' permission |
| Education | "What is owner's title insurance?" in 45 seconds |
| Fraud warnings | "We will never change wiring instructions by email" |
| Agent tips | "Three title issues that delay closings and how to avoid them" |
| Local news | Market updates and community events |
| Behind the scenes | How a title search works |

A practical rhythm is three to four posts a week, plus stories. See [how often a business should post on social media](/blog/how-often-should-a-business-post-on-social-media/).

## How should title companies use video?

Short videos answer the questions buyers ask most: what happens at closing, what to bring, how long it takes, why title insurance matters. Film your own team in your office, keep each video under a minute for short-form platforms, add captions and end with one next step. Longer versions work on YouTube and your website. See [how to repurpose content across platforms](/blog/repurpose-content-across-platforms/).

## What compliance cautions apply to title company social media?

This is general information, not legal advice:

- **RESPA Section 8**: do not offer or give anything of value for referrals, including paid promotion of an agent's listings in exchange for business. Co-marketing must reflect each party's fair share of costs.
- **Privacy**: get written permission before posting clients' photos, names or property details, and never show documents with personal information.
- **Affiliated business arrangements**: joint venture title agencies should be careful about how partners are promoted. See [affiliated business arrangement disclosures](/blog/affiliated-business-arrangement-disclosure/).
- **Accuracy**: state-specific rules, such as who pays for title insurance, vary by county and contract.

## How can social media support agents?

- Create shareable educational posts agents can send to clients.
- Spotlight agents' closings with permission, without tying it to referrals.
- Announce agent education sessions.
- Respond quickly to agent comments and messages.

## How do you keep a title company's social media consistent?

Plan monthly, film several videos in one session, schedule posts ahead and check messages daily. Assign one person to own it, with a simple approval step for anything involving clients. See [social media automation tools](/blog/social-media-automation-tools/).

## How do you measure whether it is working?

Track follower growth among local agents, engagement on educational posts, messages from agents and clients, shares, and visits to your website from social. Ask new agent partners how they heard of you.

## What does a simple month of title company posts look like?

Week one: meet-the-team post and a short video on what to bring to closing. Week two: wire fraud reminder and a closing celebration with permission. Week three: an agent tip on avoiding title delays and a local market note. Week four: behind the scenes of a title search and an announcement for your next agent education session. Batch the filming in one afternoon and schedule everything ahead.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do title companies need social media?

Not for direct leads, but it builds trust with agents, lenders and buyers who look you up before closing.

### Can title companies post client closing photos?

With written permission, yes. Never show documents or personal information, and respect clients who prefer privacy.

### What is the best platform for a title company?

Facebook and Instagram for agents and buyers, and LinkedIn for lenders and commercial partners.

### Can a title company boost an agent's listing on social media?

Be careful. Paying to promote an agent's listings in connection with referrals can raise RESPA concerns. Check with your attorney.
