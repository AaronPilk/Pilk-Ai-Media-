Trigger terms catch more mortgage marketers than any other advertising rule. A social post that says "30-year fixed, payments from $1,800!" looks harmless, but it can require several extra disclosures. This guide explains what triggers them, what does not, and how to rewrite common phrases. It is general information, not legal advice; your compliance team has the final word.

## Where do trigger term rules come from?

They come from the Truth in Lending Act and its implementing rule, Regulation Z, section 1026.24, which covers advertising for closed-end credit, including mortgages. The CFPB's official interpretations of that section explain how the rules apply, with examples. Regulators treat websites, social posts, emails, videos and texts as advertising, so the rules apply everywhere.

## What are the trigger terms under Regulation Z?

Regulation Z lists four:

1. **The amount or percentage of any down payment.**
2. **The number of payments or period of repayment.**
3. **The amount of any payment.**
4. **The amount of any finance charge.**

The official commentary limits the down payment trigger to credit sale transactions, where the seller provides the financing, so for most loan ads the practical triggers are numbers two to four. Negative references count too: "no payments for 90 days" can still mention a payment term.

## What disclosures do trigger terms require?

If an ad uses a trigger term, it must also clearly disclose:

- **The amount or percentage of the down payment.**
- **The terms of repayment**, reflecting the repayment obligations over the full term of the loan, including any balloon payment.
- **The annual percentage rate**, using that term, and if the rate may increase after closing, that fact.

For loans secured by a home, there are added rules: if an ad states payments, it must show the payments that apply over the full term and say when they do not include taxes and insurance, with equal prominence and close to the advertised payment.

## Which phrases trigger disclosures?

| Phrase | Why it triggers |
|---|---|
| "30-year fixed mortgage" | States a repayment period |
| "Pay it off in 15 years" | States a repayment period |
| "Payments as low as $1,450 a month" | States a payment amount |
| "360 easy payments" | States the number of payments |
| "Total finance charges of just $2,400" | States a finance charge amount |

## Which phrases do not trigger disclosures?

General statements that do not name a specific term usually do not trigger extra disclosures, for example:

- "Low monthly payments."
- "Flexible terms to fit your budget."
- "Pay weekly" or "monthly payment terms arranged."
- "Low down payment options available."
- "Talk to us about today's rates."

Watch rates separately: if an ad states a rate, it must state it as an APR, and the simple rate cannot be more prominent than the APR.

## How do you rewrite common ads to stay compliant?

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| "30-year fixed at 6.25%!" | "Fixed-rate loans available. Ask for today's APR." |
| "Payments from $1,800 a month" | "See what your payment could be with our calculator" |
| "Pay off your home in 15 years" | "Shorter-term loans can save on interest. Let's compare options." |

Or keep the specific claim and include the full required disclosures nearby, with the same prominence.

## How do trigger terms affect websites and calculators?

Program pages that say "30-year" or show sample payments need the disclosures near those statements. Calculators where the borrower enters their own numbers are different from an ad stating a payment, but default values that display a specific payment should be clearly labeled as examples with disclaimers. ## What about social media and video?

Short formats make full disclosures hard, which is why many lenders avoid trigger terms in captions and videos entirely. If a reel mentions "30-year fixed," the disclosure needs to be in the post itself, not buried behind a link. The same rules apply to loan officers' personal accounts when they advertise loans. Trigger terms are one part of mortgage advertising compliance, alongside the MAP rule against misleading claims, RESPA, fair lending laws and state licensing rules such as NMLS display. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is "30-year mortgage" a trigger term?

Yes. It states the period of repayment, so the ad must also disclose the down payment, the full repayment terms and the APR.

### Is "low down payment" a trigger term?

General phrases like "low down payment options" do not state a specific amount or percentage, so they generally do not trigger extra disclosures.

### Do trigger terms apply to social media posts?

Yes. Social media posts that advertise credit follow the same rules, and the disclosures need to appear with the post.

### Can I advertise a rate without triggering disclosures?

You can state a rate if you state it as an APR, and if the rate can increase after closing, say so. Other details may require more disclosures.
