Title company websites have a specific audience: real estate agents and lenders who choose title for their clients, and buyers and sellers who look you up before closing. The best sites serve both without trying to look like a bank. Here is what the top title company websites share, with examples from sites we have built.

## What do the best title company websites have in common?

### 1. Online order opening

Agents want to open a file in two minutes from a phone, between showings. The best sites put "Open an Order" in the header and keep the form short: property address, parties, agent contact, contract upload through a secure method, and closing date.

### 2. A fee or premium calculator

A calculator lets agents and clients estimate title insurance and closing costs quickly. In states like Florida where title insurance premiums are set by the state, calculators can follow the promulgated rates. See [title insurance calculators](/blog/title-insurance-calculator/).

### 3. A prominent wire fraud warning

Wire fraud is one of the biggest risks in a real estate closing. The best sites have a dedicated wire fraud page linked from every page and every closing email, explaining that wiring instructions are never changed by email and how to verify them by phone. See [real estate wire fraud](/blog/real-estate-wire-fraud/).

### 4. Plain-English closing guides

Buyer and seller guides that explain what title insurance is, what happens at closing, what to bring and how long it takes. Prepared clients mean smoother closings.

### 5. Real people

Team pages with names, photos, roles and direct contact details. Title is a trust business, and faces build trust faster than logos.

### 6. Office locations and hours

Each office with address, hours, map, parking notes and mobile or remote closing options.

### 7. Proper disclosures

Affiliated business arrangement disclosures where relevant, licensing details, and a privacy policy that matches what forms collect. See [affiliated business arrangement disclosures](/blog/affiliated-business-arrangement-disclosure/).

## What do weak title company websites get wrong?

- No way to order online; just a phone number.
- Stock photos of handshakes and keys instead of the real team.
- Wire fraud warnings buried or missing.
- Slow, outdated designs that do not work on phones.
- Forms collecting sensitive data without secure handling.
- Missing affiliate disclosures on joint venture sites.

## How should a title website handle mobile and accessibility?

Most agents open your site on a phone, often in a car between appointments. Order forms, phone numbers and the wire fraud page must work perfectly on small screens, with large tap targets and no tiny text. Accessibility matters too: clear contrast, readable fonts, labeled form fields and keyboard navigation help every visitor, including older buyers and sellers who may be closing for the first time in decades. Test the site on a real phone before launch, and ask a couple of agents to open an order while you watch.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should be on a title company homepage?

A clear statement of who you serve and where, an "Open an Order" button, a link to the wire fraud page, office details and a short introduction to your team.

### Should title companies let agents upload contracts online?

Yes, through a secure method. Make sure uploads are encrypted and stored appropriately, not emailed as open attachments.

### How important is a wire fraud page?

Very. It protects clients, reduces liability and shows agents you take security seriously. Link to it from every page and closing email.

### Can a title company website help win more agents?

Yes, when it saves agents time. Easy ordering, quick estimates and clear communication make you easier to recommend.
