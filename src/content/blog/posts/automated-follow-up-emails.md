Most leads do not buy on the first contact, and most businesses stop following up after one or two tries. Automated follow-up emails fix that without adding work to your day. Done well, they feel personal and helpful. Done badly, they feel like spam. Here is how to do them well.

## What are automated follow-up emails?

They are emails your CRM or email platform sends automatically when something happens:

- Someone fills out a form or books a call.
- You send a quote.
- A lead goes quiet.
- A job is completed.
- A customer reaches an anniversary or renewal date.

Each trigger starts a short sequence that stops when the person replies or takes the next step.

## What does a good new lead sequence look like?

A simple example for a service business:

| When | Email |
|---|---|
| Immediately | Confirm the request, say who will contact them and when, include a booking link |
| Day 1 | Short note answering a common question, with reviews or photos of work |
| Day 3 | Check in: "Still looking for help with [service]?" with the booking link |
| Day 7 | Helpful resource or guide related to their request |
| Day 14 | Final friendly check-in, leaving the door open |

Pair it with an instant text and a call. Email alone is too slow for hot leads. See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

## What does a quote follow-up sequence look like?

Many quotes die from silence, not price.

1. **Day 1**: confirm the quote was received and offer to answer questions.
2. **Day 3**: share a relevant review or project example.
3. **Day 7**: ask if anything is holding them back.
4. **Day 14**: last check-in, with an easy way to say "not now."

## How should you follow up after a job?

- Thank-you email the same day.
- Review request a day or two later with a direct link. See [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).
- Maintenance or seasonal reminders later.
- Occasional referral requests to your happiest customers.

## How do you write follow-up emails people actually read?

- **Write like a person**, from a named person, in plain text or very light formatting.
- **Keep them short**: three to five sentences.
- **One ask per email.**
- **Use specifics**: their name, the service they asked about, their area.
- **Subject lines that sound human**: "Your roof inspection request" beats "Exclusive offer inside."
- **Stop the sequence** as soon as they reply or book.

## What rules apply to automated emails?

This is general information, not legal advice. Under the US CAN-SPAM Act, commercial emails need accurate sender information and subject lines, a valid physical postal address, and a clear way to opt out that is honored promptly. Transactional messages, such as a confirmation of a request someone made, are treated differently from marketing, but it is good practice to include an unsubscribe option in any sequence.

Gmail and Yahoo also require bulk senders to authenticate their email and make unsubscribing easy, so set up authentication even if you send modest volumes.

## How do you make sure follow-up emails reach the inbox?

- **Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC** on your domain.
- **Send from your own domain**, not a free email address.
- **Keep lists clean**: remove bounces and people who never engage.
- **Avoid spammy formatting**: all caps, too many links, image-only emails.
- **Watch spam complaints** and unsubscribe rates.

## Should follow-up emails come from a person or the company?

From a person. Emails signed by a real person, sent from their name at your domain, get opened and answered more often than messages from "info@" or "The Team." Use the name of whoever will actually take the conversation, and make sure replies reach that person's inbox or your CRM, not a no-reply address. A reply is the whole point of the sequence.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many follow-up emails should I send?

Four to six over two or three weeks works for most new leads, stopping the moment they reply. Long-term nurture emails can continue monthly.

### Should follow-up emails be automated or personal?

Both. Automate the timing and the routine messages, and write them in a personal voice. Hand off to a real conversation as soon as someone replies.

### Do follow-up emails need an unsubscribe link?

Marketing emails do under CAN-SPAM. Including one in every automated sequence is good practice and helps deliverability.

### Why are my follow-up emails going to spam?

Common causes are missing email authentication, sending from a free address, spammy formatting or sending to old, unengaged lists.
