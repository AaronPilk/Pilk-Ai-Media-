There is no magic number of posts, despite what you will read online. Algorithms change, audiences differ and what works for a national brand does not fit a local business with three employees. What matters is choosing a pace you can keep and posting things people actually want to see. Here are sensible starting points by platform and how to keep it up.

## Why does consistency matter more than frequency?

Social platforms reward accounts that post regularly and get engagement. Posting daily for two weeks and then disappearing for a month does less than posting three times a week for a year. Consistency also builds habit for your audience and for you.

## How often should you post on each platform?

These are practical starting points for small businesses, not rules:

| Platform | Starting point | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 3 to 5 posts a week, plus stories | Reels reach new people; stories keep followers engaged |
| Facebook | 3 to 5 posts a week | Strong for local communities and older audiences |
| TikTok | Several times a week if it is a focus | Rewards volume and experimentation |
| LinkedIn | 2 to 3 posts a week | Best for B2B and personal brands |
| YouTube | 1 video a week or every two weeks | Long-lived search traffic |
| Google Business Profile | Weekly posts and new photos | Supports local visibility |

If you can only manage one platform well, choose the one where your customers spend time and do it properly.

## Which platforms should a small business focus on?

- **Local service businesses**: Facebook, Instagram and Google Business Profile.
- **Real estate and mortgage**: Instagram, Facebook and YouTube, with local video.
- **B2B services**: LinkedIn, plus YouTube for explainers.
- **Younger audiences or visual products**: TikTok and Instagram.

Two platforms done well beat five done poorly.

## What should a small business post?

A simple mix keeps content varied:

- **Work**: before-and-after, projects, listings, results.
- **People**: your team, customers (with permission), behind the scenes.
- **Help**: tips, answers to common questions, quick explainers.
- **Proof**: reviews and testimonials.
- **Offers**: occasional promotions with a clear next step.

Most posts should help or show; a smaller share should sell.

## How do you keep up without burning out?

1. **Batch content**: film or write a week or month of posts in one session.
2. **Repurpose**: one video becomes a reel, a story, a short clip and a written post. See [how to repurpose content across platforms](/blog/repurpose-content-across-platforms/).
3. **Schedule ahead** with a scheduling tool or your CRM's social planner.
4. **Use templates** for recurring posts like tips and reviews.
5. **Automate the boring parts**, such as publishing and reporting, while keeping replies human.

See [social media automation tools](/blog/social-media-automation-tools/) for options.

## Does posting more get more customers?

Only if the posts are good and people can take a next step. Make sure your profiles link to a page with a clear offer and that messages and comments get answered fast. A comment asking for a price is a lead. Our guide to [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/) applies to social messages too.

## How do you know if your posting schedule is working?

Track a few things monthly: reach, engagement, profile visits, website clicks and messages or leads from social. If engagement drops, change the content before changing the frequency.

## Should you use AI to write social posts?

AI can help with ideas, captions and repurposing, but posts that sound generic perform poorly. Use AI for drafts and let real photos, real stories and your own voice carry the content. See [how to use AI for small business](/blog/how-to-use-ai-for-small-business/).

## What does a realistic weekly schedule look like?

For a small local business, something like this works: two short videos showing work or answering a question, one photo post of a recent job or team member, one review or testimonial, a few stories during the week, and one Google Business Profile post with a new photo. That is roughly an hour of filming and an hour of editing and scheduling if you batch it. Adjust once you see what your audience responds to.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is posting once a week on social media enough?

For a small business with limited time, consistent weekly posts beat sporadic bursts. More frequent posting usually grows reach faster if quality holds.

### What is the best time to post on social media?

It depends on your audience. Check your platform insights for when your followers are online and test a few time slots.

### Should I post the same content on every platform?

Repurpose the same idea, adjusted for each platform's format and audience. Identical posts everywhere often perform worse.

### Do Google Business Profile posts help?

Regular posts and new photos keep your profile active and give searchers more reasons to choose you. They are worth a few minutes a week.
