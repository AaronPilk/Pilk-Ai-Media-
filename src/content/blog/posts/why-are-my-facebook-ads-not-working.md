"Facebook ads don't work for my business" is almost never the real problem. Something specific is broken. Here are the ten causes we find most often when we audit small business ad accounts, roughly in the order we check them.

## 1. Is conversion tracking set up correctly?

If Meta cannot see when someone becomes a lead, it optimizes for cheap clicks instead of customers. Check that:

- The Meta Pixel is installed on every page.
- The Conversions API sends events from your site or CRM.
- Lead submissions fire a conversion event.
- Your campaign is optimizing for that event, not clicks or traffic.

See [Meta Pixel vs Conversions API](/blog/meta-pixel-vs-conversions-api/).

## 2. Is the campaign objective right?

Traffic and engagement campaigns get clicks and likes. If you want leads, use a leads or sales objective optimized for a real conversion. Boosting posts rarely produces leads.

## 3. Is the offer strong enough?

"Call us for a quote" is not an offer. People scroll past ads that do not give them a reason to act now. Better offers are specific and valuable: a free inspection, a limited-time price, a useful guide or a quick quote with a clear turnaround.

## 4. Is the creative stopping the scroll?

Generic stock photos and text-heavy graphics get ignored. What works:

- Real video of your work, team or results.
- A clear hook in the first few seconds.
- Captions, since many people watch without sound.
- Several creative versions so Meta can find what works.

## 5. Where is the traffic going?

Sending ad clicks to your homepage makes people hunt for the offer. A landing page that matches the ad converts better. See [landing page vs website](/blog/landing-page-vs-website/).

## 6. Is the landing page fast and simple?

Most ad clicks are on phones. If the page is slow, cluttered or asks for too much, people leave. Short forms, fast loading and proof like reviews near the form all help.

## 7. Is the budget spread too thin?

A small budget split across many campaigns and ads gives Meta too little data to learn from. Consolidate into fewer campaigns with enough budget behind each to get results to learn from.

## 8. Are you changing things too often?

Every significant edit can reset Meta's learning. Give campaigns time with steady settings before judging them, unless something is clearly broken.

## 9. Are leads followed up fast?

Many "bad lead" complaints are really slow follow-up. Leads from social ads are often early in their decision and forget quickly. Reply within minutes, by text and call, and nurture the rest. See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

## 10. Are you in a special ad category without adjusting?

Housing, employment, credit, financial products and some other ads run with restricted targeting. If your ads are in one of these categories, creative and offer carry more weight, and some tactics simply do not apply. See [Meta special ad categories](/blog/meta-special-ad-categories/) and [Facebook ads for real estate agents](/blog/facebook-ads-for-real-estate-agents/).

## What should you fix first?

Work in this order:

1. Tracking and campaign objective.
2. Offer and creative.
3. Landing page.
4. Budget structure and patience.
5. Follow-up speed.

Fixing tracking alone often changes results noticeably, because Meta finally knows what a good result looks like.

## How do you know if your ads are working?

Measure cost per lead, lead-to-customer rate and cost per customer, not likes or clicks. A campaign with expensive leads that close can beat one with cheap leads that never answer. ## How do you audit your own ad account?

Open Ads Manager and check, in order: which conversion event each campaign optimizes for and whether it is firing; how many campaigns and ad sets you run and how the budget is split; when you last made significant edits; what the ads link to; and how quickly leads from the last month were contacted. Write down what you find before changing anything. Most accounts have one or two obvious problems, and fixing those beats a full rebuild.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I run a Facebook ad before deciding it does not work?

Usually at least a couple of weeks with steady settings and enough budget to get results. Stop sooner only if something is clearly broken.

### Why are my Facebook ads getting clicks but no leads?

Usually a mismatch between ad and landing page, a slow or confusing page, a weak offer, or a campaign optimizing for clicks instead of leads.

### Why are my Facebook leads low quality?

Lead forms with no friction, vague offers and slow follow-up are common causes. Adding a qualifying question or using a landing page often improves quality.

### Should I boost posts or run ads in Ads Manager?

Ads Manager gives you proper objectives, tracking and targeting. Boosting is fine for awareness but rarely the best choice for leads.
