Most owners spend hours on their Google Business Profile and never touch Bing. That is a missed opportunity. Bing powers search on more places than people realize, and an accurate listing there takes minutes. Here is how to set it up and why it is worth doing.

## Why does Bing Places matter?

Bing has a smaller share of searches than Google, but its reach is wider than its name suggests:

- **Bing search and Bing Maps**, including on Windows computers where Bing is the default.
- **Microsoft Copilot**, which searches the web through Bing.
- **Other search engines and assistants** that draw on Bing's index or data.
- **AI search tools** that use third-party search results, where consistent business details help you get cited. See [how to show up in ChatGPT search](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-chatgpt-search/).

An accurate Bing listing also adds to the consistency of your business details across the web, which supports your local rankings everywhere.

## How do you set up Bing Places for Business?

1. **Go to Bing Places for Business** and sign in with a Microsoft, Google or Facebook account. Use an account the business controls, not a personal one an employee might lose.
2. **Search for your business** to see if a listing already exists. Claim it if it does.
3. **Import from Google Business Profile** if you have one. This is the fastest route and keeps the details consistent.
4. **Or create a listing manually**: business name, address or service area, phone, website, category, hours and description.
5. **Add photos** of your work, team and location.
6. **Verify the listing.** Bing offers verification methods such as phone, email or mail depending on the business; imported listings may verify through the Google connection.

## Should you import from Google or create manually?

Import if your Google Business Profile is accurate and verified. It saves time and keeps the two listings matching. After importing, check every field, since categories do not always map perfectly between platforms. Create manually if you do not have a Google profile yet or if the Google one has problems you need to fix first.

## How do service area businesses set up Bing Places?

If you serve customers at their locations and do not want your address public, you can hide it and list the areas you serve, similar to Google. Our guide to [Google Business Profile for service area businesses](/blog/google-business-profile-service-area-business/) explains the same principles, which apply here.

## What should you put in your Bing listing?

- **Exact business name**, matching your signage and Google profile. No keyword stuffing.
- **Primary category** that matches what you do.
- **Phone number and website** that match everywhere else.
- **Hours**, including holiday hours.
- **A plain description** of your services and the areas you serve.
- **Photos** that show real work.

## How do you keep a Bing listing accurate?

Check it every few months and whenever something changes: a new phone number, new hours or a move. Update Google, Bing and Apple Business Connect at the same time. Inconsistent details across platforms confuse search engines and customers.

## Do you need Bing Webmaster Tools too?

It helps. Bing Webmaster Tools is separate from Bing Places. It lets you submit your sitemap, see how Bing crawls your site and request indexing for new pages. Bing also supports IndexNow, which lets your site tell Bing about new and changed pages instantly. We use IndexNow on our own site. Google has its own equivalent, covered in [Google Search Console for beginners](/blog/google-search-console-for-beginners/).

## What mistakes should you avoid on Bing Places?

The same ones that hurt Google listings. Do not add keywords to your business name, list a virtual office or PO box as a storefront, or create duplicate listings for the same location. Do not let an agency or former employee own the account. If you find an old duplicate listing, claim it and mark it as a duplicate or closed rather than ignoring it. And do not set it and forget it: an outdated phone number on Bing still costs you calls.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Bing Places for Business free?

Yes. Creating, verifying and updating a listing costs nothing.

### Can I import my Google Business Profile into Bing?

Yes. Bing Places lets you import listings from Google Business Profile, which is the fastest way to set up. Review the imported details afterward.

### How long does Bing Places verification take?

It depends on the method. Phone and email verification can be quick; mail can take longer. Imported listings may verify faster.

### Does Bing Places help with Google rankings?

Not directly. But consistent business details across major platforms support local search in general and help AI tools that rely on Bing data.
