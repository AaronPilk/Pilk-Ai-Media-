Plenty of owners run a busy business with nothing but a Facebook page and word of mouth, and some of them ask us whether a website would change anything. Sometimes the honest answer is "not much, yet." Here is how to tell whether a website will bring you customers, and what to do instead if it will not.

## Why do businesses still need a website when they have social media?

Social media is rented space. The platform decides who sees your posts, can change the rules overnight, and can suspend an account with little warning. A website is the one place online that you own: your domain, your pages, your contact forms, your rules.

A website also does things social profiles cannot:

- **It shows up in search.** When someone searches for your service in your city, Google mostly shows websites and Business Profiles, not Instagram posts.
- **It answers questions in detail.** Pricing, service areas, process, licences and FAQs fit on a website. They get lost in a social feed.
- **It feeds AI search tools.** ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews and Perplexity read websites to answer questions. A business without one is harder for them to describe or recommend, which we cover in [how to show up in ChatGPT search](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-chatgpt-search/).
- **It captures leads you can follow up with.** A form that feeds a CRM gives you a list of interested people. A social comment does not.

## Is a Google Business Profile enough without a website?

A complete Google Business Profile is the single most useful free listing for a local business, and for some very local businesses it does most of the work. It shows your hours, reviews, photos, phone number and directions right in Google Search and Maps.

But it has limits. You cannot explain your services in depth, control the layout, or capture detailed leads. And your profile has a "website" field for a reason: Google uses your site to understand what you do. Our guide to setting up a [Google Business Profile as a service area business](/blog/google-business-profile-service-area-business/) covers the profile side. The website is what the profile points to.

## When can a small business wait on a website?

Waiting is reasonable when:

1. **All your work comes from referrals** and you are already turning people away.
2. **You are still testing the idea** and do not know what you will sell in six months.
3. **Your customers find you somewhere else entirely**, such as a marketplace, and do not look you up.
4. **Cash is tight** and a complete Google Business Profile plus a free social page covers the basics.

Even then, buy your domain name now. It costs little, keeps someone else from taking it, and gives you a professional email address.

## What happens to businesses that do not have a website?

There are a few patterns we see when businesses reach out after going without one:

- **They lose comparison shoppers.** People check two or three businesses before calling. The one with clear prices, photos and reviews on a real site often wins.
- **Referrals look them up and find nothing official.** Even a strong referral gets a quick search. A missing or outdated site plants doubt.
- **They depend on one platform.** When an account is hacked or a platform changes its reach, they have no fallback.
- **AI tools describe them inaccurately** or not at all, because there is no authoritative source about the business.

## What does a basic business website need?

You do not need 30 pages to start. A small business website needs:

- A **homepage** that says what you do, where and for whom, in the first screen.
- A page or section for **each main service**.
- An **about** page with real photos and the people behind the business.
- **Contact options**: phone, form and, if you take appointments, booking.
- **Proof**: reviews, examples of work, licences if your trade requires them.
- **Basic search setup**: page titles, descriptions, a sitemap and a connection to Google Search Console.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use Facebook instead of a website?

You can, and some businesses do for a while. The trade-off is that you do not control reach, layout or your data, and Facebook pages rarely rank for searches like "[service] near me." A website plus a Facebook page covers both.

### Do I need a website if I only get referrals?

Not always. If you are fully booked from referrals, a website can wait. Most referred customers still search your name before they call, so even a simple one-page site helps confirm you are legitimate.

### What is the cheapest way to get a business website?

Building it yourself on a website builder is usually the lowest cash cost, paid as a monthly subscription. You pay with your time instead. A professionally built template site costs more up front but saves the hours and usually loads faster.

### Will a website help me show up on Google?

It makes it possible. A website with clear service pages, a sitemap and a Search Console connection gives Google something to rank. Ranking well still takes time and useful content, which we cover in our posts on search.
