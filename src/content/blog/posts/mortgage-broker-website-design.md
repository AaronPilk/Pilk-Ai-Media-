Borrowers check a mortgage broker's website before they hand over anything personal. They want to know you are licensed, that you work with their kind of loan and that you will not waste their time. Regulators look at the same site for different reasons. Good mortgage broker website design satisfies both. Here is what that takes, based on the mortgage sites we design and build.

## What should a mortgage broker website include?

At minimum, every page set we build for mortgage brokers and loan officers includes:

- **A homepage** that says who you help, where you are licensed and how to start, in the first screen.
- **Loan program pages**: conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, jumbo, refinance, home equity, DSCR or non-QM, whichever you actually offer. One page each, written for borrowers.
- **An about page** with your name, photo, NMLS ID and story. People borrow from people.
- **Calculators**: payment, affordability and refinance at least. We cover the options in [mortgage calculators for websites](/blog/mortgage-calculator-for-website/).
- **A clear way to start**: a short pre-qualification form, a booking link or a phone number. Keep the first step short.
- **A licensing page** listing your company and individual NMLS IDs and state licences, with a link to NMLS Consumer Access.
- **Reviews** from real borrowers, ideally pulled from Google.
- **Legal pages**: privacy policy, terms, accessibility statement and the required disclosures.

## What compliance details does a mortgage website need?

Mortgage advertising is regulated, and your website counts as advertising. The details vary by state, so have your compliance contact or attorney review the site, but these show up on almost every compliant mortgage site:

- **NMLS IDs** for the company and the loan officers, typically in the footer of every page, with a link to [NMLS Consumer Access](https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org/) so borrowers can verify you.
- **State licence numbers** where your state requires them.
- **Equal housing language and logo.**
- **Careful rate and payment wording.** Under the federal Truth in Lending rules, mentioning certain terms like a payment amount or down payment in an ad can require additional disclosures. We explain these in [trigger terms in mortgage advertising](/blog/trigger-terms-mortgage-advertising/).
- **No promises you cannot keep**, like "guaranteed approval" or "lowest rates."

Our guide to [mortgage advertising compliance](/blog/mortgage-advertising-compliance/) goes deeper. On the sites we build, licence numbers live in one settings file so they appear consistently on every page and update everywhere at once.

## Should a mortgage broker show rates on the website?

It depends on your compliance approach. Live, personalised rates are hard to keep accurate and compliant on a public site, because rates depend on the borrower, the property and the lock. Some brokers show no rates. Others show a clearly labelled benchmark, such as Freddie Mac's weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey average, with a note that it is a national average and not an offer.

On one Florida brokerage site we built, the owner chose the benchmark approach after review: a weekly national average with its source and date, and a clear statement that it is not a quote. Whatever you choose, decide it with your compliance contact, not your web designer.

## What makes borrowers trust a mortgage website?

Borrowers are cautious, and for good reason. These details build trust quickly:

- A real photo and name, not a stock image of a smiling couple.
- Verifiable licences one click away.
- Plain-language program explanations that do not oversell.
- Honest expectations: what documents they will need, how long things take, what you charge or how you are paid.
- A short, respectful first form that does not ask for a Social Security number up front.
- A fast, human reply once they reach out.

## How do you get leads from a mortgage broker website?

A website converts the people who find it; it does not create demand on its own. Most brokers combine it with:

1. **A Google Business Profile** with reviews, for local searches.
2. **Program pages that answer real questions**, which help in search over time. See [SEO for mortgage brokers](/blog/seo-for-mortgage-brokers/).
3. **Referral partners**: agents, builders, financial planners and past clients.
4. **Ads**, which on Meta run under the financial products and services category with targeting limits.
5. **Fast follow-up** on every inquiry.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my NMLS number need to be on my website?

Most state regulators expect your NMLS ID on advertising, and many require it, so nearly every mortgage site shows it on every page. Rules differ by state, so confirm the exact requirements with your regulator or compliance contact.

### Can I use a template for a mortgage website?

Yes. A mortgage-specific template already has the pages, calculators and disclosure areas a broker needs, which is why our mortgage sites can launch in about two weeks. Your content, programs and licence details make it yours.

### What should the first form on a mortgage website ask?

Keep it short: name, phone, email, what they want to do (buy, refinance, cash out) and maybe a rough timeline. Ask for detailed financial information later, through your secure application process.
