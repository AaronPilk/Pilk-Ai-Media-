Many mortgage websites are digital business cards: a logo, a rate disclaimer and a contact page. A lead generation website is different. Every page is built to answer a borrower's question and invite the next step. Here is how to build one that brings in conversations without crossing compliance lines.

## What makes a mortgage website generate leads?

Five things working together:

1. **Answers to real borrower questions** on clear program and resource pages.
2. **Tools** that give borrowers a reason to engage, such as calculators.
3. **Low-friction contact options**: short forms, booking links and click-to-call.
4. **Trust**: licensing, real people, reviews and accurate information.
5. **Fast follow-up** that turns a form into a conversation within minutes.

## Which pages bring in mortgage leads?

- **Program pages** for each loan type you offer: conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, jumbo, refinance and niche programs such as self-employed or investor loans.
- **First-time buyer guides**, including state and local assistance programs, linked to official sources.
- **Refinance pages** explaining when a refinance can make sense.
- **Local pages** for the areas you serve, with real local detail.
- **About and team pages** with NMLS numbers and real photos.

## Do calculators help mortgage websites get leads?

Yes. Payment, affordability and refinance calculators keep borrowers engaged and create natural moments to offer help: "Want an exact number? Talk to a loan officer." Keep defaults clearly labeled as examples and add disclaimers. See [mortgage calculators for websites](/blog/mortgage-calculator-for-website/).

## What should mortgage lead forms ask?

As little as possible at first:

- Name, phone and email.
- What they want: purchase, refinance or cash-out.
- Timeline.
- Optional: price range or state.

Never collect Social Security numbers, income documents or bank statements on a marketing form. Send borrowers to your secure application system for anything sensitive. A marketing form is not an application.

## How do you keep a mortgage lead website compliant?

- Show company and loan officer NMLS numbers and state licensing as required.
- State rates only as an APR with required disclosures, or avoid static rates.
- Watch trigger terms that require extra disclosures. See [trigger terms in mortgage advertising](/blog/trigger-terms-mortgage-advertising/).
- Avoid misleading claims like "no closing costs" when costs exist.
- Include equal housing language.

See [mortgage advertising compliance](/blog/mortgage-advertising-compliance/). This is general information; your compliance team has the final word.

## Where does traffic to a mortgage website come from?

- **Referral partners**: realtors and builders sending buyers.
- **Local search**: Google Business Profile and program pages.
- **Social media**: education content pointing to calculators and booking links.
- **Ads**: Meta ads run under special ad categories, with targeting limits. See [Facebook ads for mortgage brokers](/blog/facebook-ads-for-mortgage-brokers/).
- **Past clients**: refinance and referral opportunities.

## Why does follow-up matter so much for mortgage leads?

Borrowers often contact several lenders. The first to respond helpfully usually gets the conversation. Connect forms to a CRM that texts the borrower instantly, alerts the loan officer and follows up if there is no answer. See [CRM for mortgage brokers](/blog/crm-for-mortgage-brokers/) and [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

## Should you buy mortgage leads instead?

Bought leads can supplement your pipeline, but they are shared, expensive and often low intent. Your own website builds an asset that keeps producing leads you do not pay for per lead, and it makes bought leads convert better too, because borrowers who look you up find a professional presence.

## How do you know if your mortgage website is working?

Track a few numbers monthly: visitors from your market, calculator uses, form submissions and booked calls, response time to new leads, and loans closed by source. If visitors are healthy but forms are low, improve offers and calls to action. If forms are healthy but closings are low, look at follow-up speed and lead quality.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a mortgage website really generate leads on its own?

It works best combined with referral partners, local search and social media. The website converts that attention into conversations and builds trust with every borrower who looks you up.

### What is the best lead magnet for a mortgage website?

Calculators and first-time buyer guides tend to perform well, followed by a clear offer to talk through numbers with a loan officer.

### Should my mortgage website show rates?

Most brokers avoid static rates because they change daily and require disclosures. A "get today's rate" form is a common alternative.

### How fast should mortgage leads be contacted?

Within minutes. An instant automated text and a call as soon as possible during business hours win far more conversations than next-day replies.
