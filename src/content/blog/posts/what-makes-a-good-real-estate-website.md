Most agent websites look fine and do nothing. They are a brokerage template with a headshot, a search bar and a contact form that emails an inbox nobody watches. A good real estate website is built around one question: why would a buyer or seller in your area contact you instead of the next agent? Here is what makes the difference.

## What should a real estate website do?

Before design, decide the jobs it has to do:

- **Prove you are credible** to someone who just got your name from a friend.
- **Win seller leads** through valuations, market updates and results.
- **Help buyers** with listings, neighborhood guides and the buying process.
- **Capture contact details** and get them to you fast.
- **Support your marketing**: listing campaigns, ads and social posts need somewhere to land.

## What are the 12 things a good real estate website gets right?

**1. Speed on phones.** Most visitors arrive from a phone, often from social media. If it takes more than a few seconds, they leave.

**2. A clear local focus.** Say where you work in the first screen. "Real estate in St. Petersburg and Pinellas County" beats "Your dream home awaits."

**3. A real about page.** Your story, your experience, your team, real photos. People hire people.

**4. Proof.** Reviews with names, recent sales, average days on market or list-to-sale results if you can show them honestly.

**5. A home valuation offer.** The best seller lead tool on most agent sites. See our guide to [home valuation landing pages](/blog/home-valuation-landing-page/).

**6. Listings that look great.** Your own listings deserve their own pages, not just a row in a search widget. A [single property website](/blog/single-property-websites/) for each listing does even more.

**7. Neighborhood pages.** Honest guides to the areas you serve help buyers and help you show up in local searches.

**8. Simple forms.** Name, contact details and one question. Long forms lose leads.

**9. Instant follow-up.** Forms should create a contact in your CRM and trigger a text within a minute. See [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead/).

**10. Compliance.** Brokerage name, license details as your state requires, and the Equal Housing Opportunity logo or statement. Follow fair housing rules in every description.

**11. Your own domain.** Brokerage and portal profiles come and go. A site on your own domain stays with you if you switch brokerages.

**12. Ownership and access.** You should control the domain, the site and the leads. More in [who owns my website](/blog/who-owns-my-website/).

## Do you need IDX on a real estate website?

Not always. IDX lets you show MLS listings on your site, which buyers expect, but those pages rarely rank because the same listings appear on every portal. It is most useful for keeping buyers on your site and capturing their details. We explain the tradeoffs in [IDX websites for realtors](/blog/idx-website-for-realtors/).

## What do most real estate websites get wrong?

- **Generic copy** that could belong to any agent anywhere.
- **No reason to contact you** other than "call me."
- **Slow, cluttered pages** stuffed with widgets.
- **Leads that go nowhere**: forms that email a brokerage inbox or an old address.
- **Stock photos** instead of real ones of you, your listings and your area.

## How do you know if your real estate website is working?

Track three numbers each month: visitors from your area, leads from the site, and how fast you replied. If visitors are low, work on local content and your Google Business Profile. If leads are low, fix the offer and forms. If replies are slow, fix follow-up before anything else.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is my brokerage website enough?

It is a start, but it usually promotes the brokerage, not you, and you lose it if you switch. A site on your own domain builds your personal brand and keeps your leads.

### What pages should a real estate agent website have?

Home, about, listings, home valuation, buyer and seller guides, neighborhood pages, reviews and contact. Add pages for any niche you specialize in.

### Should a real estate website have a blog?

Helpful local content works well: market updates, neighborhood guides and answers to common buyer and seller questions. Consistency matters more than volume.

### How fast should a real estate website load?

As fast as possible on a phone. Aim for the main content to appear within a couple of seconds, and test on a real mobile connection.
