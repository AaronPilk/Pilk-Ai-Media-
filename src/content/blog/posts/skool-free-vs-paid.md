Free or paid is the first big decision for any Skool community, and the plan choice follows from it. The wrong call can leave you with a crowded free group that never earns or a paid group nobody joins. Here is how to decide, and the simple math behind Skool's two plans.

## What are the pros and cons of a free Skool community?

**Pros**
- Lower barrier, so it grows faster.
- Builds trust and shows your value before asking for money.
- Larger groups can rank better in Skool's discovery directory when active.
- A natural top of funnel for paid offers.

**Cons**
- Lower commitment; many members lurk.
- More moderation for less revenue.
- You still pay Skool for the plan.

## What are the pros and cons of a paid Skool community?

**Pros**
- Members who pay show up and participate more.
- Predictable recurring revenue.
- Easier to justify live calls, coaching and fresh content.

**Cons**
- Slower growth; you need an audience or traffic source.
- Members expect consistent value every month.
- Churn needs active attention.

## When should you start free?

- You are building an audience from scratch.
- Your paid offer is coaching, a service or a course sold elsewhere.
- You want proof of demand before charging.

## When should you start paid?

- You already have an audience: email list, followers or clients.
- Your community delivers a clear result worth paying for.
- You can commit to weekly calls and posts.

## How do free and paid communities work together?

A common model: a free community with valuable public content and an active feed, where members see wins from the paid group and are invited to upgrade. The paid group offers deeper access: live calls, feedback, courses and a smaller, more serious peer group. See [how to grow a Skool community](/blog/how-to-grow-a-skool-community/).

## How should you price a paid Skool community?

Price by the result and the access, not by the content volume:

- **Low monthly prices** suit broad, peer-driven communities with light owner involvement.
- **Mid-range prices** suit groups with weekly live calls and structured courses.
- **Higher prices** suit coaching-heavy groups with direct feedback and small numbers.

Offer annual pricing at a discount for members who commit. Revisit pricing after you see churn and engagement data.

## Hobby or Pro: which Skool plan costs less?

As of late 2026, Skool's plans are:

| Plan | Monthly price | Fee on member payments |
|---|---|---|
| Hobby | $9 a month | 10% plus 30 cents per payment |
| Pro | $99 a month | 2.9% plus 30 cents, higher on very large payments |

The per-payment 30 cents is the same on both, so compare the rest. Pro costs $90 more a month but takes 7.1 percentage points less of your revenue. Pro becomes cheaper once member payments pass about $1,270 a month ($90 ÷ 0.071). Below that, Hobby costs less; above it, Pro saves money every month. Check Skool's current pricing page before deciding, since plans change.

## What do members expect from a paid community?

- A clear promise and visible progress.
- A reliable weekly rhythm of calls and posts.
- Quick replies from you or moderators.
- New content or challenges over time.
- A community of peers at their level.

## How do you move a free community to paid?

Give notice, explain what members get, offer early members a founding price, and keep a free option or a free tier for the public content if you want the top of funnel to continue. Expect some members to leave; the ones who stay are your core.

## What does a free-to-paid funnel look like in practice?

A fitness coach runs a free Skool group with a weekly live Q&A, a short starter course and an active feed where members share progress. Every week she highlights a win from her paid group, which includes personalized plans, weekly coaching calls and a smaller accountability circle. New free members get a welcome message, a seven-day challenge and, at the end, an invitation to join the paid group at a founding price. The free group stays genuinely useful on its own, which keeps it growing; the paid group offers what the free one cannot: personal attention and accountability.

## How do you reduce churn in a paid community?

Watch for members who stop posting or attending calls, reach out personally, and ask what they need. Celebrate progress publicly, refresh content regularly and make the first month especially valuable, since that is when most cancellations happen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I run a free community on Skool?

Yes. Members can join free communities at no cost, but the owner still pays for a Skool plan.

### Do I pay transaction fees on a free Skool community?

Transaction fees apply to member payments. A completely free community has no member payments, so you pay only the plan.

### When should I switch from Hobby to Pro?

When member revenue passes roughly $1,270 a month, Pro's lower transaction fee usually makes it cheaper overall. Recheck with Skool's current prices.

### Should I offer annual pricing?

Annual pricing at a discount improves retention and cash flow. Many paid communities offer both monthly and annual options.
