Title companies rarely win business from strangers typing "title company" into Google. They win it from agents and lenders, and from buyers checking out the company their agent recommended. SEO for a title agency is about showing up well in those moments. Here is a plan that fits how title actually works.

## Who searches for title companies online?

- **Buyers and sellers** looking up the company named in their contract, or searching closing questions.
- **Real estate agents** new to an area or looking for a backup title partner.
- **Lenders and attorneys** checking a company's details.
- **For-sale-by-owner sellers and investors** who choose title directly.

Each group needs to find you quickly and see that you are legitimate, established and easy to work with.

## Why is Google Business Profile the first priority?

When someone searches your name or "title company near me," your Business Profile shows up first. For each office:

- Verify the profile and choose the right primary category.
- Add accurate hours, phone, website and photos of the real office and team.
- List your services: residential closings, commercial, refinance, mobile closings.
- Post updates occasionally and keep holiday hours current.

Title offices that welcome walk-ins can show their address; follow Google's rules if you also close remotely. See our [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-for-small-businesses/).

## How do reviews help title company SEO?

Reviews affect both rankings and trust. Buyers who look you up after their agent's recommendation read them. Ask every client for a Google review after closing with a direct link, and reply to every review. Never offer incentives. See [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## What pages should a title company website have for SEO?

- **One page per office**, with address, hours, team and local details.
- **Service pages**: residential purchase, refinance, commercial, for-sale-by-owner, 1031 exchange support if you offer it.
- **Closing guides** for buyers and sellers.
- **A wire fraud page.** See [real estate wire fraud](/blog/real-estate-wire-fraud/).
- **A fee calculator** with an explanation of how costs work.
- **Team pages** with names and roles.

## What content ranks for title companies?

Answers to the questions buyers and sellers ask:

- "What is owner's title insurance and do I need it?"
- "How long does a title search take?"
- "What does a title company do at closing?"
- "Who pays for title insurance in [your state]?"
- "Do I need title insurance on a cash purchase?"

Put a short direct answer at the top, explain in plain English, name the author and keep it current. These pages build trust with clients and give agents something to send. They can also earn citations in AI search tools; see [how to show up in Google AI Overviews](/blog/how-to-show-up-in-google-ai-overviews/).

## Which technical basics matter for title sites?

- HTTPS everywhere, especially on order forms.
- Fast loading on phones.
- A sitemap submitted to Google Search Console and Bing.
- LocalBusiness structured data for each office that matches your Business Profile.
- Clean page titles naming the service and location.

## How do title companies build links and mentions?

- Local real estate associations and boards.
- Chambers of commerce.
- Educational sessions for agents, listed on association calendars.
- Partner pages from law firms or builders where appropriate.
- Local news on community involvement.

Keep RESPA in mind: do not exchange anything of value for referrals, and that includes paid placements tied to referrals. See [title company marketing ideas](/blog/title-company-marketing-ideas/).

## How do you measure title company SEO?

Track Business Profile calls and website clicks, branded searches, reviews per month, orders opened online and visits to closing guides. Growth in branded searches usually means your relationships and reputation are growing.

## How long does title company SEO take to work?

Fixing your Business Profiles and listings can improve how you show up for branded and nearby searches within weeks. Reviews and helpful content build over months. Because most title business comes through relationships, the biggest early win is usually how professional and trustworthy you look when someone searches your name.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do title companies need SEO?

Yes, mainly so buyers, agents and lenders can find and trust you when they search your name or common closing questions.

### How many Google Business Profiles should a title company have?

One per office that serves customers in person. Do not create profiles for addresses where you do not have staffed offices.

### What keywords should a title company target?

Your brand name, "title company" plus each city you serve, and the closing questions buyers and sellers ask in your state.

### Can a title company blog help get more orders?

Indirectly. Helpful closing content builds trust with clients and gives agents useful material to share, which makes you easier to recommend.
