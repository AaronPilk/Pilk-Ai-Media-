Joint ventures between brokerages, builders, lenders and title agencies are common, and they are legal when set up and disclosed properly. The disclosure is where many get it wrong, especially online. This guide explains the rules in plain English. It is general information, not legal advice; have an attorney review your arrangement and disclosures.

## What is an affiliated business arrangement?

Under the Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA), an affiliated business arrangement exists when a person in a position to refer settlement business, such as a real estate brokerage, lender or builder, has an ownership interest of more than 1% in a settlement service provider, such as a title agency, and refers business to it.

These arrangements are an exception to RESPA Section 8's ban on referral payments, but only if specific conditions are met.

## What conditions make an affiliated business arrangement legal?

Under RESPA Section 8(c)(4) and Regulation X ([12 CFR 1024.15](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1024/15/)), three conditions apply:

1. **Disclosure.** The consumer receives a written disclosure of the relationship and estimated charges, at or before the time of referral, in the format set out in the regulation.
2. **No required use.** The consumer is not required to use the affiliated provider, with narrow exceptions.
3. **Return on ownership only.** The owner receives only a return on their ownership interest, not payments tied to how many referrals they make.

## What must the disclosure say?

Regulation X includes a model Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure Statement. It generally covers:

- That the referring company has a business relationship with the provider.
- The nature of the relationship and the ownership percentage.
- An estimate of the charges or range of charges the provider generally makes.
- That the consumer is not required to use the provider and is free to shop for other providers.
- An acknowledgment for the consumer to sign, where applicable.

Use the format in the regulation and your attorney's approved version rather than writing your own.

## When must the disclosure be given?

At or before the time of the referral. For in-person referrals that is usually straightforward. Referrals by phone or online raise practical questions, such as where on a website the disclosure belongs and how to document that the consumer received it. Your attorney should set the process.

## How should websites handle affiliated business disclosures?

When a brokerage, lender or builder website promotes an affiliated title agency, the disclosure should appear where the referral happens. Common approaches include:

- A clear disclosure next to any link, button or recommendation pointing to the affiliate.
- A dedicated disclosure page linked from those spots, plus the signed form in the transaction file.
- Matching language on the title agency's own site explaining its ownership.

## What makes a joint venture title agency look like a sham?

Regulators look at whether the affiliated company is a real business that does real work, or a shell that exists to pass referral fees to its owners. Warning signs include an agency with no employees of its own, no real office, no real capital, work subcontracted back to the partner, or profits paid out in proportion to referrals. A properly run joint venture has its own staff, does substantive title work and pays owners based on ownership, not referrals.

## What are the most common mistakes?

- Giving the disclosure after the consumer has already been sent to the affiliate.
- Using a disclosure that does not show estimated charges.
- Website pages that recommend the affiliate with no disclosure nearby.
- Paying owners in ways that track referral volume.
- Pressuring or requiring consumers to use the affiliate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a title joint venture legal?

Yes, when it meets RESPA's conditions for affiliated business arrangements: proper disclosure, no required use and returns based only on ownership. Have an attorney structure and review it.

### Does the disclosure need to be signed?

The model form includes an acknowledgment for the consumer to sign in many situations. Follow your attorney's process for collecting and keeping signed disclosures.

### Does the disclosure go on the title company website?

The key disclosure belongs where the referral happens, usually the referring company's materials. Many title agencies also explain their ownership on their own site for transparency.

### What happens if the disclosure is missed?

Failing to meet the conditions can remove the exception, exposing the parties to RESPA Section 8 liability. Talk to your attorney if you find gaps.
