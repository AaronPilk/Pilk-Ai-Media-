Skool makes it easy to launch a community. Growing one is a different job. Most groups stall at a few dozen members because the owner expects the platform to bring people in, or because new members join, look around and never come back. This playbook covers both sides: getting members and keeping them.

## Why do most Skool communities stop growing?

The usual reasons:

- **A vague promise.** "A community for entrepreneurs" gives nobody a reason to join this one.
- **No first win.** New members do not know what to do, so they leave.
- **An empty feed.** If the owner posts once a week, members do not either.
- **No outside traffic.** Skool's discovery helps, but most growth comes from your own audience.
- **No reason to stay** once the first month's content is consumed.

## How do you position a Skool community so people join?

Write your about page around one specific person and one specific result:

- **Who it is for**: "realtors in their first three years" beats "real estate professionals."
- **What changes**: the outcome members get, in plain words.
- **How it works**: weekly calls, courses, feedback, accountability.
- **Proof**: your experience and member results, shared with permission.
- **Price and what is included**, clearly.

Free or paid changes the strategy. See [Skool free vs paid communities](/blog/skool-free-vs-paid/).

## How should onboarding work for new members?

The first 48 hours decide whether someone becomes active. Set up:

1. **A pinned welcome post** that says exactly what to do first.
2. **An introduction thread** with a simple prompt.
3. **A quick first win**: a short lesson, template or checklist they can use today.
4. **A personal welcome** from you or a moderator.
5. **A reason to come back**: the next live call, a challenge or a weekly thread.

## What weekly rhythm keeps a Skool community active?

Consistency beats volume. A simple rhythm:

- **One live call** each week, at the same time.
- **A weekly prompt** to share wins, questions or goals.
- **Two or three valuable posts** from you: lessons, breakdowns or behind-the-scenes.
- **Fast replies** to member posts, especially early on.
- **Monthly highlights** celebrating member progress.

The leaderboard and levels help, since members earn points when others like their posts and comments. Use levels to open bonus content so participation has a payoff.

## Where should new members come from?

Plan for most growth to come from outside Skool:

- **Short-form video and social posts** that teach something useful and invite people in.
- **Email**: your list is your most reliable source of members.
- **A free lead magnet** that leads to a free community or trial.
- **Podcasts, collaborations and guest appearances** in front of similar audiences.
- **Paid ads**, once you know your offer converts.
- **Member referrals**, encouraged with recognition or a referral reward if your plan supports it.

Skool's own discovery directory can bring extra members, especially for active groups with strong about pages.

## How do you keep members from leaving?

- **Track who goes quiet** and reach out personally.
- **Keep adding value**: new lessons, guest experts, challenges.
- **Ask members what they need** and build it.
- **Make progress visible** with member wins and milestones.
- **Make the community the product**, not just the course. People stay for each other.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get my first 100 members on Skool?

Invite your existing audience first: email list, social followers and past clients. Then add a steady content and partnership plan that points to the community.

### Does Skool promote communities for you?

Skool has a discovery directory where people browse communities, which can help active groups. Most growth still comes from your own marketing.

### Should my Skool community be free or paid?

Free grows faster and builds trust; paid brings more committed members and revenue. Many owners run a free community that leads to a paid one.

### How often should I post in my Skool community?

Enough to keep it active: a weekly live call, a weekly prompt and a few valuable posts. Replying to members matters as much as posting.
