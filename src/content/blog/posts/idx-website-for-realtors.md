IDX comes up in almost every real estate website conversation. Agents assume they need it because every big brokerage site has a home search bar. Sometimes they do. Often the money is better spent elsewhere. Here is how IDX works and how to decide.

## What is IDX?

IDX stands for Internet Data Exchange. It is the set of rules and data feeds that let MLS members display each other's listings on their own websites. With IDX, a buyer on your site can search active listings in your market, not just your own.

To use IDX, you typically need:

1. **Membership in your local MLS.**
2. **Permission from your broker**, since IDX access usually runs through the brokerage.
3. **An IDX provider**, a company that pulls the MLS data and displays it on your site, for a monthly fee.
4. **Compliance with MLS display rules**, such as listing attribution, disclaimers and how often data refreshes.

## How does IDX show up on a website?

IDX providers display listings in a few ways:

- **Embedded search widgets or frames**: quick to add, but the listing pages often live on the provider's system, not your site.
- **Subdomain pages**: listings live on something like search.yoursite.com.
- **Integrated pages**: listing pages render on your own domain, which is better for SEO and branding but usually costs more.

Ask any provider exactly where listing pages live and whether search engines can read them.

## Does IDX help real estate SEO?

Less than most agents hope. The same listings appear on Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin and every brokerage site in your market, so Google has little reason to rank your copy of a listing over theirs. IDX pages also change constantly as listings sell.

What does help real estate SEO: original neighborhood guides, market updates, your own listing pages with real descriptions and photos, and a strong Google Business Profile. We cover this in [real estate SEO](/blog/real-estate-seo/).

## When is IDX worth it?

IDX makes sense if:

- **You work mostly with buyers** and want them searching on your site, not a portal.
- **You run buyer ads** and need somewhere useful to send that traffic.
- **You want saved searches and alerts** tied to your CRM, so you know what each buyer is looking at.
- **You have a team** that will follow up on every registration.

It is less useful if you mostly work with sellers, rely on referrals, or will not follow up on buyer registrations. A portal can offer search for free; your site's job is to make people choose you.

## What does IDX cost?

IDX providers usually charge a monthly fee, plus possible setup costs and MLS or broker fees. Prices vary by provider, market and features. Ask about:

- Monthly fee and contract length.
- Setup fees.
- Whether listing pages are on your domain.
- CRM integration and lead alerts.
- Design limits on how listings look.

## What can you do instead of IDX?

Many agents get better results with:

- **Your own listing pages**, rich with photos, video and neighborhood detail. See [single property websites](/blog/single-property-websites/).
- **A home valuation page** for seller leads.
- **Curated searches**: a few hand-picked links to portal searches for popular areas, while your site focuses on your expertise.
- **Strong follow-up**, so every inquiry becomes a conversation.

## What makes a good IDX setup?

If you do add IDX:

1. Keep listing pages on your domain if your budget allows.
2. Ask for registration only after a buyer shows real interest, such as saving a home or viewing several listings. Forcing sign-up immediately drives people away.
3. Send registrations straight to your CRM with instant follow-up.
4. Keep the rest of the site focused on you: your story, results and areas.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need my broker's permission for IDX?

Usually yes. IDX access typically runs through the brokerage under MLS rules. Check with your broker and MLS before choosing a provider.

### Is IDX the same as a VOW?

No. A VOW (virtual office website) is a different MLS arrangement that can show more data to registered users under stricter rules. IDX is the more common public listing display.

### Will IDX listings get my website ranked on Google?

Rarely on their own, since the same listings appear on major portals. Original local content and your own listing pages do more for search.

### Can I add IDX to an existing website?

Usually yes. Most providers offer plugins, embeds or integrations for common platforms. The quality of the result depends on how listing pages are displayed.
