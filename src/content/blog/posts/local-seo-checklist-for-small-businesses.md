Local SEO decides whether you show up when someone nearby searches for what you do. It is not mysterious, and most of it is a checklist done well and kept up. Here is the list we work through for clients, in the order that usually pays off fastest.

## Step 1: Is your Google Business Profile complete?

The map results are where most local customers find businesses. Your profile should have:

- [ ] Verified ownership in an account the business controls.
- [ ] Exact business name, no extra keywords.
- [ ] The most accurate primary category, plus relevant secondary categories.
- [ ] Address shown only if customers visit you; service areas if you travel to them.
- [ ] Phone number and website that match your site.
- [ ] Hours, including holiday hours.
- [ ] Services listed with plain descriptions.
- [ ] Real photos of work, team and location, added regularly.
- [ ] A description that says what you do and where.

Service area businesses have extra rules. See [Google Business Profile for service area businesses](/blog/google-business-profile-service-area-business/).

## Step 2: Are your listings consistent everywhere?

Search engines compare your details across the web. Mismatches weaken trust.

- [ ] Bing Places for Business. See [Bing Places for Business](/blog/bing-places-for-business/).
- [ ] Apple Business Connect, for Apple Maps and Siri.
- [ ] Yelp and the review sites that matter in your industry.
- [ ] Industry directories and associations.
- [ ] Your chamber of commerce or local business groups.
- [ ] Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn pages with the same details.

Use the exact same name, address and phone format everywhere.

## Step 3: Are you getting reviews steadily?

- [ ] Ask every happy customer, with a direct review link.
- [ ] Automate the request by text or email after each job.
- [ ] Reply to every review, good and bad, professionally.
- [ ] Never offer incentives for reviews or post fake ones; it breaks Google's rules.
- [ ] Watch for and report spam reviews.

Recency matters, so a steady flow beats a burst. See [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## Step 4: Does your website support local search?

- [ ] A separate page for each main service.
- [ ] Pages for the main areas you serve, with genuinely local content.
- [ ] Your name, address or service area, and phone number on every page.
- [ ] A clear page title and description on each page that names the service and place.
- [ ] Reviews and real photos on service pages.
- [ ] LocalBusiness structured data that matches your profile.
- [ ] A fast, mobile-friendly design.
- [ ] Clear calls to action: call, text or request a quote.

If your site is not converting the traffic it gets, start with [why a website does not generate leads](/blog/why-is-my-website-not-generating-leads/).

## Step 5: Are the technical basics in place?

- [ ] HTTPS on every page.
- [ ] A sitemap submitted in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- [ ] No important pages blocked by robots.txt or set to noindex by mistake.
- [ ] Pages load quickly on a phone.
- [ ] Old addresses redirect to the right new pages after any redesign.

New to Search Console? See [Google Search Console for beginners](/blog/google-search-console-for-beginners/).

## Step 6: Are you building local links and mentions?

- [ ] Local sponsorships, events and charities.
- [ ] Partner businesses that refer customers to you.
- [ ] Local news coverage or features.
- [ ] Associations and professional groups.
- [ ] Guest content for local blogs or publications.

Avoid paid link schemes. A handful of real local links beats hundreds of junk ones.

## Step 7: Are you tracking what matters?

- [ ] Calls, direction requests and website clicks in your Google Business Profile.
- [ ] Form submissions and calls from your website.
- [ ] Search Console queries for your services and towns.
- [ ] Rankings for a short list of key searches, checked monthly.
- [ ] Leads and customers by source in your CRM.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most important local SEO factor?

For most businesses, a complete Google Business Profile with steady genuine reviews, backed by a website with clear service and location pages.

### Do I need a physical address for local SEO?

No. Service area businesses can rank in map results without showing an address, as long as they follow Google's rules for service areas.

### How many reviews do I need to rank locally?

There is no magic number. More recent, genuine reviews than your local competitors, with replies, is a practical target.

### Can I do local SEO myself?

Yes. Most of this checklist is setup and consistency. Getting help makes sense when time is short or the market is very competitive.
