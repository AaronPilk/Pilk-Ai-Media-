St. Petersburg has no shortage of web designers, from solo freelancers to agencies downtown and across the bay in Tampa. Picking one is hard because most portfolios look good and most quotes are hard to compare. We are a St. Petersburg studio ourselves, so take our view with that in mind. Here is what we would look for if we were hiring.

## What should you look for in a St. Petersburg web designer?

Look past the portfolio thumbnails and check these:

1. **Live sites you can visit.** Open them on your phone. Do they load fast? Can you find the phone number in two seconds?
2. **Work for businesses like yours.** A designer who has built sites for local service businesses, real estate or professional firms already knows what your customers look for.
3. **A written quote** that lists pages, features, timeline and what happens after launch.
4. **Ownership in your name.** Your domain registered to you and the finished site yours once paid. See [who owns your website](/blog/who-owns-my-website/).
5. **Local search setup included**, not sold as a mystery add-on later.
6. **Someone you can actually reach.** Being local means you can meet in person if you want to, but responsiveness matters more than zip code.

## What does local search setup mean for a Tampa Bay business?

A local website needs more than nice pages. It needs to show up when someone searches "[your service] St. Pete" or "near me." That usually means:

- **A complete Google Business Profile** with the right categories, service areas, hours, photos and reviews. If you work from home or travel to customers, set it up as a service area business with your address hidden. Our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-service-area-business/) explains how.
- **Pages for each main service**, mentioning the areas you actually serve, such as St. Petersburg, Pinellas County, Clearwater or Tampa, without stuffing city names into every sentence.
- **Consistent business details** everywhere: the same business name, phone and website on your site, Google, Bing Places, Apple Business and directories.
- **Structured data** that tells search engines your business type, service area and contact details.
- **Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools** connected, with a sitemap submitted.

## Does it matter if your web designer is local?

It matters less than it used to. You can work well with a designer anywhere. A local designer helps in a few specific ways:

- They know the area names, neighborhoods and how people here search.
- They can photograph your location or team without travel costs.
- You can meet face to face for kickoff or training if you prefer.
- They understand local realities, like posting closures and hours changes quickly during hurricane season.

If none of those matter to you, judge designers on work, process and responsiveness alone.

## What do St. Petersburg businesses often get wrong on their websites?

Patterns we see when local owners reach out:

- **No clear service area.** Visitors cannot tell whether you serve their part of the county.
- **An address shown that should be hidden**, or a home address listed as a storefront, which can cause problems with Google's guidelines.
- **Mismatched details** between the website, Google profile and old directory listings.
- **No plan for storm season**: no quick way to post a closure banner or updated hours.
- **Slow, image-heavy pages** that look great on office Wi-Fi and crawl on a phone at the beach.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to build a website for a local business?

A template-based site usually takes one to two weeks once your content and access are ready. Custom sites follow a schedule agreed in writing. Gathering your content early is the biggest factor.

### Do I need a separate page for each city I serve?

Only where you have something genuinely different to say for that area, such as local projects, reviews or details. Thin pages that just swap the city name can hurt more than help.

### Should my home address be on my website?

If customers do not visit you, usually not. Service area businesses can hide their address on Google and list the areas they serve instead. Keep the business name and phone number consistent everywhere.

### Can a St. Petersburg designer help with my Google Business Profile?

Many can. Ask whether it is included in the quote. The owner usually completes verification, by phone, video or postcard, because Google sends the code to the business.
