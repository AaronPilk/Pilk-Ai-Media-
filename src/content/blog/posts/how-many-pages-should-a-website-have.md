Website quotes often hinge on page count, and owners reasonably ask how many they actually need. There is no perfect number, but there is a practical way to decide: one page for each distinct thing a customer needs to know or do. This guide shows how to plan that, and why more pages is not automatically better.

## What pages does every small business website need?

These cover the basics for almost any business:

1. **Home**: who you are, what you do, where, and the main next step.
2. **About**: your story, team, experience and why customers choose you.
3. **Services**: either one page listing everything or, better, an overview linking to individual service pages.
4. **Contact**: phone, email, form, hours and the areas you serve.
5. **Reviews or work**: proof that you deliver.
6. **Privacy policy**: required if you collect any information through forms.

That is six pages, and many small businesses can launch with exactly that.

## Why should each service have its own page?

A separate page for each main service does three jobs:

- **Search**: someone searching "roof repair Clearwater" is better matched by a roof repair page than a general services page.
- **Clarity**: each page can answer that service's questions, show relevant photos and use the right call to action.
- **Ads**: a service page makes a better destination for a specific ad.

A rule of thumb: if a service has its own customers, questions and price range, it deserves its own page. If it is a minor add-on, mention it on a related page.

## Do you need location pages?

Only for areas you genuinely serve, and only if you can say something useful about each. A good location page includes real local details: neighborhoods you work in, local projects, reviews from customers there, and anything specific about serving that area. Pages that are identical apart from the city name help nobody and can be treated as low-quality by search engines.

If you serve one metro area, a single well-written service area page often does the job. See our [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-for-small-businesses/).

## Should a small business website have a blog?

A blog, or a resources section, gives you a place to answer the questions customers ask before they buy. Each good answer can bring search traffic and gives your team something to send. It only works if the articles are genuinely useful and kept accurate. Quality beats quantity, and a handful of excellent articles outperforms dozens of thin ones.

## How many pages do different businesses typically need?

| Business type | Typical starting point |
|---|---|
| Solo service provider | 5 to 8 pages |
| Local service business with several services | 8 to 15 pages |
| Real estate agent | 8 to 15 pages plus listing pages |
| Mortgage broker | 10 to 20 pages, with a page per loan program |
| Multi-location business | Core pages plus one page per location |

These are starting points. Plan for the site to grow as you add services, locations and content.

## Can a website have too many pages?

Yes, when pages exist only to target keywords without helping anyone. Thin pages dilute your site, are harder to maintain and can drag down how search engines judge your site overall. Signs of too many pages:

- Several pages saying almost the same thing.
- Pages nobody visits and nobody would miss.
- Outdated pages with wrong prices or services.

Merge, update or remove them, and redirect removed pages to the closest relevant one.

## How do you plan your site's pages?

1. List the questions customers ask before they hire you.
2. Group them by service and stage: researching, comparing, ready to buy.
3. Give each group a page.
4. Map how visitors move from page to page toward contacting you.
5. Start with the pages that bring in the most valuable customers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a one-page website enough for a small business?

For a brand-new business or a very simple offer, it can work at first. Most businesses outgrow it quickly because separate service pages help with search and clarity.

### Do more pages help SEO?

Only if each page is useful and distinct. More helpful pages can bring more search traffic; more thin pages do not.

### How long should each page be?

Long enough to answer the visitor's questions. Service pages often need several hundred words plus photos, reviews and a clear next step.

### Should I remove old pages from my website?

Update them if they are still relevant. Remove pages that are outdated and unhelpful, and redirect them to the closest relevant page.
